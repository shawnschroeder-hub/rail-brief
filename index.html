President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The Mexican tank car trade case is now formally open. Commerce has initiated antidumping and countervailing duty investigations on railway tank cars and subassemblies from Mexico, with the notices published in the Federal Register today. The ITC's preliminary injury vote is now expected on or around December 4, later than the November 16 date reported earlier. No duty is in place, and a negative ITC finding would end the case.
  2. Short-line volumes held up through the third quarter. The RailConnect Index of 444 short lines was up 3.3% in carloads through early October, with intermodal up 12.7% and coal down 21%. Gains were led by grain, crude and chemicals on carriers such as Watco and several grain-heavy regionals. That is steady traffic at the network's edges, not a broad-based upturn.
  3. A heavy week for the merger record. Opening comments on the UP–NS application are due at the STB on Wednesday, November 18, and the Board told BNSF, CSX and a shipper coalition to renew their merits arguments there. The AAR's weekly traffic report also lands Wednesday. The National Steel Car strike in Hamilton, which began August 12, still has no reported settlement.

Commerce initiates antidumping and countervailing duty investigations on railway tank cars from Mexico
U.S. Department of Commerce, International Trade Administration · Nov 10 (Federal Register notices Nov 16) · trade policy · official notice
Commerce has opened antidumping and countervailing duty investigations on certain railway tank cars and subassemblies from Mexico, on petitions from UTLX Manufacturing that allege dumping margins of 11.97% to 31.72%. The ITC's preliminary injury determinations are now expected on or around December 4, and if they are affirmative, Commerce's preliminary subsidy and dumping determinations are scheduled for January 14 and March 30, 2027, subject to extension. Why it matters: any duties would raise the cost of Mexican-built tank cars and subassemblies used by several North American builders and lessors, though nothing is in force and a negative ITC vote would end the case.

Q3 traffic: Short lines' tallies still mostly in the black
Progressive Railroading (RailPrime), citing the RailConnect Index and carriers · Nov 3 · trade press · short-line traffic
The RailConnect Index of 444 short lines logged 4,599,582 carloads in the 39 weeks ended October 3, up 3.3% year over year, with intermodal up 12.7% and coal down 21%. Watco said its third-quarter carloads rose 11.8%, while grain-heavy Twin Cities & Western and Red River Valley & Western reported gains of 28% and 26%. Why it matters: short-line originations feed Class I carload traffic, but these gains are concentrated in grain, crude, chemicals and intermodal while coal keeps falling.
Washington state port, short line win FRA grant to modernize 19 crossings
Progressive Railroading (RailPrime) · Nov 10 · trade press · short lines · federal funding
The Port of Pend Oreille will receive an $8.92 million FRA Railroad Crossing Elimination grant toward an $11.15 million project to modernize 19 grade crossings along 40 miles of its corridor in northeastern Washington and northern Idaho, with a $2.23 million local match. Its Pend Oreille Valley Railroad owns 61 miles of track, only 23 of them active, and leases 24 miles from BNSF. Why it matters: federal crossing money is reaching small short lines, though the port expects the work to take about five years and has not set a start date.

October 2026 national auto sales
CarPro, citing preliminary GlobalData estimates · Nov 4 · automotive · sales data
Preliminary GlobalData estimates put October U.S. new-vehicle sales at about 1.3 million units, up 4% from a year earlier, helped by an extra selling day and an easy comparison. The seasonally adjusted annual rate was 15.6 million, the first reading below 16 million since February, while fleet deliveries rose 9%. Why it matters: a softer selling rate could temper finished-vehicle rail volumes into 2027, though sales are only a loose proxy for autorack demand and production data were not available.

Guest Comment: North America's trade depends on keeping goods moving
Progressive Railroading; guest comment by the AAR, ASLRRA, RAC and AMF leaders · Nov 2026 · opinion · trade policy
The heads of the AAR, ASLRRA, the Railway Association of Canada and Mexico's AMF jointly urge policymakers to keep USMCA trade rules predictable and fix border bottlenecks, such as a second inspection they say can add up to 72 hours of dwell to grain unit trains bound for Mexico. They cite roughly $203 billion of cross-border rail trade in 2024 and North American rail volume up 3.3% through October, figures given without sources. Why it matters: the joint position shows the railroads of all three countries pressing for USMCA continuity, which bears on cross-border carload flows to and from Mexico.
