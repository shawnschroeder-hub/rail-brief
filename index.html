President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Trinity says 2026 is the bottom for car deliveries. In third-quarter results released this morning, Trinity kept its outlook of industry deliveries below 25,000 railcars this year and said it believes 2026 will mark the cyclical low point. Its own orders (2,190 cars) slightly exceeded deliveries (2,030), and its lease fleet was 97.0% utilized. That is the company's view of the cycle; new-build component volumes stay at low levels until industry deliveries actually turn.
  2. Traffic on the network keeps growing. AAR's weekly data for the week ending October 24 showed U.S. traffic up 7.8% year over year, with intermodal up 11.3% and carloads up 3.6%. CPKC's third quarter showed revenue ton-miles up 6%, with grain revenue up 29% and intermodal up 22%. More car-miles on the in-service fleet supports wear-part demand over time, but it is not the same as new-car orders.
  3. Mixed supplier signals and dates ahead. Knorr-Bremse's rail division grew revenue 6.1% in the third quarter, but its rail order intake fell 10.9%. Timken reports on November 4 and FreightCar America on November 9, and UP–NS merger comments are due at the STB on November 18. The National Steel Car strike that began August 12 has not been reported settled.

Trinity Industries, Inc. Announces Third Quarter 2026 Results
Trinity Industries (SEC filing) · Oct 29 · company release · earnings
Trinity reported third-quarter revenue of $535.1 million (versus $454.1 million a year earlier) and income from continuing operations of $0.27 per diluted share, with 2,190 railcar orders valued at $325.0 million, 2,030 deliveries and a new-railcar backlog of $1,646.7 million. Lease fleet utilization was 97.0% and the future lease rate differential 0.1%, and the company kept 2026 EPS guidance of $2.20–$2.40 and its outlook for industry deliveries below 25,000 railcars, saying it believes 2026 will mark the cyclical low point. Why it matters: orders slightly above deliveries and a near-full lease fleet support the company's bottoming view, but new-build component volumes stay at cycle-low levels until industry deliveries actually recover.
AAR Reports Weekly Rail Traffic for the Week Ending October 24, 2026
AAR · Oct 28 · association data · weekly traffic
U.S. railroads moved 538,503 carloads and intermodal units in the week, up 7.8% year over year, with carloads up 3.6% to 234,826 and intermodal up 11.3% to 303,677; eight of ten carload groups rose, led by metallic ores and metals (+2,272), motor vehicles and parts (+2,234) and petroleum products (+1,565), while grain fell 322 carloads. Through 42 weeks, U.S. combined traffic is up 3.8% and North American traffic up 3.3%, according to AAR. Why it matters: sustained volume growth adds car-miles on the in-service fleet, which supports running-repair wear-part demand over time, though weekly gains do not translate directly into new-car orders.

Knorr-Bremse delivers another strong quarter
Knorr-Bremse · Oct 28 · company release · earnings
Knorr-Bremse reported third-quarter revenue of €2,051 million, up 9.0%, and an order book of €7,883 million; its Rail division's revenue rose 6.1% to €1,114 million while rail order intake fell 10.9% to €1,045 million. The company confirmed 2026 guidance of €8,100–8,300 million in revenue, expecting the upper end, and the release does not break out New York Air Brake's North American freight business. Why it matters: lower rail order intake at a major brake supplier is one data point on the equipment cycle, not a direct read on North American freight-car demand.

CPKC reports solid third quarter results; on track to deliver a strong 2026
CPKC via PR Newswire · Oct 28 · company release · earnings
CPKC reported third-quarter revenues of C$4.2 billion, up 15%, on a 6% rise in revenue ton-miles, with diluted EPS of C$1.04 and core adjusted diluted EPS of C$1.28, up 16%. Grain revenue rose 29% to C$904 million and intermodal 22% to C$815 million, while higher fuel prices helped push the reported operating ratio up 130 basis points to 64.8%. Why it matters: strong grain and intermodal growth on a major customer's network is a positive signal for covered-hopper and well-car utilization, even as fuel costs squeeze its margins.

UP-served grain facility opens in Texas Panhandle
Progressive Railroading · Oct 28 · trade press · grain · Union Pacific
5V LLC, a partnership of Five Rivers Cattle Feeding and Viserion Grain, has opened a shuttle-train grain receiving facility in Dalhart, Texas, connected to Union Pacific's network, after breaking ground in June 2025. The article did not state capacity or train size. Why it matters: a new shuttle-capable destination for feed grain is a modest, positive signal for unit-train covered-hopper use on UP in the Southern Plains.
