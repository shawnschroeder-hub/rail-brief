President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Timken's quarter was steady, and its bearings segment was the slow part. Third-quarter sales rose 4.7% to $1.21 billion and adjusted EPS rose 20.4% to $1.65, and the company raised its full-year EPS outlook. But Engineered Bearings sales grew only 0.7%, while Industrial Motion grew 12.4%. The release does not break out rail, so it says little directly about the freight-car bearing market.
  2. CSX's two-day IT outage showed how much of railroading now runs on software. A systems failure that began October 27 stopped CSX's data exchange with other Class I railroads, halting interchange until October 29, though dispatching and positive train control kept working. CSX says it was not a cyberattack. Trackside data showed the interchange backlog clearing by October 29, so the lasting effect looks like delay rather than lost volume.
  3. Deadlines to watch, and a quiet supplier day. The strike deadline for about 250 ILWU Local 26 watchmen at Los Angeles and Long Beach terminals is 5 p.m. Pacific today; no report of a deal or a further extension had been published by this morning. Comments on the UP–NS merger are due at the STB on November 18, and FreightCar America reports third-quarter results on November 9. Competitor news was otherwise quiet, and the National Steel Car strike that began August 12 has not been reported settled.

Timken Reports Third-Quarter 2026 Results
Timken · Nov 4 · company release · earnings · bearings
Timken reported third-quarter sales of $1,211.4 million, up 4.7% (2.8% organic), net income of $93.8 million and adjusted EPS of $1.65 versus $1.37, with Engineered Bearings sales up 0.7% to $771.4 million and Industrial Motion up 12.4% to $440.0 million. The company raised its 2026 outlook to adjusted EPS of $6.35 to $6.50 on revenue up about 5.5%. Why it matters: Timken is a major rail-bearing competitor, but the release does not discuss rail, so the flat bearings line cannot be read as a freight-car signal.
CSX says interchange is moving again after a technology outage
American-Rails.com, citing Trains and RailState · Oct 30 · trade press · Class I service · CSX
A CSX technology outage that began October 27 knocked out systems including crew calling, yard inventory and waybills and stopped data exchange with other Class I railroads, halting interchange until core functions returned on October 29; dispatching and positive train control were not affected, and CSX said it was not a cyberattack. RailState's trackside sensors, as reported by Trains, showed CSX traffic down 46% against its 30-day average for 29 hours before recovering, with BNSF-to-CSX interchange at 141% of normal as the backlog cleared. Why it matters: a two-day stoppage delays shipments and car cycles on a major customer's network, but the reported recovery points to a short-lived service hit rather than lost volume.
