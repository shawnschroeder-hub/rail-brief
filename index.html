President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Railroads are adding terminal capacity and reshuffling port switching. BNSF finished yard projects at Galesburg, Winslow and Memphis, and says the Memphis work was added outside its original 2026 capital plan because volume grew faster than expected. At the Los Angeles and Long Beach ports, the Union Pacific–BNSF Alameda Belt Line has told the STB it intends to take over rail operations from Pacific Harbor Line as soon as October 16. Both point to railroads planning for steady volume, though this is track and terminal spending rather than car orders.
  2. Weather and safety events marked the week, with no major hazmat releases. Flooding disrupted BNSF at Lincoln, Nebraska and on its El Paso line in New Mexico, about 25 cars derailed near Pleasantville, Iowa, and nine empty Conrail cars derailed in Jersey City. The NTSB issued a preliminary report on a Norfolk Southern conductor seriously injured falling from a tank car during a shoving move. In Washington, a bipartisan Senate bill would raise pay for FRA inspectors, whose pay levels its sponsor says have not risen since 1979.
  3. New public service data and the start of earnings season give fresh yardsticks. The first public release of the STB's weekly carload service data shows CPKC's US manifest on-time in the mid-70s percent, according to one independent analyst, who cautions against comparing railroads directly. CPKC also reported record September grain volumes in Canada and the US. Wabtec reports third-quarter results on October 21, an early read on equipment and aftermarket demand.
Top Stories 3 stories
BNSF completes capacity projects at Galesburg, Winslow and Memphis
Trains · Sep 30 · capacity · customers
A second hump lead at Galesburg, Illinois raises that yard's capacity from about 2,000 to 2,300 cars a day, and new tracks at Winslow, Arizona let locomotives be serviced without blocking the Southern Transcon main line. At Memphis, three new receiving and departure tracks added more than 200 cars of capacity in September, work BNSF says was not in its original 2026 capital plan and was added because volume grew quickly. Why it matters: terminal spending signals BNSF expects sustained carload volume, though it is infrastructure spending, not car or component spending.
Alameda Belt Line files to take over rail operations at the Los Angeles and Long Beach ports
Trains · Oct 2 · ports · STB filing
The Union Pacific–BNSF partnership told the Surface Transportation Board it intends to replace Pacific Harbor Line, the operator since 1998, as soon as October 16, and a Port of Los Angeles spokesman said an agreement has been reached but still needs approval by both ports' commissioners. The filing notes Pacific Harbor Line has not consented to discontinuing its operating authority, so further Board action may be needed. Why it matters: the San Pedro Bay switching operator handles the on-dock rail moves that feed intermodal traffic, so a smooth handover matters for port rail fluidity.
First public STB carload service data puts CPKC's US manifest on-time in the mid-70s
Trains, citing analyst Rick Paterson · Oct 2 · service metrics · STB data
The STB began requiring weekly manifest on-time and local spot-and-pull reporting on July 8, and the first data became public on September 29; independent analyst Rick Paterson compiled a 13-week history. Paterson reads it as Union Pacific running slightly better than BNSF, CSX better than Norfolk Southern and CPKC's US-only manifest on-time in the mid-70s percent, while warning that differences in business mix and methodology make cross-railroad comparisons uncertain by at least a few percent. Why it matters: regular public service data gives shippers and regulators a new yardstick during the merger review, though these early readings are one analyst's interpretation.

BNSF recovers from flooding in Nebraska and New Mexico; about 25 cars derail in Iowa
Trains · Oct 2 · service · weather
Four to five inches of rain flooded BNSF's Lincoln, Nebraska yard on October 1, prompting a customer delay advisory, after washouts along a 150-mile stretch of the El Paso Subdivision in New Mexico took that line out of service under an embargo from September 30. Separately, about 25 cars of a 57-car westbound train derailed near Pleasantville, Iowa on October 1, with no injuries or hazardous materials reported and no cause yet given. Why it matters: weather outages slow car cycles and lengthen transit times, but BNSF expected both the New Mexico line and the Iowa main line to reopen on October 2.
R.J. Corman sues to block sale of Luzerne County rail lines to Reading & Northern
Trains, citing the Wilkes-Barre Times Leader · Oct 3 · short lines · litigation
Short-line operator Corman filed suit in federal court on October 2 seeking to void the sale of 60 miles of active track to the Reading & Northern, whose offer of $7 million in cash plus $3 million in upgrades was accepted after the county council vetoed an $8.2 million sale to Corman. The suit argues the sale broke state competitive-bidding law and seeks a preliminary injunction; Corman's operating agreement on 27 miles of the lines expires October 30. Why it matters: the dispute could leave shippers on these lines uncertain about who will serve them once the current contract ends.
Nine empty Conrail freight cars derail in Jersey City
ABC7 New York · Oct 2 · safety
Nine empty cars left the track on Conrail property near 10th and Brunswick streets on the night of October 1; Conrail said the cars stayed intact, nothing was released, no locomotives derailed and no one was hurt. Why it matters: a low-consequence event carried for completeness, and with the cause under investigation no conclusion about equipment or track should be drawn.

CPKC reports record September grain volumes in Canada and the United States
BNN Bloomberg, citing CPKC · Oct 2 · agriculture · grain
CPKC said it moved 2.94 million metric tons and 30,324 carloads of grain and grain products in Canada in September, topping marks set in 2020, plus 2.51 million metric tons and 26,236 carloads in the United States, and also reported record third-quarter volumes in both countries. September was the second month of the 2026–27 crop year. Why it matters: strong early-season grain movement keeps covered hoppers cycling, though these are company-reported figures and the Canadian and US measures are kept separately.
Regulatory & Policy 2 stories
Senators introduce a bill to raise pay for FRA inspectors
Trains · Oct 1 · legislation · bill introduced
Senators Gary Peters (D-Michigan) and Jim Justice (R-West Virginia) introduced Senate bill 5450, which would raise FRA inspectors' pay classification by one level and lift maximum salaries by nearly $20,000; Peters says those pay levels have not increased since 1979. The American Federation of Government Employees says 11 inspectors left within eight months after FRA staffing peaked last year. Why it matters: the bill has only been introduced, but inspector staffing affects how consistently car, track and operating rules are enforced.
NTSB preliminary report on a Norfolk Southern conductor injured riding a tank car at Conway Yard
Trains, citing NTSB · Oct 1 · safety · preliminary report
The conductor was seriously injured on September 8 at Conway Yard in Pennsylvania after falling from a tank car he was riding while the crew shoved a 219-car train from the main line into the yard; he was airlifted to Pittsburgh. The investigation, with FRA, Norfolk Southern and two unions as parties, is ongoing and no cause has been determined. Why it matters: it adds to the NTSB's case against riding cars during shoving moves, which could eventually lead to operating-rule changes.
