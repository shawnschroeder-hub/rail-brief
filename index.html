President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The short-line tax credit bill now has a Senate majority behind it. The American Short Line and Regional Railroad Association says the 45G modernization bill has 51 Senate and 183 House co-sponsors, including the Senate's majority and minority leaders. It would lift the credit cap from $3,500 to $6,100 per mile of track and index it to inflation. Co-sponsorship is not passage: the association is looking for a tax bill to attach it to this Congress and otherwise expects to reintroduce it next year.
  2. Import containers are still pouring through Los Angeles. The port handled 1,026,872 TEUs in October, a record for the month, with loaded imports up 25% and exports down 7%. A record 373,271 empty containers also moved as carriers sent equipment back to Asia. The release does not break out rail moves, so this is a supportive signal for Western intermodal rather than a measured rail gain.
  3. Train theft turned violent on a key western line. A shooting occurred after Union Pacific police found an open container on the Mojave Subdivision near the Tehachapi Loop on November 12; no injuries were reported and two men were arrested. Separately, opening comments on the UP–NS merger application are due at the STB tomorrow, November 18, and the AAR's weekly traffic report also lands Wednesday.
Top Stories 1 story
Majority of U.S. senators back short line track maintenance credit bill
American Short Line and Regional Railroad Association · Nov 16 · legislation · association release
The association says the Senate's Railroad Track Maintenance Credit Modernization bill now has 51 co-sponsors, while its House companion has 183. The bill would raise the 45G credit cap from $3,500 to $6,100 per mile, index it to inflation and make track on short lines formed since 2015 eligible, and the association hopes to attach it to the next tax bill this Congress. Why it matters: a larger credit would support short-line track and bridge upgrades that help lines handle modern heavy cars, though passage is not assured and any effect on railcar component demand is indirect.

Union Pacific police in shootout after alleged train robbery on southern California freight line
FreightWaves, citing the Kern County Sheriff's Office · Nov 16 · Class I · security
Union Pacific police patrolling after recent thefts found an open cargo-container door on the Mojave Subdivision east of the Tehachapi Loop at about 10:40 p.m. on November 12, and a shooting followed; the sheriff's office reported no injuries. Two men were later arrested, one booked on suspicion of attempted murder and assault with a firearm on a peace officer, on a line FreightWaves says carries 30 to 40 freight trains a day, with BNSF holding trackage rights. Why it matters: theft from trains on a key western corridor remains a live security and service risk for railroads and their shippers.
Shipper Industries & Demand 1 story
Port of LA sets October volume record
Progressive Railroading, citing the Port of Los Angeles · Nov 16 · intermodal · port data
The Port of Los Angeles handled 1,026,872 TEUs in October, its best October and second-highest month on record, with loaded imports up 25% year over year to about 538,620 TEUs while exports fell 7% to 114,981. Empty containers set a second straight monthly record at 373,271 TEUs as carriers repositioned equipment to Asia, and the 10-month total of 9,108,497 TEUs is up 5%. Why it matters: strong import flows support Western intermodal loadings, though the release does not break out on-dock or inland rail moves.
Global Rail 1 story
Lineas extends wagon leases with Ermewa
Railway Gazette International, contract round-up · Nov 9 · Europe · wagon leasing
Belgian freight operator Lineas has extended wagon-lease contracts with lessor Ermewa that were first agreed in 2021, COO Alban François announced on November 4, without giving a term, value or wagon count. Why it matters: a lease renewal rather than a fleet purchase is a modest sign of European operators continuing to rely on lessors, with no direct read-through to North American demand.
