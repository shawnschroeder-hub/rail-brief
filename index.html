President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. A new trade case against Mexican-built tank cars names undercarriage parts in its proposed scope. UTLX's anti-dumping and anti-subsidy petitions allege dumping margins of 12.70% to 32.58%, and the proposed scope lists truck sets, wheelsets, couplers and draft systems as parts of a tank car and covers underframe assemblies imported on their own. Commerce must decide whether to open investigations within 20 days of the September 30 filing (extendable by 20 more), and the ITC holds its preliminary conference on October 21. These are allegations and the scope may be revised, but any components supplied from Mexico into tank car production are worth reviewing with trade counsel now.
  2. Trinity and Greenbrier say they will fight. Trinity, which has built tank cars at Monclova, Mexico since 1998, says it will defend itself at every stage, and Greenbrier, which points to its Marmaduke, Arkansas tank car plant, says the petitions are not supported by the facts or the law. The outcome could affect tank car sourcing and pricing for lessors and shippers, though a decision is months away.
  3. The merger record and federal policy moved in small steps. Shintech, a PVC producer served only by Union Pacific, told the STB it supports the UP–NS merger, while a former Class I and short-line executive warned of harm to short lines. USDOT designated $426 million in INFRA freight grants and the AAR backed a bipartisan Senate permitting-reform bill, both aimed mainly at infrastructure rather than equipment. Wabtec (October 21) and Union Pacific (October 22) report third-quarter results in two weeks.

UTLX files anti-dumping and anti-subsidy petitions against tank cars and parts from Mexico
Trains; scope and margins from Clark Hill trade-law summary · Oct 5 · trade · petition filed
UTLX Manufacturing, a Berkshire Hathaway unit, filed with the Commerce Department and the International Trade Commission on September 30, alleging dumping margins of 12.70% to 32.58% plus subsidies; Commerce has 20 days, extendable by 20, to decide whether to open investigations, and the ITC's preliminary conference is October 21. The proposed scope, which Commerce may revise, lists truck sets, wheelsets, couplers, draft systems and running gear as parts of a tank car and also covers underframe assemblies imported on their own. Why it matters: these are allegations, not duties, but if the cases proceed the scope language could reach undercarriage components moving from Mexico into tank car production.
PVC producer Shintech backs the UP–NS merger; a former rail executive warns of harm to short lines
Trains · Oct 6 · UP–NS merger · STB filings
Shintech, described by Trains as the largest US producer of polyvinyl chloride, told the Surface Transportation Board that Union Pacific carries all of the rail traffic from its three plants in Louisiana and Texas and that single-line service to eastern markets would increase speed and reduce costs. Separately, George Avery Grimes, who worked at Missouri Pacific, Union Pacific and Kansas City Southern, filed 23 pages arguing the merger poses serious harm for short lines and urging the Board to remove paper barriers and adopt a service standard. Why it matters: a captive chemical shipper's support cuts against chemical trade groups' opposition, while the short-line arguments point to conditions the Board could weigh.

Trinity and Greenbrier say they will contest the tank car trade petitions
Greenbrier; Trinity statement as reported by Trains · Oct 5 · trade · company statements
Trinity, which has built tank cars at Monclova, Mexico since 1998, said it will fully defend against the petitions in both the Commerce and ITC proceedings and noted that it ships American-made steel to its Mexican plants. Greenbrier said the petitions are not supported by the facts or the law, citing its tank car plant in Marmaduke, Arkansas and more than $500 million invested in its Arkansas operations since 2019. Why it matters: with two major tank car builders contesting the case, its outcome could affect tank car sourcing and pricing for lessors and shippers.

Court order puts the Luzerne County rail line sale on hold until an October 22 hearing
Trains · Oct 6 · short lines · court order
In a temporary order on October 5, a federal judge recorded the parties' agreement to keep the county redevelopment authority's ownership and Corman's service in place until the court rules on Corman's request for a preliminary injunction, with a hearing set for October 22. The order does not stop the authority from negotiating with the Reading & Northern or seeking Surface Transportation Board approval for a sale. Why it matters: shippers on the line keep their current service for now, but Corman's operating agreement still expires October 30.
JAXPORT renews Watco subsidiary's lease to run on-dock rail at Talleyrand Marine Terminal
Progressive Railroading · Oct 5 · ports · short lines
The JAXPORT board approved a six-year lease renewal, with options for four one-year extensions, for Jacksonville Port Terminal Railroad, which runs the on-dock rail system linking the terminal to CSX and Norfolk Southern. The railroad handles automobiles, chemicals, farm and food products, intermodal containers and pulp and paper. Why it matters: the renewal keeps a mixed-carload port connection to two Class I railroads in place for at least six years.

AAR backs a bipartisan Senate permitting-reform bill
AAR · Oct 2 · policy · trade-body statement
The AAR welcomed the introduction of the American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026, sponsored by Senators Capito, Lee, Heinrich and Whitehouse, with chief executive Ian Jefferies saying freight rail projects are too often delayed for years by burdensome environmental reviews. Jefferies called the bill's provisions an important step toward comprehensive permitting reform. Why it matters: the bill has only been introduced, and faster permitting would mainly speed track and terminal projects rather than equipment spending.

USDOT designates $426 million in INFRA freight grants for 12 projects
Progressive Railroading · Oct 5 · grants · funding designated
The awards under the Nationally Significant Multimodal Freight and Highway Projects program include $28 million for Lafayette, Louisiana to eliminate a high-risk grade crossing and freight bottleneck. Los Angeles Metro received $25 million for a State Route 71 expansion that rebuilds two highway bridges over Union Pacific and Metrolink tracks. Why it matters: most of the rail-related money goes to crossing and bridge work, so any direct effect on rail equipment demand is small.
