President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. A broken wheel is the preliminary cause of last week's Norfolk Southern derailment at Maplesville, Alabama. The Alabama Public Service Commission's transportation director told commissioners on October 6 that the eight-car derailment of a double-stack trash train was preliminarily caused by a broken wheel, and that the state is not investigating either of the two October 1 derailments because the causes are known. The finding is preliminary and no wheel maker or failure mode has been named, but wheel-related derailments tend to draw regulator and customer attention to wheel inspection and condition.
  2. Labor remains split on the UP–NS merger as earnings season approaches. The IBEW became the latest union to oppose the deal, saying Union Pacific declined to make meaningful commitments on jobs, while UP points to its jobs-for-life pledge and six supporting unions. Comments, protests and responsive applications are due at the STB on November 18. CSX reports third-quarter results on October 21, Union Pacific on October 22 and CN on October 30.
  3. Inspection and automation technology keeps attracting investment. Wabtec showed a rail-mounted inspection robot aimed first at yard brake checks, and autonomous rail start-up Parallel Systems closed a $100 million funding round. On the tank car trade case, Commerce's decision on whether to open investigations falls due around October 20, with the ITC conference on October 21.

Broken wheel preliminarily blamed for Norfolk Southern's Maplesville derailment; rail dropped from a truck caused the Springville wreck
Trains, citing WBMA-TV · Oct 7 · safety · state regulator briefing
Amanda Sheehan of the Alabama Public Service Commission told commissioners that the Maplesville derailment of eight cars of double-stack trash containers was preliminarily caused by a broken wheel, while at Springville a length of stick rail fell from a tractor-trailer struck at a private crossing and caused the derailment. She said the state is closing both incidents with no investigation because the causes are known. Why it matters: the wheel finding is preliminary and gives no detail on the wheel or how it failed, but it is a reminder that wheel condition remains a visible derailment cause for customers and regulators.
IBEW becomes the latest union to oppose the UP–NS merger
Trains · Oct 6 · UP–NS merger · labor position
The International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, which represents more than 10,000 rail workers who maintain locomotive, communications and train control systems, said Union Pacific declined to make meaningful commitments on craft jurisdiction, forced relocations and subcontracting. Union Pacific responded that the merger is expected to create about 1,200 net new union jobs by year three and that every union employee at the time of the merger will keep a union job, a pledge it has formalized with several unions including SMART-TD. Why it matters: Trains counts six unions on each side, with opponents saying they represent more than 66% of the two railroads' unionized employees, so labor conditions are likely to be part of what the Board weighs.

Wabtec shows RailGhost, a rail-mounted robot that inspects under trains
Railway Gazette International · Oct 6 · technology · InnoTrans demonstration
Wabtec says the platform from its Forgeworks incubator uses LiDAR, high-definition cameras and other sensors to travel beneath a train and capture condition data on hard-to-reach underframe and between-car components, with three iterations tested so far at MxV Rail in Pueblo. Its first application is replacing manual brake checks before freight trains leave US classification yards. Why it matters: automated undercar inspection, if adopted, would generate more consistent condition data on running gear and could change how railroads find and schedule component repairs.

NTSB final report on fatal BNSF conductor accident points to lack of on-track safety rule
Trains · Oct 6 · safety · NTSB final report
The NTSB said the lack of a requirement for on-track safety while releasing hand brakes on a parked train, plus an inadequate safety briefing, contributed to the October 2025 death of a BNSF conductor struck by a passing grain train at Columbus, Montana, where track centers are 14 feet, 4 inches apart. BNSF has since required ground crews to carry a handheld radio. Why it matters: hand-brake work beside live tracks is getting renewed attention, which could feed into crew procedures and interest in safer hand-brake and car-securement equipment.

Autonomous rail start-up Parallel Systems closes $100 million Series C
Parallel Systems via PR Newswire · Oct 7 · technology · company release
The battery-electric autonomous freight rail company says the round, led by AVP, will fund production of its third-generation Panther rail vehicle and international expansion, and that several major railroads are under contract to deploy its system, with a Georgia pilot running with Genesee & Wyoming under FRA supervision. The company says it has raised more than $200 million to date. Why it matters: the claims are the company's own and the system is still in pilot, but the funding shows continued investor interest in new short-haul rail vehicle concepts.

Rheinmetall reaches agreement in principle to take over Alstom's Kassel locomotive plant
Railway Gazette International · Oct 7 · Europe · agreement in principle
Alstom confirmed on October 6 an agreement in principle for defense company Rheinmetall to take over the former Henschel factory in Kassel, Germany, where Alstom builds Traxx locomotives, with details, union talks and a timetable still to be settled. Rheinmetall says Alstom employees at the site will be able to work for its land systems unit. Why it matters: the deal shifts European rail manufacturing capacity toward defense work, though what happens to Traxx production has not been announced.
