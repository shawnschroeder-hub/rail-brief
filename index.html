President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. October was a strong traffic month, and more of the fleet is back at work. Citing AAR data, FreightWaves reports record weekly-average intermodal volume in October and carloads up 3.4%, with 15 of 20 carload categories higher. Railcars in storage fell to 17.5% of the North American fleet, the lowest share since December 2022. That points to more cars in service, but storage and traffic figures do not by themselves predict new-car orders.
  2. Scrap-fed steelmaking is showing up in rail on both sides of the Atlantic. In the UK, WH Davis will build 56 high-capacity box wagons to carry scrap to an electric arc furnace in Cardiff. In the U.S., AAR's October data, as reported by FreightWaves, put iron and steel scrap carloads at their strongest since September 2008 and steel products at their best October since 2018. These are traffic signals for metals-related service, not evidence of a broader car-ordering trend.
  3. Three dates to watch in the next eight days, and no new rulings today. The USITC's preliminary determination in the antidumping and countervailing duty case on railway tank cars and parts from Mexico is expected around November 16, according to an October trade advisory from Sandler, Travis & Rosenberg. Comments in the UP–NS merger case are due November 18. The National Steel Car strike in Hamilton, which began August 12, is nearing three months with no reported settlement.

US rail traffic hits intermodal record as October freight gains signal economic resilience, says AAR
FreightWaves, citing AAR · Nov 11 · trade press · AAR monthly data · traffic and fleet storage
Citing AAR data, FreightWaves reports U.S. intermodal rose 9.6% in October to an average of more than 298,000 units a week, the highest weekly average for any month on record, while carloads rose 3.4% (4.5% excluding coal), motor vehicles nearly 10% and grain fell for the first time in 12 months. Railcars in storage fell to 17.5% of the North American fleet, the lowest share since December 2022. Why it matters: fewer stored cars means more of the fleet is in service, which can support running-repair demand over time, though it does not by itself signal new-car orders.

OmniTRAX names Strickland VP, state government affairs
Progressive Railroading · Nov 10 · trade press · short-line holding company · people
OmniTRAX promoted Justin Strickland, its director of economic and industrial development for the past six years, to vice president of state government affairs, leading engagement with state governments across its network on freight transportation, infrastructure investment and economic growth. Why it matters: it is a minor personnel move, but it shows a short-line holding company putting more weight on state infrastructure funding and industrial development, the usual routes to new carload customers.

WH Davis to build 56 MWA box wagons for GBRf scrap traffic to Cardiff
RailFreight.com (paywalled; readable summary only) · Nov 10 · trade press · UK · wagon order
UK builder WH Davis will build 56 MWA high-capacity box wagons, leased by Porterbrook to GB Railfreight, to carry scrap to 7 Steel's electric arc furnace in Cardiff, and Porterbrook says each holds 75 cubic metres, about 25% more than a standard box wagon. Value and delivery timing were not in the readable text. Why it matters: it is a small order, but scrap-fed electric-furnace steel is also showing up in North American traffic, where AAR reported October iron and steel scrap carloads at their strongest since September 2008.
