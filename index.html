President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Opposition to the UP–NS merger is widening. An opposition coalition says 57 more parties, including Cleveland-Cliffs, Marathon Petroleum, J.B. Hunt and the American Farm Bureau Federation, formally opposed the deal in the latest STB comment round. Those are the coalition's own counts, and the applicants' CEOs restated their case at RailTrends on November 23 without new figures. The Board has not ruled; completeness comments on the responsive applications are due November 27.
  2. Union Pacific faces a labor arbitration bill estimated near $98.8 million. The BLET says a November 17 supplemental award requires UP to compensate engineers for late implementation of an 11-days-on, 4-days-off work/rest agreement, and UP's panel member dissented. The union says the final amount may be higher or lower. It is a one-time labor cost, not a signal about car or component spending.
  3. Small equipment and demand signals heading into Thanksgiving. Ohio approved a $140,000 grant toward a shipper's purchase of 20 hopper cars, and Intramotev is taking its autonomous railcars to a second Watco site in Texas. In trucking, SONAR data show the premium shippers pay when a contract carrier rejects a load rose above 10% last week. A tighter truck market can favor intermodal at the margin, but one week of data does not set a trend.

America's Biggest Manufacturers, Farmers, Railroads and Labor Unions Unite Against UP-NS Merger
PR Newswire, Stop The Rail Merger Coalition · Nov 24 · UP–NS merger · advocacy release
The coalition says 57 additional parties, including Cleveland-Cliffs, Marathon Petroleum and the American Farm Bureau Federation, formally opposed the proposed $85 billion merger in the latest STB comment round, bringing its count to 97 parties seeking rejection and 142 seeking conditions; it also lists CSX, BNSF and CPKC as opponents. These are the coalition's own tallies, and the STB has issued no decision on the merits. Why it matters: the conditions being sought, as much as outright rejection, could shape routing and interchange terms if the deal is approved.

BLET wins UP arbitration, anticipates record $98.8 million payout
BLET · Nov 20 · Union Pacific · labor · union release
The union says a November 17 supplemental award from Special Board of Adjustment 1213 requires Union Pacific to compensate engineers for not implementing a negotiated 11/4 work/rest agreement on time, covering claims from June 30, 2024 through January 27, 2025, with a payout it estimates at approximately $98.8 million that "may be higher or lower." UP's representative on the board dissented. Why it matters: a one-time labor cost at a major customer, with no stated bearing on car or component spending.
Intramotev expands partnership with Watco
Progressive Railroading, citing Intramotev · Nov 23 · short lines · technology
Intramotev will deploy its TugVolt autonomous railcars at a second Watco site, in Seadrift, Texas, to switch cars inside an industrial facility, building on its Wood River, Illinois, terminal where TugVolt is in revenue service moving soda ash. Why it matters: self-propelled cars remain a niche in-plant switching application, but they are a new car type moving from pilot toward wider use.

Ohio commission approves funding for 3 rail projects
Progressive Railroading, citing the Ohio Rail Development Commission · Nov 23 · shippers · state grants
The commission approved help for three projects it says account for nearly $151 million in investment, including a $140,000 grant for Winchester Ag Service to buy 20 hopper cars and a $90,000 grant for a Weidmann Electrical Technology spur in Middletown with at least 208 new carloads committed within three years. Why it matters: small in size, but it shows state money going directly into freight-car purchases and new rail-served shippers.
Shippers' backup plan is getting more expensive
FreightWaves, citing SONAR data · Nov 21 · trucking · market data
SONAR's second-tender routing guide premium, the extra cost when a primary contract truck carrier rejects a load, reached 10.69% on Thursday, up from 8.82% a week earlier, while first-tender acceptance slid 88 basis points to 88.06%. Why it matters: a tighter truck market can make intermodal more competitive at the margin, though a single pre-holiday week is not a trend.
