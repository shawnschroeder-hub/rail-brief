President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. FreightCar America lowered its 2026 outlook after removing a 1,285-car tank car retrofit order from its backlog. Backlog at September 30 was 2,660 units valued at $284.5 million, and the company now expects 3,100–3,300 deliveries this year against 3,500–3,900 previously. It said it believes the customer is unlikely to meet its contract obligations, so the figures describe one builder's order book rather than industry orders. Full third-quarter results are due November 9.
  2. A large railcar lessor is changing hands: KKR agreed to sell Infinity Transportation to Ares funds at an enterprise value of about $2.9 billion. Infinity owns and manages about 50,000 railcars leased to more than 350 customers in the U.S., Canada and Mexico, and closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027. This transfers an existing fleet rather than placing a new-build order, although Ares said it plans to support fleet growth.
  3. Intermodal is the clearest demand signal in today's sweep. J.B. Hunt's third-quarter intermodal loads rose 8%, with eastern-network shipments up 18% against 1% on its transcontinental network. The NTSB is also investigating the October 15 derailment of about 39 Union Pacific cars at Greensburg, Kansas, with no cause reported. J.B. Hunt's figures are one marketer's results and the derailment's cause is not yet known, so neither is a demand signal for components on its own.

FreightCar America, Inc. Updates Full-Year 2026 Outlook and Reports Preliminary Third Quarter 2026 Results
FreightCar America (SEC filing) · Oct 19 · railcar builder · outlook and backlog update
FreightCar America removed 1,285 railcars tied to a tank car retrofit order from its backlog because it believes the customer is unlikely to meet its contract obligations, leaving a September 30 backlog of 2,660 units valued at $284.5 million, and cut its 2026 outlook to 3,100–3,300 deliveries, $370–410 million of revenue and $15–19 million of adjusted EBITDA (from 3,500–3,900, $410–460 million and $36–44 million). Preliminary unaudited third-quarter results show 662 deliveries and $75–77 million of revenue, against 1,304 deliveries and $160.5 million a year earlier. Why it matters: the removal is specific to one customer's tank car retrofit contract, so it is a company-specific signal rather than a read on industry-wide orders.
KKR To Sell Infinity Transportation To Ares Management For About $2.9 Bln
RTTNews · Oct 19 · railcar lessor · M&A (fleet figures per Pulse 2.0's report of the announcement)
KKR agreed to sell Infinity Transportation, a railcar and intermodal equipment lessor, to funds managed by Ares at an enterprise value of about $2.9 billion, with closing expected in the first quarter of 2027 subject to regulatory approvals. Infinity owns and manages about 50,000 railcars leased to more than 350 customers across the U.S., Canada and Mexico, per Pulse 2.0's report of the announcement. Why it matters: this is a change of ownership of an existing fleet rather than a new-build order, so it does not by itself change demand for new cars or components.
NTSB to investigate Union Pacific derailment in Kansas
Trains (via Yahoo News) · Oct 15 · Union Pacific · derailment under NTSB investigation
Union Pacific said approximately 39 cars of a freight train derailed at about 8:45 a.m. on October 15 near Highway 54 in Greensburg, Kansas, on its Pratt Subdivision, with no injuries and no releases. The NTSB said in a social media post that it is investigating, and the cause remains under investigation. Why it matters: no cause has been reported, so it is not yet known whether track, equipment or operations were involved.

CSX tests drone technology for enhanced rail-car inspections
Progressive Railroading · Oct 19 · CSX · car inspection trial
CSX tested drones to find leftover coal in empty cars at its Curtis Bay coal terminal in Baltimore, covering the terminal's empty-car yard in about 30 minutes, with approved routes for both day and night inspection. No partially loaded cars appeared during the trial because of seasonal conditions, and the risk is higher in cold weather when residual coal can freeze in place. Why it matters: it is an inspection-process trial at one terminal and says nothing about car design or purchasing.

State leaders share projects selected for CRISI grants
Progressive Railroading · Oct 19 · FRA CRISI program · grant awards announced by states
Federal lawmakers and state transportation departments announced seven projects in five states from the latest round of the FRA's CRISI program, totaling roughly $470 million, including $218 million for Wisconsin's Hiawatha West expansion and $130 million for Massachusetts' Springfield Hub Modernization. The FRA had not yet published the full recipient list, so the figures reflect state announcements only. Why it matters: the awards fund track, bridge and siding work rather than rolling-stock purchases.

J.B. Hunt experiences growing pains in Q3
FreightWaves · Oct 16 · J.B. Hunt third-quarter results (volume and revenue figures also checked against its Oct 15 SEC filing)
Hunt reported third-quarter intermodal volume up 8% to 585,582 loads, with segment revenue of about $1.83 billion (up 20%) and operating income of $147.5 million (up 18%). FreightWaves reported that eastern-network shipments rose 18% against 1% on the transcontinental network, and that container utilization was 92.1%. Why it matters: the growth is concentrated in the East and reflects one intermodal marketer's results, so it speaks to intermodal demand rather than to railcar-component demand.

New agreement standardises Middle Corridor operations, more countries can join
RailFreight.com · Oct 20 · Kazakhstan and Azerbaijan · China–Europe Middle Corridor
Kazakhstan and Azerbaijan agreed to streamline rail operations on the Middle Corridor, with a minimum guaranteed throughput (figure undisclosed), tariff stability, a single rail-maritime transport document and a single logistics operator, and other countries may join. The article cites Middle Corridor freight volume up 30% and China–Europe container transit up 64% in January–September 2026 against the same period of 2025. Why it matters: it is a corridor-coordination agreement rather than a wagon order, so it is an indirect signal for rolling-stock demand.
DB Cargo Iberia to close axle-change facility in France
RailFreight.com · Oct 20 · DB Cargo Iberia · Cerbère facility closure
DB Cargo Iberia will close its axle-change facility at Cerbère, on the French side of the Spanish border, by the end of 2026, citing a continuing decline in business; about 35–40 employees work at the site. Staff held a work-to-rule strike this summer, and unions plan a demonstration in Perpignan on November 5. Why it matters: it is a European operating-facility closure with no direct North American read-through.
