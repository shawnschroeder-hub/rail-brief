President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The STB has spelled out how far federal rail jurisdiction reaches over local rules. Its new policy statement in docket EP 791 gathers Board and court precedent on preemption, including zoning, blocked crossings and locomotive idling, and the AAR welcomed it. The Board says the statement makes no binding determinations, so its practical effect will show only in future disputes over rail-served facilities.
  2. BNSF's third quarter adds to a strong Class I earnings season. BNSF's net earnings rose 19.3% to $1.73 billion on volumes up 3.9%, though coal volumes fell 8.4%. Strong railroad earnings support maintenance and service spending, but they do not by themselves translate into new-car orders.
  3. The Los Angeles–Long Beach watchmen dispute has been pushed to November 20. Employers and ILWU Local 26 extended talks rather than settle, so the risk to western import intermodal flows is deferred, not removed. Competitor news was quiet over the weekend: FreightCar America reports third-quarter results after market close today, and the National Steel Car strike that began August 12 has not been reported settled.

STB Issues Policy Statement on Federal Preemption
Surface Transportation Board · Nov 9 · agency release · policy statement · EP 791
The STB issued a policy statement in docket EP 791 consolidating Board and court precedent on its jurisdiction and on preemption of state and local laws under the ICC Termination Act, covering issues such as abandonments, blocked crossings, locomotive idling, zoning and nuisance claims, in response to a September 2025 request from railroad and shipper groups. FreightWaves reports the statement proposes a two-part test for when restrictions aimed at a rail customer's facility effectively regulate rail service, says it makes no binding determinations, and was welcomed by AAR President Ian Jefferies. Why it matters: clearer preemption rules could reduce local challenges to rail-served plants and terminals, though the statement is guidance rather than a ruling.
BNSF Q3 earnings climb 19.3% as rail freight volumes rise
FreightWaves, citing Berkshire Hathaway's filing · Nov 9 · trade press · earnings · BNSF
BNSF's third-quarter net earnings rose 19.3% to $1.73 billion from $1.45 billion, on railroad operating revenue of $6.87 billion, up 14.7%, and car and unit volumes up 3.9%, while coal volumes fell 8.4%. Berkshire Hathaway attributed the gain to higher freight volumes and operating efficiencies that offset higher fuel costs. Why it matters: BNSF is the largest railroad outside the proposed merger, and its results confirm volume growth this year, though railroad profits do not translate directly into car or component orders.
Employers, Watchmen at California Ports Extend Contract Talks
Reuters, via GV Wire · Nov 7 · wire report · ports · labor
The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26 extended their bargaining deadline for Los Angeles and Long Beach port watchmen through November 20, averting a strike that could have begun after the November 6 deadline. The watchmen are roughly 2% of workers at the port complex, but the employers have said a strike would disrupt terminals handling most of Long Beach's cargo. Why it matters: the risk to import containers feeding western intermodal trains is deferred to a new deadline rather than resolved.

CSX Leaves Three Days Out of the Weekly Reports
American-Rails.com, citing a CSX note posted by the STB · Nov 8 · trade press · Class I service data · CSX
CSX told the STB it will omit October 27 through 29 from its EP 724 and EP 787 weekly service reports for the week ending October 30, because a system outage on October 27 disrupted the systems that feed those metrics. CSX said it cannot recover the missing days but will file restated reports if the data is recovered. Why it matters: the gap leaves a hole in public service metrics for the week of the outage, so CSX's performance that week cannot be compared with its peers.
Colonial Terminals opens NS-served Savannah breakbulk facility
Progressive Railroading · Nov 6 · trade press · new rail-served terminal · Norfolk Southern
Colonial Terminals opened Foundation 3, a 25-acre breakbulk terminal on the Savannah River with an 810-foot dock, Norfolk Southern service and more than 14,000 feet of onsite track. It will handle steel, forest products, project cargo and other general cargo, with 130,000 square feet of rail-served warehouses. Why it matters: new rail-served capacity for steel and forest products adds carload opportunities in the Southeast, though no volume figures were given.

Rotterdam Rail Feeding in trouble: suspension of payments granted by court
RailFreight.com, citing SpoorPro · Nov 9 · trade press · Netherlands
A Rotterdam court granted provisional suspension of payments to Rotterdam Rail Feeding, a rail freight and shunting operator, with a creditors' meeting set for February 9, 2027, and the cause of its financial problems not yet disclosed. Why it matters: it adds to signs of financial strain among European freight operators, though the company's size and fleet were not reported.
