President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The US International Trade Commission's final vote clears the way for duty orders on Czech and Indian couplers. The Commission determined on October 16 that the US industry is materially injured by freight rail couplers and parts from the Czech Republic and India. Commerce will now issue antidumping orders on imports from both countries and a countervailing duty order on India's. The duty rates were set by Commerce in September; this vote is the step that turns them into formal orders, and it covers imports from these two countries only.
  2. The separate trade case on Mexican tank cars held its first Commission hearing on October 21. MLex reported that industry representatives argued for antidumping and countervailing duties and that US tank car companies were split on the antidumping question. Trinity Industries has said it will participate fully in both the Commerce and Commission proceedings. The Commission's preliminary vote decides whether the investigations continue, so it is the next point to watch.
  3. Public infrastructure money keeps flowing to short lines and ports, but into track rather than cars. New CRISI awards to the Chesapeake and Indiana ($6.8 million) and Northern Lines ($1.3 million), and Canada's C$254 million for Port Saint John, fund ties, bridges, welded rail and sidings. CPKC reports third-quarter results after the market closes on October 28, Timken on November 4 and FreightCar America on November 9, and Greenbrier has scheduled its fiscal fourth-quarter release. The National Steel Car strike that began August 12 has not been reported settled.

Imported Czech and Indian wagon couplers harmed US suppliers, USITC rules
Railway Gazette International · Oct 23 (Commission determination announced Oct 16) · couplers · trade remedy
The US International Trade Commission determined that US manufacturers are materially injured by freight rail couplers and parts from the Czech Republic and India, which Commerce found are sold at less than fair value, with the Indian products also subsidised. Antidumping orders will be imposed on imports from both countries and a countervailing duty order on imports from India, following petitions the Coalition of Freight Coupler Producers filed in July 2025. Why it matters: the orders make Commerce's duty rates enforceable on covered coupler parts from these two countries, which would raise their landed cost in the US but does not affect imports from other sources.
US tank car companies split on antidumping duties for Mexican imports in USITC hearing
MLex (lead paragraph only; full article paywalled) · Oct 21 · tank cars · trade remedy
At the Commission's October 21 preliminary-phase hearing, industry representatives argued that it should impose antidumping and countervailing duties on railway tank cars and components from Mexico, and MLex reported that US tank car companies were split on the antidumping question. The case stems from petitions UTLX Manufacturing, a Berkshire Hathaway subsidiary, filed on September 30; which companies took which side could not be confirmed from the readable text. Why it matters: a negative preliminary vote would end the investigations, so the Commission's decision is the first official signal of whether Mexican-built tank cars face duties.

Pinsly Railroad Co. adds Illinois-based short line to portfolio
Progressive Railroading · Oct 22 · Pinsly Railroad Co. · short-line acquisition
Pinsly Railroad Co. is adding the 160-mile Kankakee, Beaverville and Southern Railroad, which serves a mostly agricultural region of Illinois and Indiana, handles more than 10,000 carloads a year and connects with CN, CSX, Norfolk Southern, Union Pacific and Genesee & Wyoming's Toledo, Peoria and Western. The deal takes Pinsly to 10 short lines, and the company said it plans to invest in the line's infrastructure and attract new customers; the seller was not named. Why it matters: it is an ownership change on an existing grain-oriented short line rather than a source of new car demand.
Shipper Industries & Demand 1 story
CHS's $700 Million Wisconsin Soybean Project Targets New Oil and Meal Flows
TankTransport (reporting CHS's announcements) · Oct 16 · agriculture · Union Pacific-served plant
CHS held a ceremonial groundbreaking on October 7 for a planned soybean-processing plant of about $700 million in Evansville, Wisconsin, designed to process roughly 80 million bushels a year into about 1 billion pounds of oil and 2 million tons of meal, with three in-plant rail loops connecting to Union Pacific. Construction depends on required permitting, and the September 14 announcement targeted completion in fall 2028, with oil aimed at renewable-fuel markets and CHS's Mankato, Minnesota refinery. Why it matters: it is a potential rail-served source of oil and meal traffic about two years out, but the truck-versus-rail split for outbound product has not been disclosed.
Regulatory & Policy 3 stories
Canada invests C$254M to boost Port Saint John capacity
Progressive Railroading · Oct 23 · Government of Canada · port and rail infrastructure funding
The Government of Canada allocated C$254 million to the Saint John Trade Corridor project in New Brunswick, whose partners include New Brunswick Southern Railway, the Saint John Port Authority and provincial agencies, as the port targets 850,000 to 1 million TEUs a year. Planned work includes continuously welded rail across 64 miles of track, shoreline stabilization at the Bayshore Rail Terminal and reconstruction of Simms Corner to separate rail and road traffic. Why it matters: it is public spending on track and port capacity rather than railcar purchasing.
Chesapeake and Indiana Railroad Co. to receive $6.8M CRISI grant
Progressive Railroading · Oct 23 · Chesapeake and Indiana Railroad (Pinsly) · FRA CRISI award
The Chesapeake and Indiana Railroad Co., a Pinsly subsidiary running 28 miles of track in LaPorte County, Indiana, with connections to Norfolk Southern and CSX, will receive $6.8 million in FRA CRISI funding, with a 30% non-federal match, for tie replacement, freight bridge rehabilitation, grade-crossing renewal and 1 mile of new siding. Why it matters: it is track and bridge work on one short line rather than rolling-stock purchasing.
Northern Lines Railway attains CRISI grant for Minnesota track rehab
Progressive Railroading · Oct 23 · Northern Lines Railway (Anacostia Rail Holdings) · FRA CRISI award
Northern Lines Railway, which operates 25 miles of BNSF-owned track in Minnesota's St. Cloud region and interchanges 10,000 carloads a year with BNSF, received a $1.3 million CRISI grant, with a 35% non-federal share, to replace deteriorated ties and correct track surface and alignment defects. Why it matters: the work is aimed at reducing derailment risk on a line that moves hazardous materials, but it is track maintenance rather than equipment spending.

Transnet Freight Rail claims record for the world's longest train
Railway Gazette International · Oct 21 · South Africa · heavy-haul operations
Transnet Freight Rail said it ran a 7,602-metre train of 696 empty CR13/14 ore wagons on the Sishen–Saldanha heavy-haul line, reaching Loop 18 from Saldanha on October 18, with Class 15E electric and Class 43D diesel-electric locomotives spread along the train under Radio Distributed Power. The record it cites as beaten is 7,353 metres, set in June 2001 by a BHP Iron Ore train of 682 wagons in Western Australia, according to Guinness World Records. Why it matters: it is the company's own record claim and a test of long-train handling, not a change in wagon demand.
