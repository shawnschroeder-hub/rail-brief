The Rail Data Brief
Edition 067 · Thursday, November 19, 2026 · 4 stories
President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. U.S. rail traffic rose 7.3% in the week ending November 14. AAR data show intermodal up 10.7% and carloads up 3.3%, with eight of 10 carload groups higher, led by motor vehicles and parts (up 16.2%) and metallic ores and metals (up 10.7%), while grain fell 4.7%. Year-to-date U.S. volume is up 4%. Carload gains are the part of the traffic most tied to freight-car wear, but one week's data does not signal new car orders.
  2. New rail-served terminals keep coming online in the Southeast. BWC Terminals opened the first phase of a $316 million, CSX-served petroleum products terminal at Pascagoula, Mississippi, with unit-train unloading. Silvi Materials signed a 30-year lease for a CSX-served cement import terminal at the Port of Wilmington with two rail loadouts, but it is not expected to operate until fall 2029. These add origins and destinations for tank and covered-hopper traffic over time rather than immediately.
  3. Short-line dispatching is going digital. Watco has finished rolling out Tracsis's computer-aided dispatch platform across its roughly 48 short lines, and Tracsis says its system now covers over half of U.S. short-line and regional route miles. This is an operations-technology story with no direct read-through to railcar component demand.

US freight-rail traffic ticks up in week 45
Progressive Railroading, citing AAR data · Nov 19 · weekly traffic · week ending Nov 14
U.S. railroads moved 529,919 carloads and intermodal units, up 7.3% from a year earlier, with carloads up 3.3% to 230,260 and intermodal up 10.7% to 299,659; motor vehicles and parts rose 16.2% and grain fell 4.7%. Canadian carloads rose 5.6% and Mexican carloads fell 2.7%, while U.S. year-to-date volume of 23,171,466 units is up 4%. Why it matters: steady carload growth keeps the existing fleet working, though weekly traffic is a usage measure rather than a signal of new car or component orders.

BWC Terminals opens CSX-served facility in Mississippi
Progressive Railroading · Nov 19 · CSX · petroleum terminal
BWC Terminals has completed the first phase of a $316 million terminal at Pascagoula, Mississippi, next to Chevron's Pascagoula refinery, with seven above-ground storage tanks and unit-train unloading. Chevron Products Co. will use it to receive, store and deliver petroleum products. Why it matters: a new unit-train destination adds a potential source of tank-car moves on CSX, though the article gives no volume or car counts.
CSX to serve new Silvi Materials facility at North Carolina port
Progressive Railroading · Nov 18 · CSX · cement import terminal
NC Ports and Silvi Materials signed a 30-year lease for a cement import terminal at the Port of Wilmington with a 100,000-ton storage dome, two rail loadouts and four truck loadouts, with construction starting in spring 2028 and operations expected in fall 2029. Silvi plans to distribute cement across the Southeast over CSX's network. Why it matters: this is a long-dated new source of covered-hopper traffic, with no effect on car demand before the end of the decade.
Tracsis deploys dispatch tech on Watco railroads
Progressive Railroading, citing Tracsis · Nov 18 · short lines · dispatch technology
Watco has completed its rollout of Tracsis's computer-aided dispatch platform across its roughly 48 short lines and more than 8,000 miles of track, under a multi-year service agreement. Tracsis says its dispatch technology now covers over 50% of U.S. short-line and regional route miles. Why it matters: digital dispatching can improve short-line fluidity, but the effect on railcar component demand is indirect at most.
