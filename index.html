President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. West Coast imports hit a record. The Port of Los Angeles moved 1,042,652 TEUs in September, its busiest month ever, with loaded imports up 19% from a year earlier. The port's executive director credited lower tariffs and problems on the Suez and Panama Canal routes for sending more cargo to the West Coast. That supports intermodal volume out of Southern California this fall, though it says little directly about carload equipment.
  2. Mexican vehicle output fell sharply. Mexico's car production dropped 15.1% in September to 301,803 units, with steep declines at General Motors and Nissan amid uncertainty over US trade policy. Lower assembly could trim cross-border parts and finished-vehicle rail traffic. This is one month of data, and some production may shift to US plants rather than disappear.
  3. Key supplier and trade dates are approaching. Greenbrier will report its fiscal fourth quarter on October 26, joining the Class I and Trinity reports already scheduled for late October. In the Mexican tank car trade case, Commerce's decision on whether to open investigations is due around October 20, with the ITC conference on October 21. Together these will shape the read on 2027 car-building demand.

Port of LA marks busiest month on record
Progressive Railroading · Oct 12 · intermodal · Port of Los Angeles data
The Port of Los Angeles handled 1,042,652 TEUs in September, the busiest month in its 118-year history, with loaded imports up 19% and loaded exports up 10% from a year earlier; the July–September quarter was also a record at nearly 3 million TEUs. Executive Director Gene Seroka cited lower tariffs and challenges on the Suez and Panama Canal routes, which sent more shipments to the West Coast. Why it matters: heavy San Pedro Bay volume feeds inland intermodal rail, though the report gives no rail-specific figures and record empty containers (about 371,206 TEUs) point to import-heavy flows.

State, CSX agree to rail upgrades in the Berkshires
GBH News (State House News Service) · Oct 8 · CSX · state agreement
Massachusetts' transportation department agreed with CSX to fund a new siding and other track work around Pittsfield and to study a new platform at Pittsfield Station; MassDOT's capital plan carries $31 million for the Pittsfield track capacity project. The state says the work will reduce conflicts between passenger and freight trains on CSX-owned track and support permanent seasonal weekend Berkshire Flyer service, while CSX's own commitments were not specified. Why it matters: it is a concrete case of a state paying for capacity on a Class I line that freight trains also use.
Louisiana port receives FastSites loan for UP rail spur
Progressive Railroading · Oct 12 · Union Pacific · state economic development
Louisiana Economic Development is lending $1 million through its FastSites program to the Port of Columbia in Caldwell Parish, closing the funding gap on a $7 million spur linking the port's 400-acre certified site to a Union Pacific main line. Bidding is expected to start in November, with construction slated to begin in the first quarter of 2027 and finish in 2028. Why it matters: new rail-served industrial sites are one way railroads add carload customers over time, though no tenant or cargo has been named.

Mexico auto output falls as US tariff risks weigh
Trading Economics · Oct 7 · automotive · production data
Mexico's car production fell 15.1% from a year earlier to 301,803 units in September, after a 1.4% decline in August, according to Trading Economics, which tied the drop to uncertainty over changing US trade policy. Output fell 27.1% at General Motors and 26.3% at Nissan. Why it matters: lower Mexican assembly can reduce cross-border auto-parts and finished-vehicle rail traffic, though the effect on equipment demand depends on how much production shifts to US plants.

Alstom delivers 18 locomotives to CLIP Intermodal
RailFreight.com · Oct 13 · Europe · equipment delivery
Alstom has delivered 18 Traxx Universal locomotives, partly built in Poland, to Polish operator CLIP Intermodal, against an order for 13 placed in 2024 and five more announced in 2025. Thirteen are approved for Poland, Germany, Belgium, Luxembourg, Austria and France, and five for routes through Central Europe, all fitted with ETCS-capable onboard signalling. Why it matters: continued investment in cross-border traction by European intermodal operators is a sign of confidence in long-haul rail freight there, though it is locomotive rather than wagon demand.
