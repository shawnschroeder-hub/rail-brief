President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. A major short-line holding company is preparing to break itself up. Genesee & Wyoming plans to split into three regional holding companies on January 1, 2027, pending regulatory approval, and FreightWaves reports the move is meant to position the regional pieces for eventual sale. G&W also provides industrial railcar switching and repair, so a change of owners could, over time, change who makes purchasing decisions for parts of that network. Separately, a one-day engineers' strike on the Wheeling & Lake Erie ended with the railroad agreeing to stop using managers in place of union conductors.
  2. Rail volumes are strong, but some demand signals are softening. AAR data for the week ending November 7 show U.S. traffic up 6.6% and intermodal up 11%, with Mexican carloads up 20.3%. At the same time, Mexico's October light-vehicle output fell 17.7%, and NRF and Hackett expect U.S. import growth to slow to about 1% in November and December. Weekly gains and the monthly indicators may not move together through the winter.
  3. Three dates to watch in the next eight days. The USITC's preliminary determination in the antidumping and countervailing duty case on railway tank cars and parts from Mexico is due by November 16. Comments in the UP–NS merger case are due November 18. The National Steel Car strike in Hamilton, which began August 12, has now passed three months with no reported settlement.

Genesee & Wyoming prepares to split into three rail holding companies
FreightWaves (Trains staff) · Nov 12 · trade press · short-line holding company · restructuring
Effective January 1, 2027, pending regulatory approval, G&W will separate into Atlantic & Great Lakes Transportation (New England, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania), Canyon & Cascade Transportation (five western states) and a remaining Genesee & Wyoming with the Midwest, Southeast, Central U.S. and Canada, about 65% of today's business, all still owned by Brookfield and GIC. G&W operates more than 100 railroads serving about 2,000 customers, and FreightWaves reports the split is intended to position the regional entities for eventual sale. Why it matters: separate boards and capital structures, and possibly new owners later, could change how purchasing for track, car repair and switching services is handled across a large share of the short-line network.
BLET ends Wheeling & Lake Erie strike
FreightWaves · Nov 11–12 · trade press · regional railroad · labor
About 140 BLET engineers and conductors struck the roughly 840-mile Wheeling & Lake Erie at noon on November 11 over managers filling crew positions, and the union ended the strike the same day after the railroad agreed to honor contractual protections and stop using managers in place of union conductors. The network runs from Hagerstown, Maryland, to Toledo, Ohio, and serves steel mills and other industrial customers. Why it matters: the stoppage was brief, but crew-staffing disputes on regional railroads can quickly interrupt service to metals and industrial shippers.
US freight railroads report traffic gains in Week 44
Progressive Railroading, citing AAR · Nov 12 · trade press · AAR weekly data · week ending Nov 7
U.S. railroads moved 526,283 carloads and intermodal units, up 6.6%, with carloads up 1.3% to 227,914 and intermodal up 11% to 298,369; motor vehicles and parts rose 10.4% and forest products 10.1%, while coal, grain and nonmetallic minerals fell. Mexican carloads rose 20.3% and Canadian carloads 4.3%, and U.S. volume is up 3.9% year to date. Why it matters: the gains are led by intermodal and automotive traffic, so they say more about container and autorack demand than about the carload fleet as a whole.

Port Tracker report: Import volume 'strong' as peak season winds down
Progressive Railroading, citing NRF and Hackett Associates · Nov 10 · trade press · imports forecast
According to the NRF and Hackett Associates Global Port Tracker, U.S. ports handled 2.37 million TEUs of imports in September, up 12.6% and the busiest month of 2026, with October estimated at 2.26 million TEUs, up 9.1%. The forecast slows to 2.05 million TEUs in November (up 1.2%) and 2.03 million in December (up 1.3%), for about 26 million TEUs in 2026, up 2%. Why it matters: import-driven intermodal volumes may cool from record levels through year-end, though the forecast is the publishers' estimate, not reported port data.
Georgia Ports execs share auto business successes
Progressive Railroading · Nov 11 · trade press · ports · automotive
At the State of the Port of Brunswick event on November 10, Georgia Ports Authority executives said Brunswick has grown 277% over 10 years and leads U.S. ports in automotive exports and imports, with Hyundai, Kia and Rivian plants nearby and Audi's newly announced Georgia plant linked to Norfolk Southern service. Savannah's Mason Mega Rail facility handles 42 double-stack trains a week, served by Norfolk Southern and CSX. Why it matters: Southeast auto manufacturing is a growing source of finished-vehicle rail traffic, but no rail volumes were given, so it is a direction rather than a measured demand figure.
USDA November WASDE cuts corn crop and exports, raises soybean exports
DTN, citing USDA · Nov 10 · ag press · USDA WASDE · grain
USDA cut its 2026/27 corn yield to 179.6 bushels an acre from 181.2, lowering production to 15.893 billion bushels and exports to 3.275 billion bushels from 3.3 billion. Soybean exports were raised to 1.72 billion bushels from 1.695 billion, and wheat production and exports were unchanged. Why it matters: the export changes roughly offset each other, so the report points to a large but slightly smaller corn movement, consistent with AAR's recent declines in grain carloads.
Mexico car output slumps 17.7% in October
Trading Economics · Nov 9 · data service · Mexico · automotive
Mexico's light-vehicle production fell 17.7% year over year to 302,803 units in October from 367,928, and January–October output was about 3.25 million vehicles, down 3.9%. Why it matters: Mexican assembly feeds cross-border autorack and auto-parts traffic, so a sustained drop would weigh on that flow even as U.S. motor-vehicle carloads are currently rising.

Indian Railways orders 9,638 additional wagons in ₹3,925-crore freight capacity push
Maritime Gateway · Nov 4 · trade press · India · wagon orders
The Railway Board used an option clause in existing contracts to order 9,638 more open wagons worth about ₹3,925 crore, a 24.57% increase in quantity, split among Titagarh (4,955), Jupiter Wagons (2,583), Texmaco (1,020), Besco (720) and Oriental Foundry (360), with deliveries due by June 2027. This is an add-on to existing contracts, not the separately reported 100,000-wagon tender, which has not been confirmed as floated. Why it matters: India keeps adding wagon capacity through its domestic builders, which supports demand for bogies, couplers and bearings in that market.
