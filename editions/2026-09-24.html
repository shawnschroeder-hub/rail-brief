President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Commerce finalized steep antidumping and countervailing duties on freight rail couplers from India and the Czech Republic. India's largest named producers (Bhilai Engineering, Jupiter Wagons) face a 71.01% dumping margin plus a 75% subsidy rate; the Czech lead respondent (CKD Kutna Hora) faces 73.74%; India's "all others" rate is 5.24% dumping plus 9.71% subsidy. The scope covers couplers, knuckles, coupler bodies, locks, lock lift assemblies, knuckle pins, knuckle throwers and rotors — core Amsted Rail territory. The ITC's concurrent injury investigation will determine whether formal duty orders are issued.
  2. Greenbrier booked $600 million of new railcar orders (3,400 cars) in its fiscal fourth quarter ended August 31, including a 780-car order from Saudi Arabia's SAR railway. Industry-wide, roughly 200,000 railcars are approaching retirement while 2026 output of about 25,000 cars runs below replacement need, with production expected to rise to 30,000-35,000 cars in 2027. Strong builder order books are a leading indicator for undercarriage component demand across the industry.
  3. The NTSB urged the FRA and Class I railroads to prohibit workers from riding rail equipment for point protection during shoving movements. The board has investigated 16 such accidents since October 2020 that killed 14 workers and seriously injured 2 more, with two additional investigations opened since August 2026. It wants documented hazard assessments before work begins and recommends ground-based alternatives — vehicles, cameras and other technology — in place of riding equipment; this is a recommendation, not a binding rule, but signals where FRA enforcement attention may go next.

Commerce finalizes AD/CVD margins on freight rail couplers from India and Czech Republic
US Department of Commerce (International Trade Administration) · announced Sep 21, published Sep 24 · regulatory · trade · couplers
Commerce's final determinations set dumping margins of 71.01% for India's Bhilai Engineering and Jupiter Wagons (plus a 75% countervailing subsidy rate for each), 73.74% for the Czech Republic's CKD Kutna Hora, and an India "all others" rate of 5.24% dumping plus 9.71% subsidy; Texmaco Rail drew a 15.79% dumping margin. The scope covers couplers, knuckles, coupler bodies, locks, lock lift assemblies, knuckle pins, knuckle throwers and rotors. Why it matters: the ITC's concurrent injury investigation decides whether formal duty orders follow, which would materially raise the US landed cost of coupler imports from these countries.
Greenbrier books $600 million in new railcar orders for fiscal Q4
Greenbrier (press release) · Sep 22 · competitors · orders
Greenbrier reported 3,400 new railcar orders worth roughly $600 million in its fiscal fourth quarter ended August 31, spanning multiple car types and end markets, including a 780-car order from Saudi Arabia's state-owned SAR railway for phosphoric-acid and molten-sulfur tank cars plus intermodal units. Why it matters: roughly 200,000 US railcars are approaching retirement against 2026 industry output of only about 25,000 cars, and builder order books like this one are a leading indicator for undercarriage component demand as production ramps toward a projected 30,000-35,000 cars in 2027.
NTSB calls for better worker protection during shoving movements
Progressive Railroading, citing NTSB · Sep 22 · regulatory · safety
The NTSB urged the FRA and Class I railroads to prohibit workers from riding rail equipment to provide point protection during shoving movements, after investigating 16 such accidents since October 2020 that killed 14 workers and seriously injured 2 more, with two more investigations opened since August 2026. It wants documented hazard assessments before work begins and recommends ground-based alternatives — vehicles, cameras and other technology. Why it matters: this is a recommendation, not a binding rule, but it signals where FRA enforcement attention is likely headed next.

CN ships first unit train from expanded Total Grain Marketing facility
Progressive Railroading, citing CN · Sep 16 · customers
CN shipped the first unit train from Total Grain Marketing's expanded Illinois facility, which increased loop track capacity from 25 to 115 cars and storage to 3.1 million bushels, with unit trains now loadable in about eight hours. Why it matters: expanded shipper-side loop-track capacity like this supports higher unit-train utilization and is a modest positive signal for covered-hopper demand in CN's grain network.
