Kentucky awards grants for 6 rail projects
Progressive Railroading, citing the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet · Nov 13 · trade press · short lines · state funding
The fourth round of Kentucky's Short Line Infrastructure Preservation program awards $7.3 million to six projects, led by the Paducah & Louisville Railway with $2 million for continuous-welded rail in Marshall County, $2 million to rehabilitate its Paducah car shop and $1.9 million for Paducah Yard ties. The Louisville & Indiana Railroad ($339,953 for an industrial lead) and RJ Corman Railroad Group (two track projects totaling about $986,000) received the rest. Why it matters: state grants are paying for short-line track and shop work, including a car-repair facility, though the sums are small next to Class I capital budgets.

Martinus Group to build Port of Moses Lake rail link
Railway Gazette International · Nov 10 · trade press · short lines · new rail-served industry
The Port of Moses Lake in Washington State has appointed Martinus Rail as principal contractor, under a $31.75 million contract, for the first phase of the Northern Columbia Basin Railroad Project: about 7.6 km of new track and 4.3 km of upgraded track linking the port's industrial area to the Columbia Basin Railroad, which meets BNSF at Connell. The work is backed by $33 million from Washington State and the Federal Railroad Administration and is planned for completion in spring 2028. Why it matters: new rail-served industrial sites can add carload origins for short lines and their Class I partners over time, though no traffic estimates were given.
Shipper Industries & Demand 1 story
Rising Q3 transportation costs squeeze manufacturers, reward railroads and reshape trade
FreightWaves · Nov 10 · trade press · fuel and freight costs · company earnings calls
Drawing on third-quarter earnings calls, FreightWaves reports that Ford estimated a year-over-year headwind of about $2 billion from commodities, memory and logistics costs, while XPO said its fuel, operating expense and supplies costs rose 29%, or $66 million, mainly on higher fuel prices. Union Pacific said fuel prices reduced its third-quarter operating ratio by 140 basis points, but CEO Jim Vena argued that higher fuel favors rail because railroads move freight for much less per ton than trucks. Why it matters: sustained high diesel prices could widen rail's cost edge over trucking for some shippers, though these are company comments, not measured mode-shift data.
