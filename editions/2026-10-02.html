President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Norfolk Southern had two derailments in central Alabama on Thursday. At Springville, a train struck a tractor-trailer at a grade crossing and derailed two locomotives and 34 cars, prompting a brief evacuation; earlier, a trash train derailed at Maplesville. Officials reported no hazardous-material releases and no serious injuries, and both remain under investigation. Derailments draw attention to crossing safety and car condition, but the cause of either has not been determined.
  2. The rail safety debate in Washington is active again. A coalition led by the Competitive Enterprise Institute urged Congress to keep the Railway Safety Act out of surface transportation reauthorization, arguing it would raise shipping costs without improving safety. Separately, PHMSA announced $128.2 million in hazmat safety grants across six programs, including awards to the Short Line Safety Institute. Whether the Act is folded into reauthorization will shape any new equipment or inspection mandates for the industry.
  3. Small rail-access projects and European signaling contracts continue. Massachusetts awarded $680,000 to rebuild a quarry siding expected to add about 1,700 rail cars a year. In Europe, Hitachi Rail and Siemens Mobility won a 30-year, €2.6 billion contract from ÖBB to modernize about 300 interlockings, and InnoTrans closed with 3,190 exhibitors. None of these is a freight-car order, but they show continued public and private spending on rail.

Two Norfolk Southern trains derail in central Alabama in one day
Birmingham Free Press; WVTM 13 · Oct 1–2 · safety · customers
At Springville, a northbound train struck a tractor-trailer at a grade crossing, derailing two locomotives and 34 cars; a half-mile precautionary evacuation was later canceled, and officials reported no hazardous materials in the derailed cars and no serious injuries. Earlier the same day, a trash train derailed at Maplesville, with local reports of 40 to 50 cars involved, no hazardous materials aboard and cleanup expected to take two to three days or longer. Why it matters: both are under investigation with no link reported, so no conclusion about equipment or track should be drawn yet.

Regulatory & Policy 2 stories
Free-market coalition writes Congress opposing the Railway Safety Act
Progressive Railroading · Oct 1 · legislation · advocacy letter
A coalition of free-market and conservative groups led by the Competitive Enterprise Institute urged lawmakers not to include the Act in surface transportation reauthorization, arguing it would raise shipping costs without improving safety and that technology mandates should not be frozen into statute. The letter notes the 2023 East Palestine train had a three-person crew, countering the case for a two-person crew mandate. Why it matters: the Act's fate in reauthorization remains open, and any version that passes could carry new equipment, inspection or detector requirements.
PHMSA announces $128.2 million in hazmat safety grants
Progressive Railroading, citing PHMSA · Oct 1 · hazmat · grants
The awards span six programs; the Short Line Safety Institute received $200,000 for hazmat instructor training and $250,000 for local emergency-response training, and Maryland, Montana, Ohio and Vermont agencies received state inspection funds. Why it matters: the grants fund training and shipper inspections rather than equipment, so they are not a direct demand signal for tank-car components.

Hitachi Rail and Siemens Mobility win ÖBB's €2.6 billion interlocking modernization
Progressive Railroading supplier roundup · Oct 1 · global · Europe · signaling
Austria's ÖBB selected the two companies, splitting the contract equally, to modernize about 300 digital interlocking units over 30 years, with a pilot starting in the fourth quarter of 2030. Why it matters: this is signaling, not rolling stock, but it shows the scale of long-dated European rail infrastructure awards competing for the same capital budgets.
InnoTrans 2026 closes with record international reach
Progressive Railroading, citing organizers · Oct 2 · global · trade show
Organizers report 175,591 visitors from 128 countries and 3,190 exhibitors from 59 countries, including 753 first-time exhibitors, at the September 22–25 Berlin show; the next edition is in 2028. Why it matters: the number of first-time exhibitors suggests more suppliers are entering the global rail market, though attendance is not a measure of orders.
