President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. There are 16 days left to take a formal role in the UP–NS case. The Board's schedule sets September 30 for notices of intent to participate and November 18 for comments, with final briefs in May 2027. That deadline is already an extension, granted after the National League of Cities sought more time on behalf of an estimated 12,000 rail-served communities. A notice makes a filer a party of record entitled to service of filings rather than simply a commenter, so the question in front of interested suppliers is whether to take a formal role — public comment stays open either way.
  2. Friday's WASDE cuts the corn crop, and the rail read is genuinely mixed. USDA cut corn production 213 million bushels to 15.800 billion and lifted the farm price 30 cents to $4.80, while raising soybean — not corn — exports 25 million bushels to 1.685 billion. A smaller corn crop lowers the total tonnage available to move, even as firmer prices and the soybean revision support export-direction loadings, so the net effect on covered-hopper volumes is ambiguous. Either way this is a forecast rather than booked tonnage, and it would bear on car-miles and repair incidence well before it bears on new build.
  3. Hamilton would be at day 34, and the search record around it is a trap. The stoppage by roughly 1,200 USW Local 7135 members began August 12, and no source we could read reports a settlement, a revised offer or a scheduled bargaining date — silence that does not itself confirm the plant is still down. A USW page announcing that the strike has ended with significant gains sits high in search results, but it is dated August 8, 2023 and describes the previous dispute, which is worth naming before someone briefs off it. Our own call-offs and shipment timing into Hamilton remain the best internal read on near-term demand, though they are not proof of what the plant is doing.

Sixteen days remain to file notices of intent to participate in the UP–NS merger review
Surface Transportation Board news page, with the extension reported by FreightWaves · schedule adopted Aug 18; deadline Sep 30 · merger · regulatory
The Board's procedural schedule, adopted August 18 when it removed the proceeding from abeyance, sets September 30 as the deadline for notices of intent to participate and November 18 for comments, with final briefs due in May 2027; adopting a schedule is a procedural step and not a decision on the merits. The September 30 date is itself an extension, granted after the National League of Cities said the estimated 12,000 local governments in rail-served communities needed more time to decide whether to take part. Why it matters: a timely notice makes a filer a party of record entitled to service of filings in the largest rail combination in decades, while public comment remains open to anyone — so the decision facing interested suppliers is whether to take a formal role, not whether to be heard at all.
September WASDE cuts the corn crop to 15.8 billion bushels and raises soybean exports
USDA WASDE-675 data as reported by AgOptimus — the USDA release itself was not reachable to our reader · Sep 11 · agriculture · demand
USDA's September 11 report cut corn production 213 million bushels to 15.800 billion on a yield of 178.5 bushels per acre, down from 180.7 in August, with ending stocks at 1.567 billion and the season-average farm price up 30 cents to $4.80. Soybean production rose to 4.535 billion bushels, soybean exports were raised 25 million to 1.685 billion and ending stocks stand at 310 million, while wheat's farm price rose 20 cents to $6.40 — all figures for the 2026/27 marketing year in dollars per bushel. Why it matters: the signals pull in opposite directions — a 213-million-bushel smaller corn crop lowers the tonnage available to move, while firmer prices and the soybean export revision support export-direction loadings — so the net covered-hopper effect is ambiguous, and a forecast is not booked tonnage in any case.

Union Pacific's weekly intermodal fuel surcharge steps up to 56.0% for the week beginning today
Union Pacific customer announcements, a company posting · announced Sep 9, effective Sep 14–20 · customers · intermodal · cost
Union Pacific set its domestic weekly intermodal fuel surcharge at 56.0% for September 14–20, announced September 9, against 52.5% for the week just ended. A separately published monthly surcharge for September, posted August 25, was 55.5% under its own schedule, so the two figures are not directly comparable. Why it matters: the surcharge tracks diesel price rather than rail demand, so a 3.5-point weekly step is a cost signal only — and because truck freight carries its own fuel surcharges on different indices and lags, any modal shift turns on relative all-in pricing rather than this figure alone.

French investigators treat sabotage as the main line of inquiry after a Normandy derailment injures 44
France 24 · Sep 12, on a Sep 11 derailment · global · safety and security
A regional train carrying 186 passengers derailed at about 7.45pm on Friday, September 11 near Cléon, between Rouen and Caen, injuring 44 people including an 18-year-old woman evacuated in critical condition. Investigators found a fragment of rail on the track and are treating a deliberate act as the main line of inquiry, according to Rouen prosecutor Sébastien Gallois and a police source cited by AFP; the driver tested negative for drugs and alcohol, and SNCF pledged "full cooperation in complete transparency". Why it matters: this is a passenger service on French infrastructure with no read-across to North American freight equipment, and we carry it as a security watch item only — no authority or operator has announced a response that would bear on our markets.
