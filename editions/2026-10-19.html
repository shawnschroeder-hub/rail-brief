New Long Beach record as volume nears 1M containers
FreightWaves · Oct 16 · Port of Long Beach · monthly cargo record
The Port of Long Beach handled 997,177 TEUs in September, up 25% from a year earlier and above its prior monthly record of 987,191 set in October 2024, with loaded imports up 31.3% to 509,390 TEUs and Total Terminals International setting a port monthly rail record of 59,410 moves. Port CEO Noel Hacegaba said shippers relied more on Long Beach amid high diesel costs, Panama Canal access restrictions and the prospect of a new U.S.-China trade agreement, and that the record came with no congestion. Why it matters: the volume is container traffic that feeds railroad intermodal service rather than carload freight, so it speaks to intermodal demand more than to railcar-component demand.

R8.9bn enhancement package to boost capacity on South Africa's heavy haul rail corridor
Railway Gazette International · Oct 19 · Transnet · infrastructure programme
Transnet Rail Infrastructure Manager is running an R8.9 billion modernisation programme to 2030 on the 861 km Sishen–Saldanha iron-ore and manganese line, which carried 52.8 million tonnes of iron ore in 2025–26 against a licensed capacity of 60 million tonnes a year, with approval sought to raise that to 67 million. Locomotive maintenance is funded separately, with R1.47 billion allocated this year for overhauls of Class 15E electric and Class 43 diesel units. Why it matters: the spending is on infrastructure and locomotives rather than wagons, so it shows public money flowing to a major heavy-haul ore corridor without itself signalling wagon orders.
Jordan breaks ground on UAE-backed $2.5bn freight railway
RailFreight.com · Oct 19 · Jordan · new freight line (groundbreaking Oct 5)
The 403 km Aqaba–Al-Shidiyeh–Maan line, with about 55 bridges and six tunnels, is meant to carry about 16 million tonnes of mineral exports a year, mainly phosphate and potash, plus containers; the Jordan-UAE Railway Company is owned by Abu Dhabi's L'IMAD Holding and four Jordanian shareholders, including Jordan Phosphate Mines Company and Arab Potash Company. The article gives no opening date and no rolling-stock details. Why it matters: a new bulk-freight railway could eventually need wagons, but nothing disclosed indicates when or whether that procurement would happen.
