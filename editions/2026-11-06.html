President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The Vena recording is now public, and the merger opposition is organizing customers. Union Pacific released the recording of CEO Jim Vena's March remarks on November 5 after the STB denied its appeal and ordered it made public, and BNSF has written to its customers that the merger would put about half of US rail shipments under one railroad. Merger comments are due November 18, and the applicants must answer CPKC's request to dismiss the application by November 19. None of this is a Board ruling on the merger itself.
  2. US traffic is still growing, and Canadian grain is at record levels. AAR reported US traffic up 3.5% for the week ending October 31, led by metals, chemicals and autos, and CN and CPKC each reported record October Canadian grain volumes on different measures. US grain carloads, by contrast, fell 2,092 from a year earlier. Weekly gains support car utilization but do not by themselves signal new-car orders.
  3. A port labor deadline tonight and a recent weather outage are the near-term service risks. The contract extension for about 250 watchmen at Los Angeles and Long Beach runs to midnight Pacific tonight, and the affected terminals handle about 85% of Long Beach cargo. BNSF's Southern Transcon was shut for about 60 hours in late October, which dented its weekly intermodal volume. Competitor news is quiet: Wabtec refinanced maturing notes, FreightCar America reports on November 9, and the National Steel Car strike that began August 12 has not been reported settled.

EXCLUSIVE: 'Humor is good,' Union Pacific CEO says of video rate threat
FreightWaves · Nov 5 · trade press · UP–NS merger · STB
Union Pacific released the recording of CEO Jim Vena's March remarks to the Southwest Association of Rail Shippers on November 5, a day after the STB denied its appeal and ordered it made public. FreightWaves reports that Vena said on the video he would likely raise rates by three or four percent for shippers who did not want a seamless railroad, and that he told the outlet he was not making a threat and meant it as humor. Why it matters: CPKC argues the remarks chilled shipper participation in the merger case, so the recording now sits in a contested record, though the Board has not ruled on the merger itself.
BNSF: Merger Would Put Half of U.S. Rail Shipments Under One Railroad
American-Rails.com, citing a BNSF customer letter · Nov 6 · trade press · UP–NS merger · BNSF
In a November 3 letter to customers, BNSF executive vice president and chief marketing officer Tom Williams said the proposed Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger would put about half of US rail shipments under one railroad and eliminate two of today's four transcontinental options. He said the STB wants to hear from individual customers and pointed shippers to a BNSF web page for submitting comments. Why it matters: this is a competing railroad's characterization, not a Board finding, but it shows opponents mobilizing customers ahead of the November 18 comment date.
Port strike delayed again at Los Angeles, Long Beach ports
FreightWaves · Nov 5 · trade press · ports · labor
The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26, which represents about 250 port watchmen, extended their contract again, to midnight Pacific tonight, Friday, November 6, with talks resuming Friday alongside federal mediators. The terminals that would be affected handle about 85% of Long Beach cargo and 33% at Los Angeles, and the employers warned that other ILWU members might honor picket lines. Why it matters: a walkout would most directly hit the import containers that feed western intermodal trains, and the deadline falls tonight.

8 inches of rain shut down busiest US rail line for 60 hours
FreightWaves · Nov 4 · trade press · Class I service · BNSF
Eight inches of rain over three days at Laguna, New Mexico, closed BNSF's Southern Transcon from Saturday, October 24, for about 60 hours, after a second wave of storms extended a closure that would otherwise have lasted roughly 24 to 30 hours. BNSF's weekly intermodal volume fell 0.4% after a 14% gain the prior week, while Union Pacific, unaffected by the flooding, posted a 17% year-over-year intermodal gain. Why it matters: the outage shows how quickly weather on a major corridor can interrupt car and container flows, though the effect reported so far is a one-week volume dip.
Conrail tabs Wright as president and chief operating officer
FreightWaves · Nov 5 · trade press · leadership change · Conrail
Conrail named John Wright president and chief operating officer, succeeding Brian Gorton, who retired. Wright most recently was general manager of the Indiana Harbor Belt Railroad, and FreightWaves did not give an effective date. Why it matters: it is a leadership change at a railroad customer, with no stated change to operations or purchasing.

CN and CPKC Report October Grain Records
American-Rails.com, citing CN and CPKC releases · Nov 6 · trade press · agriculture · Canada
CPKC said it moved 3.26 million metric tonnes (33,822 carloads) of Canadian grain and grain products in October and 2.65 million metric tonnes (27,739 carloads) in the US, while CN reported a record 3.54 million metric tonnes of Canadian grain and processed grain, up from 3.51 million a year earlier. The two railroads' figures use different measures and are not a national total. Why it matters: the volumes support grain-car utilization in Canada, though AAR data for the week ending October 31 show US grain carloads down 2,092.

AAR Reports Weekly Rail Traffic for the Week Ending October 31, 2026
Association of American Railroads · Nov 4 · association data · rail traffic
US railroads moved 233,816 carloads, up 3.0% from a year earlier, and 280,264 intermodal units, up 3.9%, with seven of ten carload groups rising, led by metallic ores and metals (up 3,883), chemicals (up 2,542) and motor vehicles and parts (up 1,351). Year to date, US carloads are up 2.7% and intermodal 4.7%. Why it matters: steady carload growth supports car utilization, but weekly gains do not by themselves signal new-car orders.
Regulatory & Policy 2 stories
Ground Rules Set for Responsive Applications
American-Rails.com, citing STB Decision 46 · Nov 5 · trade press · STB decision served
In Decision 46, served November 4 in the UP–NS docket, the STB let CPKC file consolidated corporate information for its US rail affiliates and let CN cover several sub-dockets in fewer responsive applications, but denied a request to label four CPKC transactions minor in advance. Those four are trackage rights on the Polo Line and in the Kansas City Terminal, the Techny line and the Lufkin Subdivision, plus reconveyance of the Wylie Intermodal Terminal to Norfolk Southern. Why it matters: the conditions rival railroads seek would shape traffic flows around a combined network, and these four proposals are not pre-cleared as minor.
Union Pacific: More Time to Comment on an Inspection Waiver
American-Rails.com, citing an FRA notice · Nov 5 · trade press · comment deadline
FRA extended to December 2 the comment period on Union Pacific's petition (docket FRA-2026-1420) for a waiver of certain provisions of 49 CFR part 229, the federal locomotive safety standards, on inspections by qualified mechanical inspectors, and gave a CSX petition (FRA-2026-1684) the same 30-day extension. The notice, published in the Federal Register on November 2, followed a request from the AFL-CIO's Transportation Trades Department, and FRA has not ruled on either petition. Why it matters: the petitions cover locomotives rather than freight cars, but the outcome would show how FRA treats changes to railroad inspection practice.

Stella-Jones shares Q3 results, performance optimization plan
Progressive Railroading · Nov 4 · trade press · earnings · tie supplier
Stella-Jones reported third-quarter sales of C$979 million versus C$958 million a year earlier, net income of C$51 million versus C$88 million and adjusted EPS of C$1.39 versus C$1.59, after C$32 million of impairment and restructuring costs tied mainly to network optimization. Why it matters: Stella-Jones is a tie supplier rather than a car-component competitor, so its commentary is a read on Class I track maintenance, not on railcar demand.
Global Rail 2 stories
Combined transport in Europe back in decline in Q3
RailFreight.com, citing UIRR · Nov 5 · trade press · Europe · intermodal
Combined road-rail transport volumes in Europe fell 1.23% year on year in the third quarter, according to the trade association UIRR, after a 0.29% gain in the second quarter. UIRR cited German corridors running at minimum capacity, very low Rhine water levels, rising track access charges and weak growth, and said its members expect a negative business climate over the next 12 months. Why it matters: European intermodal weakness contrasts with North American gains, but it is at most a soft signal for European wagon and component demand.
Freight volume collapses by 36%: Ukrainian Railways set for more severe loss in 2026
RailFreight.com, citing Forbes Ukraine · Nov 5 · trade press · Ukraine
Ukraine's Deputy Minister of Infrastructure Volodymyr Shemayev told Forbes Ukraine that Ukrainian Railways' freight volumes have fallen about 36% since the end of July, and Forbes Ukraine reported 400 locomotives lost to Russian drone attacks this year. RailFreight.com reports the railway could end 2026 with a net loss of up to 18 billion hryvnia (about €355 million), against a summer forecast of 10 to 12 billion. Why it matters: the figures point to heavy financial strain at a major eastern European freight railway.
