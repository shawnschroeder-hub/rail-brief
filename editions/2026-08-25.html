President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Two Canadian labour files moved in opposite directions. CPKC and the IBEW council representing its signals and communications employees have ended a stoppage that began May 31 by referring unresolved bargaining issues to binding arbitration, after roughly three months during which the railway kept operating. At National Steel Car in Hamilton, by contrast, the strike is in its fourteenth day and the most recent account our reader could open is a week old. The read-across differs by case: the CPKC outcome removes a labour-relations overhang at a customer with no disclosed effect on traffic, earnings or capital plans, while the National Steel Car stoppage is likely curtailing or halting normal production and, with it, delaying or rescheduling some supplier releases into that plant.
  2. The Belt Railway file is now generating a public reporting record. Following the Board's August 3 letter, BRC has filed twice — on August 11 and again on August 19, the second filing covering three weeks and moving the cadence to Wednesdays. That is a change from yesterday's position, when no public update could be located. Two caveats matter for how much weight to put on it: the filings are posted as spreadsheets we could not open, so the direction of dwell and inventory since early August is not something this brief can yet report; and more broadly, this edition carries little direct evidence about railcar or component demand — the AAR was unreachable today and its next weekly traffic report is due Wednesday.
  3. The near-term regulatory calendar tightens this week and next. Refiled workpapers in the UP–NS proceeding are due Friday, August 28, and notices of intent to participate on September 4 — the point at which the prospective party-of-record roster becomes visible, though not necessarily each party's position, ahead of the November 18 comment deadline. Separately, a PHMSA final rule removing rail reporting requirements the agency describes as obsolete takes effect September 3. Neither is a decision on anything; both are dates worth having on the calendar rather than discovering afterwards.

CPKC and IBEW end three-month strike, referring unresolved issues to binding arbitration
Progressive Railroading · Aug 24 · labour · Class I customer
The dispute between CPKC and the IBEW Canadian Signals and Communications System Council No. 11 began May 31 and has been ended by referring the outstanding bargaining issues to binding arbitration rather than reaching a negotiated settlement; no terms were disclosed, and the status of the union's separate complaints was not stated. CPKC said its proposals were consistent with agreements in place with its other Canadian unions, and maintained operations throughout. Senior general chairman Jason Sommer attributed the length of the dispute to "the absence of timely, real-time enforcement and the prolonged nature of the Board proceedings," referring to 19 complaints the union filed with the Canada Industrial Relations Board over the use of replacement workers. Why it matters: the immediate effect is the removal of a labour-relations overhang at a customer — CPKC kept operating throughout, and nothing reported indicates a material traffic, earnings or capital-plan effect. Because the outcome now rests with an arbitrator, no wage terms or precedent are yet known; the award, when it lands, could still carry weight at other Canadian rail bargaining tables.
Belt Railway of Chicago has now filed twice on Clearing Yard; reporting moves to Wednesdays
Surface Transportation Board — BRC data reporting page · filings dated Aug 11 and Aug 19 · standing watch item
BRC submitted data on August 11 covering July 31 to August 6, then filed again on August 19 covering three weeks — July 27 to August 2, August 3 to August 9, and August 10 to August 16 — with the reporting date shifted to Wednesdays after discussion with Board staff. The underlying figures are posted as downloadable spreadsheets, which our reader could not open, so the trend since the Board's August 3 letter cannot be characterised here. That letter had cited average weekly railcar inventory up 57% year over year, from 3,212 cars to 5,048, and average dwell up 72%, from about 18 hours to 31. Why it matters: the substantive question — whether Clearing Yard is stabilising — remains open, but Board-requested weekly reporting on a fixed cadence is itself meaningful, since it creates a public record and gives the Board and stakeholders recurring data with which to judge whether further action is warranted. Sustained dwell reduces car turns and effective fleet capacity in the Chicago gateway; it does not automatically create new-car demand, since railroads and shippers may first draw on stored cars, rebalance fleets or reduce loadings, and whether it instead suppresses traffic or strands equipment elsewhere is not determinable from what has been published.

National Steel Car strike enters its fourteenth day; last readable account is a week old
CHCH News, Hamilton · Aug 18 · labour · status carried forward
United Steelworkers Local 7135 members have been out since August 12. In the most recent account our reader could open, dated August 18, an Ontario Ministry of Labour mediator had been assigned and was described as remaining available, but the company had not responded to the union's overtures to resume talks after walking out the previous week; no later report was found in the sources checked today. The vote that triggered the strike drew about 91% turnout and rejected the offer by nearly 80%, with union president Frank Crowder citing wages that in his account are "not even meeting inflation" alongside a disputed incentive scheme the union says pushes workers to compromise safety to hit rising output thresholds. Why it matters: a stoppage of this length is likely curtailing or halting normal car production and component installation at the plant, though the reporting does not confirm a total shutdown. The practical effect on a supplier is timing and working capital rather than lost demand: releases may be delayed, rescheduled or redirected depending on the builder's inventory and purchasing instructions, backlog and underlying orders are a separate question the reporting does not address, and output can in principle be recovered once work resumes. The absence of news after August 18 should be read as a gap in what is publicly readable, not as evidence that nothing has moved.

Palmetto Railways names Tarek Ravenel president and chief executive, effective September 8
Progressive Railroading · Aug 24 · leadership · short line / terminal switching
Palmetto Railways, a division of the South Carolina Department of Commerce, operates as a terminal switching and short line railroad serving the Port of Charleston and industries across five Lowcountry counties, with direct interchange to CSX and Norfolk Southern. Ravenel most recently led freight-rail market initiatives at HDR and previously spent nearly a decade at Palmetto in senior roles covering capital improvement programmes, design, environmental review and construction. He succeeds Patrick McCrory, who is leaving to become chief operating officer of South Carolina Ports. Why it matters: a single short-line appointment carries no read-through to component demand on its own. It is worth noting only for the relationship it creates — an incoming executive whose background is in capital projects, and an outgoing one moving to the port authority next door. Whether that produces infrastructure work at this gateway is a matter to monitor, not something the appointment establishes; no project, budget or timeline was announced.

RSI board chair sets out the case for incoming president Thomas Jensen
Railway Supply Institute · Aug 24 · association leadership
Board of directors chair Greg Dalpe has published a message on Jensen's appointment as RSI president, which the institute announced on July 22 and framed as leading its legislative, regulatory and advocacy work for the railway supply industry. The message is positioning rather than policy: no change to RSI's agenda, dues structure or committee arrangements is announced in it. Why it matters: RSI is the trade body through which supply-side positions reach the Board, FRA and Congress, so a change of president can affect the institute's priorities and how well it executes on them. Nothing in the announcement commits RSI to a position or an intervention in any particular proceeding, so this is a signal about advocacy capacity rather than a development in any docket.
New Georgia Coastal Trade PAC formed to back port, rail and logistics interests
Progressive Railroading · Aug 24 · political advocacy · ports and intermodal
The Georgia Coastal Trade PAC describes itself as non-partisan and says it will support candidates and officials who recognise the role of the state's ports, transportation infrastructure, workforce and business climate. Board chair Matt Campbell framed the rationale plainly: "if our industry is not engaged, someone else will shape the conversation." No fundraising target, membership list or endorsement slate was disclosed. Why it matters: Georgia's ports anchor a significant Southeast intermodal gateway, and organised political engagement — with spending still prospective rather than demonstrated — is a marker of how contested state-level infrastructure and trade policy has become. The practical effect on freight volumes or equipment demand is indirect and slow, and nothing in the announcement quantifies it.
Regulatory & Policy 2 stories
UP–NS: filtered workpapers must be refiled by Friday, notices of intent to participate due September 4
Surface Transportation Board — Decision No. 26-21 · Aug 18 · procedural schedule
The Board's August 18 decision removed the proceeding from abeyance and set the schedule: applicants must refile within ten days any workpapers to which screening or filtering criteria were applied, without those criteria — a deadline that falls on August 28. Parties seeking party-of-record status must file notices of intent by September 4; comments, protests and requests for conditions are due November 18, with responses due February 16, 2027 and a public hearing to be scheduled in a later decision. The Board stated that "the record is now sufficient to resume the procedural schedule" and that this "determination is not a finding on the merits of the revised application." Why it matters: September 4 is the date on which the prospective party-of-record roster becomes visible — a notice of intent signals participation and the associated procedural rights, not a position, and the roster will include supporters, agencies and parties seeking conditions as well as opponents. For planning purposes nothing changes: a schedule is not an approval, no decision is close, and the applicants' 2027 completion target is theirs rather than the Board's.
PHMSA final rule removing rail hazmat reporting requirements takes effect September 3
Federal Register — PHMSA final rule 2026-15815 · published Aug 4 · effective Sep 3
The rule, titled "Hazardous Materials: Removing Burdensome Rail Reporting Requirements," eliminates or replaces rail transportation requirements in the hazardous materials regulations that the agency describes as obsolete, overly burdensome, or in conflict with other requirements. It was published on August 4 and becomes effective September 3; the document is a final rule, not a proposal, so no additional pre-finalisation notice-and-comment round is scheduled. Why it matters: the change is administrative and sits with shippers and carriers of hazardous materials rather than with component manufacturers — there is no design, qualification or interchange requirement in it. It is carried because the effective date falls next week; on its own it is a discrete administrative simplification rather than evidence of a broader shift in rail hazmat regulation.
Global Rail 1 story
Deutsche Bahn puts DB Cargo UK up for sale as it refocuses on Central Europe
Sky News, via Yahoo Finance UK · Aug 17 · divestment · European rail freight
DB Cargo UK, which employs about 2,200 people hauling freight, infrastructure and charter traffic across Britain and through the Channel Tunnel, has appointed legal and financial advisers and is running a structured sale with Interpath, with talks already under way with prospective bidders. The company said it is "seeking a new owner who can provide access to additional capital, broader market opportunities," and the parent is restructuring after a European Commission state aid ruling; the precise financial position of the UK business was not disclosed. Why it matters: there is no direct North American read-through — this is a UK operating business, not a manufacturing asset, and European freight wagons run to different standards from AAR interchange equipment. It matters as a market signal: a state-owned incumbent retrenching to its home region could contribute to consolidation in European rail freight if another operator buys the business, though the buyer may equally be financial or independent and no transaction has occurred. Any effect on component procurement depends on which assets actually transfer — wagons may be leased, separately owned or outside the perimeter — and the perimeter has not been disclosed.
