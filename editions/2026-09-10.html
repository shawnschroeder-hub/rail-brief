President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. CPKC has moved a border bottleneck inland, and the beneficiary is cross-border agriculture. Working with Mexico's food-safety agency SENASICA, the railway has opened an inspection point at its Puerta México terminal in Toluca so that eligible agri-food and animal-based shipments clear final inspection there rather than at Nuevo Laredo or another border facility. Nearly all of the traffic originates in the US Midwest and Canada and moves on CPKC's Mexico Midwest Express. CPKC has published no transit-time or throughput figures, so the operational gain is the railway's assertion rather than a measured result, and the dwell could move to Toluca rather than disappear — but the direction of travel favours a lane running through our Mexican footprint.
  2. Six Class I railroads say they are ready for a very large harvest. BNSF, Union Pacific, CSX, Norfolk Southern, CN and CPKC told the Surface Transportation Board's National Grain Car Council — an advisory body, not the Board acting as regulator — that equipment and operating resources are in place for 2026, against USDA August projections of a 16-billion-bushel corn crop and a record 4.5-billion-bushel soybean crop. These are readiness claims made before the fact, not service results, and the same account separately flags a longer-term concern raised in the council discussion: ageing railcars on short lines, where replacement cost is becoming material. Those are different fleets, though — the harvest itself moves largely in covered hoppers owned by railroads, shippers and lessors — so higher mileage may lift wheel, bearing and brake repair exposure, while any new-build effect depends on ownership, condition and lease availability.
  3. Hamilton would be at day 30, and the record has now been quiet for ten days. The most recent account we could open, dated September 9, describes the National Steel Car stoppage as in its fourth week; it is a partisan labour publication writing about a rank-and-file committee, not about bargaining. No source we could read this week reports a settlement, a revised offer or a scheduled bargaining date, and mainstream coverage has not been refreshed since August 31. That silence is not evidence in either direction — continued shutdown is unconfirmed — which is why our own call-offs and shipment timing into Hamilton remain the most direct read we have on plant status.

CPKC opens an inland agri-food inspection point at Toluca, moving clearance off the border
Progressive Railroading · published today, Sep 10 · customers · cross-border
CPKC and Mexico's National Service of Agri-Food Health, Safety and Quality (SENASICA) have established an inspection facility at the railway's Puerta México terminal in Toluca, allowing eligible agri-food and animal-based shipments to complete final inspection and certification inland rather than on arrival at Nuevo Laredo or another border facility. Nearly all eligible shipments originate in the US Midwest and Canada and move on CPKC's Mexico Midwest Express service, and Coby Bullard, the railway's senior vice president of sales and marketing for merchandise, energy and business development, said the arrangement supports "truck-like speeds with end-to-end visibility and enhanced security." Why it matters: border inspection has been an operational constraint on eligible agri-food traffic, and moving it inland may cut border dwell and variability on a lane running through our Mexican footprint — though CPKC has published no transit-time, throughput or equipment-cycle figures, and the dwell could shift to Toluca rather than disappear.
Six Class I railroads tell the STB's grain council they are positioned for a record-scale harvest
RFD-TV, reporting the STB National Grain Car Council · Sep 9 · shipper demand · agriculture
BNSF, Union Pacific, CSX, Norfolk Southern, CN and CPKC reported having enough locomotives, grain cars and crews positioned for the 2026 harvest and said they expect strong transportation demand without major harvest-service problems, against USDA's August projections of a 16-billion-bushel corn crop and a record 4.5-billion-bushel soybean crop. The same account puts domestic soybean crush 18% above its five-year average and grain-mill carloads at a record 61,752 in July, and separately notes a longer-term concern raised over ageing railcars on short lines, where replacement expense is becoming significant. Why it matters: these are the carriers' own forward claims rather than measured service, and the harvest moves largely in covered hoppers owned by railroads, shippers and lessors rather than by short lines — so heavier mileage may lift wheel, bearing and brake repair exposure, while any new-build effect depends on fleet ownership, condition and lease availability.

Patriot Rail names Jason Bradt chief operating officer
Progressive Railroading · published today, Sep 10 · customers · leadership
Bradt succeeds Jerry Hall, who has held the role since October 2022 and retires at the end of 2026, and arrives with about three decades at Genesee & Wyoming and CSX spanning safety, transportation, mechanical, engineering, maintenance, customer service and commercial development. President and chief executive Brandy Christian said he "understands every aspect of the railroad business," and he will run day-to-day operations alongside service quality and growth work. Why it matters: short-line holding companies are a diffuse but real channel for wear parts and car repair, and an incoming operating chief whose career has included mechanical roles is a sensible aftermarket call — though his actual remit, Patriot's shop network and who owns the cars it handles decide whether it is worth making.

MxV Rail sets out its metallurgy lab's bench for wheel, weld and steel-component failure analysis
MxV Rail · Sep 1 · associations · testing and standards
The laboratory analyses rail welds, wheels and other steel components using a Hitachi SU 3900 scanning electron microscope, energy-dispersive and optical emission spectrometry, Rockwell, Vickers and Brinell hardness testing, ultrasonic, eddy-current, magnetic-particle and liquid-penetrant inspection, and a rolling contact fatigue simulator. Principal investigator Dr Ananyo Banerjee said the findings "empower those in the field to make immediate improvements"; the item is a description of capability rather than a research result. Why it matters: MxV is the AAR-affiliated laboratory whose failure analyses can inform interchange rules and component specifications, so its metallurgical bench is worth knowing when wheel or weld defect questions come under industry review.

Britain consults on how Great British Railways will allocate network access between passenger and freight
RailAdvent · Sep 9 · global · policy consultation
The consultation on Great British Railways' future Access and Use Policy would, for the first time in about three decades, bring passenger and freight access decisions under a single framework, with the stated aims of clearer capacity allocation and more predictable treatment of freight and open-access operators. Anit Chandarana, Network Rail's group director of system operator, framed the aim as continuing to make "fair, transparent and consistent decisions about access"; no closing date appeared in the coverage we could read. Why it matters: how a network rations capacity between passenger and freight determines whether freight paths — and the wagon fleets that fill them — can actually grow, which is the practical test of the UK Department for Transport's target, announced September 8, of at least 40% rail freight growth by 2040.
First commercial freight train reaches the Sizewell C site over a new 2.8 km rail link
RailBusinessDaily · published today, Sep 10, on a Sep 8 arrival · global · infrastructure
The train ran early on September 8 over the new Green Rail Route off the upgraded Sizewell branch in Suffolk, carrying more than 1,000 tonnes of aggregate from Tunstead Quarry in Derbyshire in box wagons behind a Class 66. Two overnight services operate now, rising to as many as four a day at peak, with about 100,000 tonnes planned by Christmas — around 5,000 lorry journeys avoided — under a commitment to move 60% of construction materials by rail and sea. Why it matters: major projects committing to rail are the clearest source of incremental European aggregate-wagon utilisation, though British wagon design and loading gauge differ from North America's and the read for us is directional only.
