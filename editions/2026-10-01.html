The Rail Data Brief
Edition 033 · Thursday, October 1, 2026 · 7 stories
President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. US rail volume is running ahead of last year, led by intermodal. AAR reports US traffic up 4.8% for the week ending September 26, with carloads up 3.0% and intermodal up 6.3%, and FreightWaves data shows loaded domestic intermodal at an annual high. Chemicals, metals and minerals led the carload gains, while coal and motor vehicles fell and Mexican carloads dropped 16.8%. Higher traffic adds car-miles and wear, though it does not by itself mean new railcar orders.
  2. Railroad labor is settling at CPKC, while the National Steel Car strike continues. CPKC reached a tentative five-year agreement with BLET covering about 250 train and engine employees on the southern DM&E, pending a member vote. At National Steel Car in Hamilton, the strike that began August 12 has not been reported settled. Stable railroad labor supports service, while a long stoppage at a car builder could affect delivery schedules for that builder's customers.
  3. Rail-served capacity is being added for shippers. OmniTRAX launched a transload platform aimed at shippers who now rely on long-haul trucks, with hubs planned in select markets on its short lines. Savannah's renovated Ocean Terminal, served by CSX and Norfolk Southern, began receiving ships on a project that will lift its capacity from 200,000 to 1.75 million TEUs by the end of 2028. These projects aim to bring more freight to rail over time, but neither is a railcar order on its own.

AAR: US rail traffic up 4.8% for the week ending September 26
AAR data, via American Journal of Transportation · Sep 30 · weekly traffic · North America
US railroads moved 235,787 carloads (up 3.0%) and 301,610 intermodal units (up 6.3%); chemicals rose 2,261 carloads to 33,843 and metallic ores and metals rose 1,988, while coal fell 1,236 to 58,280 and motor vehicles and parts fell 315. Year to date through 38 weeks, US carloads are up 2.7% and intermodal up 4.1%, while Mexican carloads fell 16.8% for the week and Canadian carloads rose 0.9%. Why it matters: broad carload gains outside coal point to firmer industrial demand, though one week is not a trend and the Mexican drop is worth watching.
CPKC reaches a tentative five-year agreement with BLET on the southern DM&E
CPKC release (PR Newswire), via StockTitan · Oct 1 · labor · customers
The tentative deal covers about 250 train and engine employees on the southern portion of the Dakota, Minnesota and Eastern property in Minnesota, Iowa, Illinois and Missouri, with increased wages and more flexible work rules, subject to union ratification. CEO Keith Creel called it the "latest" tentative agreement for CPKC's US railroaders. Why it matters: settled crew contracts reduce the risk of service disruption on a Midwest grain and industrial corridor.
Loaded domestic intermodal volume hits an annual high
FreightWaves (SONAR data) · Sep 30 · intermodal · demand
FreightWaves says the seven-day average of loaded domestic intermodal containers reached 21,697 on September 28, about 8% above a year earlier, with its contract-savings index showing intermodal roughly 30.9% cheaper than truck, down from more than 33% in mid-August. It projects about 4% more volume heading into Thanksgiving. Why it matters: stronger intermodal demand supports well-car and container-platform use, though FreightWaves' forecast is its own and not railroad guidance.

OmniTRAX launches its Connect transload platform
Progressive Railroading · Sep 30 · short lines · transload
OmniTRAX says "OmniTRAX Connect: Where Rail Meets Road" will help truck-dependent shippers try rail through transload partners, with hubs planned in select markets on its network; it did not give a number of hubs or markets. Phillip Hoskins was named vice president of transload. Why it matters: transload programs are one way short lines try to win new carload traffic from truck, though results depend on how many shippers convert.

Ships begin calling the Port of Savannah's renovated Ocean Terminal
Progressive Railroading · Sep 30 · intermodal · ports
The $1.6 billion project, more than 60% complete, opened a new 2,650-foot berth and will raise the terminal's annual capacity from 200,000 to 1.75 million TEUs when finished at the end of 2028; the terminal is served by CSX and Norfolk Southern. Why it matters: more East Coast container capacity could add international intermodal volume for both eastern railroads over the next few years.

Rail Cargo Group adds 70 double-deck car-carrier wagons, with 130 more planned
RailFreight.com · Oct 1 · global · Europe
The Austrian group is adding 70 Laaers wagons, 31 meters long and suited to electric vehicles, and plans to procure 130 more in 2027; it says it moves about one million finished vehicles a year across more than 20 European locations. Why it matters: European automotive rail fleets are still being renewed, even as North American motor-vehicle carloads slip.
Ukraine approves a roadmap to open its rail market to competition
RailFreight.com · Oct 1 · global · Europe
The government approved a gradual opening of freight and passenger markets as part of EU-linked reforms to Ukrzaliznytsia, but said the main 1,520-millimeter network will not open to competition before martial law ends. Why it matters: any opening is long-dated, but it would eventually create new private operators that need rolling stock.
