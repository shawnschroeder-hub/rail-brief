CHCH News / multiple outlets · strike began Aug 12; last union update Sep 16 · competitors · labor · standing watch item
The strike by roughly 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members at National Steel Car's Hamilton, Ontario plant reached its seventh week today; the union's most recent update, on September 16, reported no new negotiations scheduled and said the company had not returned with a revised offer. The dispute centers on wages that have lagged inflation and a piecework incentive program the union links to three worker deaths at the plant since 2020. FRA supplements shoving-movement safety advisory after two more grade-crossing casualties
Federal Register / FRA · published Sep 17 · regulatory · safety
The FRA supplemented Safety Advisory 2025-02 after two more accidents since its December 2025 issuance: a fuel-truck driver killed by a CPKC railcar during a shoving movement in Bensenville, Illinois on June 13, and a CSX conductor who suffered an amputation in a similar collision in College Park, Georgia on August 6. The supplement adds two recommendations — mandatory face-to-face briefings on point-protection and "track is clear" criteria, and an immediate review of training and examination programs for employees performing shoving movements. Why it matters: it is the second shoving-movement safety document from federal authorities this month, alongside NTSB's broader recommendation carried in yesterday's edition, sharpening near-term compliance and training expectations across every Class I and short line.

Alstom signs €22 million deal to maintain Saudi Arabia Railways' freight fleet
Alstom press release · Sep 22 · global rail · Saudi Arabia
Alstom signed a five-year, €22 million FlexCare Perform contract with Saudi Arabia Railways to maintain its North Freight fleet on the North-South corridor, which carries roughly 13.5 million tons of freight annually between mining sites and export points. The deal follows Greenbrier's $600 million railcar order that included SAR's first intermodal-unit purchase, announced the same week and carried in yesterday's edition. Why it matters: two separate freight-fleet investments from the same customer in one week point to continued Saudi rail capacity expansion that global suppliers, including Amsted Rail's international operations, will want to track for future sourcing opportunities.
