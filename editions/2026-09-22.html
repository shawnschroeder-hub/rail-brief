President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern announced that SMART-MD, the union representing railroad mechanical-department employees, has reached a jobs-for-life agreement covering the combination. The deal guarantees that every employee with a union job at the time of merger approval keeps one for life, extending labor backing for the deal to a majority of the two railroads' organized workforce. It is a distinct development from the Surface Transportation Board's September 18 denial of dismissal motions already covered in this brief, and it narrows one of the factors the Board weighs as the case proceeds toward the companies' targeted second-half-2027 close.
  2. Greenbrier booked 3,400 railcar orders worth roughly $600 million in its fiscal fourth quarter ended August 31, including 780 units for Saudi Railway Company. The SAR order marks the first time Greenbrier has sold that customer intermodal units, alongside tank cars for phosphoric acid and molten sulfur. A large, multi-region order book from a direct competitor is a useful demand signal even as recent weekly rail traffic data has been mixed.
  3. The NTSB urged the FRA and every Class I railroad to prohibit crew members from riding railcars during shoving movements, citing 16 accidents since October 2020 that killed 14 workers. FRA data cited by the Board show shoving involved in 70% of conductor deaths from January 2020 through mid-2025. The recommendation carries no binding force yet, but it names every Class I directly and signals where FRA attention on switching-operation safety could turn next.

Greenbrier books 3,400 railcar orders worth $600 million, including first SAR intermodal sale
PR Newswire, citing Greenbrier · Sep 22 · competitors · orders
Greenbrier received orders for 3,400 railcars valued at approximately $600 million in its fiscal fourth quarter ended August 31, including 780 units for Saudi Railway Company — the first time SAR has ordered intermodal units from the builder, alongside tank cars for phosphoric acid and molten sulfur. Why it matters: a large, multi-region order book from a direct competitor signals still-healthy railcar demand even as recent weekly traffic data has been mixed.
Rail mechanical union reaches jobs-for-life deal with UP-NS, deepening labor backing for the merger
Union Pacific / Norfolk Southern, via GlobeNewswire · Sep 22 · regulatory · UP-NS merger · standing watch item
SMART-MD, the union representing railroad mechanical-department employees, reached a jobs-for-life agreement with Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern guaranteeing that "every employee with a union job when the merger is approved will continue to have one for life," extending union support for the combination to a majority of the two railroads' organized workforce. Why it matters: broader union backing narrows one of the factors the STB weighs as the case proceeds toward the companies' targeted second-half-2027 close.
NTSB calls for ban on crew riding railcars during shoving moves after 14 deaths
NTSB, via Railway Supply · Sep 21-22 · regulatory · safety
The NTSB urged the FRA and every Class I railroad to prohibit crew members from riding equipment during shoving movements, citing 16 accidents since October 2020 that killed 14 workers and seriously injured two more, with FRA data showing shoving involved in 70% of conductor deaths from January 2020 through mid-2025. Why it matters: a safety recommendation naming every Class I directly, with no binding force yet but a strong signal of where FRA attention on switching operations could turn next.

Wabtec signs $700 million-plus locomotive services deal for Guinea's Simandou railway
Railway Gazette International · Sep 22 · competitors
Wabtec signed a long-term services agreement worth more than $700 million with La Compagnie du TransGuinéen to maintain its fleet of Evolution Series ES43AC locomotives on the 600-kilometer Simandou iron-ore railway, bringing Wabtec's total contracted value on the project past $1.2 billion combined with its 2024 locomotive order. Why it matters: it's Wabtec's largest services agreement in Africa, showing a direct competitor extending aftermarket-service revenue into major new-build rail corridors outside North America.
Knorr-Bremse wins Europe's first commercial Digital Automatic Coupler contract
Knorr-Bremse Newsroom · Sep 17 · competitors · couplers
Knorr-Bremse won Europe's first equipment contract for Digital Automatic Couplers, from Austria's ÖBB Rail Cargo Group under the PioDAC project, with commercial fleet deployment set to begin in 2028. Why it matters: DAC adoption in Europe is a multi-year competitive front in freight-car coupling technology that could eventually inform coupler and draft-gear standards Amsted Rail also competes in.

Railroads raise corn tariff rates ahead of fall harvest, per USDA
USDA Agricultural Marketing Service, Grain Transportation Report · Sep 17 · shipper industries · agriculture
USDA's weekly Grain Transportation Report detailed corn rail-tariff increases for the 2026/27 marketing year: Union Pacific and CPKC are each raising corn tariffs $225 per car, CN's corn rate rose $275 per car in September and rises a further $330 per car in October before falling $220 per car in December, and BNSF is raising Pacific Northwest export tariffs $200 per car beginning in October. Why it matters: higher per-car tariffs during peak harvest movement affect railroads' grain-car economics and, indirectly, shipper demand for hopper-car capacity and components.
US-Canada tariff fight escalates on steel, aluminum and autos
Department of Finance Canada · Sep 8 (retaliation) / Sep 15 (auto duty) · shipper industries · trade policy
Canada's retaliatory tariffs of 15-50% on more than 700 US goods took effect September 8, doubling duties on US steel and aluminum to 50% and covering $27.6 billion of US imports, while Canadian passenger vehicles have owed a 50% US duty since September 15, with Washington planning to raise Canadian auto, parts and steel tariffs to 50% again from January 1, 2027. Why it matters: sustained steel, aluminum and auto-related tariff escalation between the two largest North American rail markets could affect both new-railcar input costs and cross-border automotive carload volume.
Rail dwell at LA/Long Beach hits a 2026 high as import peak season extends
IndexBox, citing port data · Sep 2026 · shipper industries · intermodal
Rail container dwell time at the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach rose to 6.75 days in September, the year's highest, up from 6.34 days in July, after the ports moved more than 2.9 million TEUs from June through August — their busiest three-month stretch on record. Why it matters: extended dwell signals intermodal rail capacity is under strain during a stretched peak shipping season, a demand indicator for intermodal-car components even as broader weekly carloads have softened.

Regulatory & Policy 1 story
FRA drops special-approval requirement for freight cars over 50 years old
Federal Register / FRA · effective Sep 30 · regulatory · freight cars
The FRA finalized a rule, effective September 30, that repeals the requirement for railroads to obtain special FRA approval before placing or continuing in service a freight car more than 50 years old or fitted with certain older design components, replacing it with a notice-based process that keeps existing inspection, testing and recordkeeping standards. Why it matters: it eases an administrative step for railroads and lessors running older freight-car fleets, a segment where Amsted Rail's aftermarket wheel, bearing and undercarriage components see ongoing demand.

Knorr-Bremse Newsroom · Sep 15 · global rail · China
