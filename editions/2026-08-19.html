President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Merger — the Board has resumed the procedural timetable on UP–NS. The STB yesterday adopted a procedural schedule in Docket FD 36873 and removed the proceeding, including the environmental review, from abeyance; notices of intent to participate are due September 4, comments November 18, and responses to comments February 16, 2027, with a public hearing date to be set in a later decision. The Board stated plainly that the decision "does not constitute a ruling on the merits." Filing deadlines now exist for the early stages while the hearing and any decision remain undated, and the November window is the point at which the shipper coalition, the seven state attorneys general and the opposing railroads may put their evidence on the record.
  2. Supply base — one OEM customer's line is down, and another renewed its takeover defence. The National Steel Car strike in Hamilton is into its second week with a provincial mediator assigned but no talks reported; while an assembly line is stopped, component call-offs to that plant stop with it, which is the most direct effect on a supplier of undercarriage systems. Separately, FreightCar America's board adopted a limited-duration stockholder rights plan on August 5 with a 15% trigger, replacing one that expired the same day. That is continuity in a standing defence rather than evidence of a bidder, and no acquirer has been disclosed.
  3. Customers — this week's announcements are terminals and storage, and none carries an equipment commitment. CPKC anchors a new $80m Americold cold-storage hub at Port Saint John reported today, CMC Railroad is financing roughly 1,100 additional railcar storage spaces near Houston, and the Port of Vancouver USA extended its Subaru lease to 2040 on traffic that moves about 80% by rail. Read these as throughput and staging infrastructure; none of them establishes new-car demand, and added storage in particular can reflect either productive staging or idle cars. AAR's weekly traffic report, normally the Wednesday anchor, had not published by the time this edition closed.
Top Stories 3 stories
STB adopts a procedural schedule for the UP–NS merger and removes the proceeding from abeyance
Surface Transportation Board, Release No. 26-21 · Aug 18 · decision, Docket FD 36873
The Board set unfiltered workpapers due August 28, notices of intent to participate September 4, comments November 18, and responses to comments February 16, 2027, with the public hearing date to be established in a subsequent decision; it said the record "is now sufficient to resume the procedural schedule" and that the decision "does not constitute a ruling on the merits of the merger application or any issues raised by commenters or in motions filed since Applicants' July 27 submission." Why it matters: the early stages of the review now carry dates while the hearing and any decision do not, and the filing sequence alone runs past February 2027 — the applicants' mid-2027 completion target remains their own, not the Board's.
National Steel Car strike reaches its second week; mediator assigned, no talks reported
CHCH News, Hamilton · Aug 18 · labour
United Steelworkers Local 7135 said it had still not heard back from the company on the seventh day of the walkout, which began August 12 after a vote with 91% turnout rejected the offer by roughly 80%; local president Frank Crowder cited wages against inflation and the company's output-based incentive program, and an Ontario Ministry of Labour mediator has been assigned and "remains actively available to support both parties." Why it matters: National Steel Car is an OEM car builder that installs undercarriage components, so a stopped assembly line pauses component deliveries into that plant for as long as it lasts. Railcar work does not move quickly between builders — designs, tooling and supplier qualifications all constrain it — so the near-term effect is delayed deliveries and call-offs rather than reallocated orders.
FreightCar America's board adopts a limited-duration stockholder rights plan with a 15% trigger
FreightCar America via GlobeNewswire, read on StockTitan (wire mirror) · Aug 5 · governance · car builder
The rights become exercisable if a person or group acquires 15% or more of the common stock without board approval, or 20% for eligible passive 13G filers, at which point other holders may buy shares at a 50% discount and the acquirer's rights are void; the plan replaces one that expired the same day, and the company framed it as protecting long-term value and fair treatment in any potential takeover. It was adopted two days after the company reported a backlog of 3,972 units valued at US$344m. Why it matters: this is a governance signal at an OEM car builder, not a demand signal, and because it renews an expiring plan rather than introducing a new one it is better read as continuity than as a response to a specific approach — no acquirer has been disclosed.

CPKC anchors an $80m Americold cold-chain hub with DP World at Port Saint John
FreightWaves · Aug 19 · customer investment
The roughly $80m, 22,000-pallet-position temperature-controlled facility opened in June 2026 and relies on CPKC for the rail link inland, following DP World's $247m West Side terminal modernisation launched in January that lifted terminal capacity to about 1 million TEUs a year; the port handled 239,364 TEUs in 2025, up 29.4% year over year and 175% since 2021. Why it matters: a growing East Coast Canadian gateway gives CPKC an opportunity in refrigerated and protein traffic, though how much reaches the railroad depends on rail share and routing that were not disclosed. Cold cargo through a container terminal moves in refrigerated containers rather than refrigerator cars, so any equipment effect sits on the intermodal side.
Modern Rail Capital names Kevin McHugh president, succeeding Kevin Cook
Progressive Railroading · Aug 18 · leadership
McHugh takes the role at the Chicago-based rail lessor effective September 1, arriving from Wells Fargo Rail, where he was senior vice president of sales, with earlier leadership time at GE Capital Rail Services; Cook, president since 2021, moves to an advisory role with the board. Why it matters: a change of commercial leadership at a lessor, filled from the leasing sales side — the kind of appointment worth noting because lessors sit between the builders and the shippers on fleet decisions.
CMC Railroad closes an $87.9m facility that funds more than doubling railcar storage near Houston
CMC Railroad via GlobeNewswire — wire listing only; the release page did not load for our reader · Aug 18 · short line finance
The credit facility with Bank of Texas serves two purposes — refinancing existing debt and funding expansion at the 3,800-acre Gulf Inland Logistics Park in Dayton, northeast of Houston — so the full US$87.9m is not project spending; the expansion adds roughly 1,100 railcar storage spaces, taking the site from about 900 to about 2,000. Why it matters: storage and switching capacity at the edge of the Gulf Coast chemical and plastics complex is where cars wait between loads. More of it can mean productive staging or simply more idle cars, and stored cars accumulate less mileage-related wear — so read this as capacity positioning, not as a demand indicator either way.
Shipper Industries & Demand 1 story
Port of Vancouver USA takes its two millionth Subaru; lease extended to 2040
Progressive Railroading · Aug 18 · automotive · port data
About 2,000 vehicles a week arrive from Japan at the BNSF-served port, roughly 80% of which move to market by rail; the 33-year relationship reached its first million in 2014, and the agreement has been extended through 2040 with the leasehold growing from 40 to 43 acres. Why it matters: a commitment to 2040 keeps a rail-served vehicle gateway in place, and the second million vehicles arrived in roughly half the time the first took — a steadier read on the lane than the single AAR week available to us, in which national motor-vehicle-and-parts carloads were down 5.3%. It preserves autorack traffic on the lane without establishing future volume.

AAR launches a Freight Rail Research Consortium with four universities
FreightWaves, citing the AAR · Aug 10 · association initiative
The consortium pairs the association with MIT, the University of California, Berkeley, Rutgers and the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign to build research capacity on freight-rail economics, operations and technology; no funding figure was disclosed. AAR chief economist Rand Ghayad said industries stay competitive by investing in "ideas, research, talent, and institutions," not physical infrastructure alone. Why it matters: the association is building research capacity of its own at a moment when rail economics are contested before the Board; whether that output carries weight in policy argument will depend on the projects chosen and how they are published, neither of which has been set out.
Regulatory & Policy 1 story
STB expands delegations of authority and codifies its grant-stamp process, effective September 17
Surface Transportation Board, Release No. 26-20 · Aug 18 · final procedural rule
The Chief Counsel gains clarified authority over discovery disputes and routine procedural matters plus certain unopposed motor-carrier passenger transactions, the Chief of Passenger Rail and Investigations receives delegations in passenger proceedings under 49 U.S.C. § 24308, and the Board formally codified its practice of letting designated officials approve uncontested routine matters without a full written decision. Why it matters: procedural rather than substantive — it codifies practice the Board already follows, so it may improve administrative efficiency without necessarily changing processing times, and it does not alter the merits review or the merger schedule adopted the same day. Also today: no public update from the Belt Railway of Chicago's weekly Clearing Yard reporting to the Board, and the AAR weekly traffic report had not posted by our deadline.
Global Rail 1 story
Indian Railways moves container train operators to a single nationwide licence
Railway Supply weekly review, citing Indian Railways · Aug 12 · policy · India
The approved framework replaces route-specific permissions with an all-India licence and a uniform ₹25 crore registration fee for new applicants. Why it matters: the reform removes route-by-route fragmentation for container operators, though with no prior fee schedule given its net effect on entry costs is unclear, and any wagon demand depends on whether operators actually enter and how they source. It is the more concrete Indian development this week — the long-trailed 100,000-wagon tender has still not floated.
