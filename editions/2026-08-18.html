President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Merger — opposition to UP–NS is now formal and broad, and the Board has yet to set a schedule. A coalition of five shipper groups (American Chemistry Council, AFPM, The Fertilizer Institute, NITL, Alliance for Chemical Distribution) moved on August 6 to deny the application at the threshold stage, and seven state attorneys general (Montana, Iowa, Kansas, Florida, North Dakota, South Dakota, Tennessee) wrote on August 11 that the railroads' Committed Gateway Pricing "creates no new rail option for shippers" and covers only 0.9% of US rail traffic; UP and NS replied on August 13. Neither filing was carried in the first two editions and both are dated here. With BNSF, rail unions and now shippers and states on record, the shipper groups are arguing the application fails the Board's initial evidentiary threshold — and gateway conditions remain the issue most likely to affect competitive routings if the deal advances.
  2. Regulation — the two-person crew rule stands, for now. The 11th Circuit on August 11 rejected, 2–1, the challenge by the AAR, ASLRRA, BNSF, Union Pacific and four short lines to the FRA's 2024 crew-size rule, writing that "none of the railroads' arguments have merit"; the associations say they are reviewing their options, so further review remains possible. Crew size is a railroad cost item rather than an equipment one, but the ruling constrains one-person and autonomous operating models and is the biggest regulatory outcome of the month.
  3. Customers — two service-risk items and two standing watches. About 300 CPKC signals and communications workers in Canada (IBEW), on strike since May 31, rejected the railroad's latest offer by 89% on August 17 while CPKC runs on contingency plans; Norfolk Southern had its third derailment in six months at Horseshoe Curve on August 11 (12 cars, no injuries — the two earlier ones were traced to a mechanical fault and to track). No settlement is reported at National Steel Car (strike since August 12), and no public update yet from the Belt Railway of Chicago's new weekly reporting to the STB. AAR's weekly traffic report comes tomorrow.

Seven state attorneys general urge the STB to deny the UP–NS merger; shipper coalition filed to deny it a week earlier
FreightWaves (Aug 12, letter dated Aug 11); American Chemistry Council release (Aug 6) · not carried in earlier editions · regulatory docket FD 36873
The AGs of Montana, Iowa, Kansas, Florida, North Dakota, South Dakota and Tennessee told the Board the Committed Gateway Pricing remedy "creates no new rail option for shippers," would set rates "at the 70th percentile of their own comparable traffic rates," and applies to only 0.9% of US rail traffic; on August 6 the ACC, AFPM, The Fertilizer Institute, NITL and the Alliance for Chemical Distribution jointly moved to deny the application for failing the initial evidentiary threshold, and UP–NS answered the AGs on August 13. Why it matters: the review is still without a procedural schedule, and the opposition now spans shippers, states, BNSF and unions — the shipper groups argue the application fails the initial evidentiary threshold, and gateway conditions could materially affect competitive routings if the deal advances.
Federal appeals court upholds the FRA's two-person crew rule, 2–1
FreightWaves · Aug 12 (decision Aug 11) · not carried in earlier editions · court ruling
The 11th Circuit's 93-page decision rejected the challenge by the AAR, ASLRRA, BNSF, Union Pacific, Florida East Coast, Indiana Rail Road, Texas & Northern and Nebraska Central to the April 2024 rule, which generally requires a two-person minimum for covered freight operations, subject to specified exceptions and an approval process for one-person operations; SMART-TD called two-person crews "just common sense," and the associations are "reviewing the decision and considering their options." Why it matters: the largest regulatory outcome of the month for the railroads' cost structure and single-crew or autonomous operating plans; with the associations weighing their options, treat it as upheld for now rather than final.
Canadian IBEW members reject CPKC's latest contract offer, 89% against; strike has run since May 31
Progressive Railroading · Aug 17 · labor
About 300 communications and signals employees represented by IBEW System Council No. 11 voted 256–31 (96.6% turnout) to reject the offer; the union has filed 19 complaints with Canada's Industrial Relations Board over CPKC's use of replacement workers, and CPKC says operations continue under contingency plans. Why it matters: a signals-and-communications strike is a service-reliability risk at a major customer that has run eleven weeks with no end in sight; no service impact has been disclosed, but the CIRB complaints add regulatory exposure.
Norfolk Southern train derails at Horseshoe Curve — 12 cars, the third derailment there in six months
WPSU / Pennsylvania Capital-Star · Aug 12 (derailment Aug 11) · not carried in earlier editions · safety
Twelve cars derailed at about 3:30 p.m. on August 11 near Altoona, Pennsylvania, with no injuries and no hazardous materials; the FRA attributed the February 6 derailment (about 65 cars) to a mechanical error and the March 7 derailment (17 cars) to a track problem, and Norfolk Southern said it expected all tracks back in service the next afternoon. Why it matters: three derailments at one location in six months, with reportedly different causes, at a customer already under a merger microscope; the February finding of a mechanical cause is the part with equipment relevance — mechanical derailments often involve running gear such as wheels, bearings or trucks, though the failed subsystem has not been identified publicly.

BNSF and NFI announce a strategic partnership at Barstow International Gateway
BNSF Railway · Aug 17 · press release
NFI will provide warehousing, distribution, port drayage and transload services alongside BNSF's intermodal operations at the Southern California hub designed to connect freight from the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach to destinations across the country; no investment, acreage or opening date was disclosed. Why it matters: BNSF keeps building out its West Coast intermodal capacity while opposing the merger — this may support BNSF's intermodal throughput over time, though the announcement carries no equipment commitments and implies no incremental railcar requirement.

Shipper Industries & Demand 2 stories
Port of Long Beach traffic dips in July — imports flat, exports up 14.8%
Progressive Railroading, citing the Port of Long Beach · Aug 17 · port data
July volume was 1.7% below July 2025 (the port's second-busiest July), with imports about flat at 467,461 TEUs, exports up 14.8% to 104,843 and empties down 7.4%; seven-month volume is 5,758,086 TEUs, up 1.2% (figures as reported from the port's release). Neighboring Los Angeles reported July down 1.8% from June and is forecasting a 7% cargo decline for 2027. Why it matters: West Coast import softness is a mild headwind for inbound intermodal loadings toward Chicago, partly offset by the export rebound — with the caveat that one port's TEUs are not rail loadings, which depend on rail share and destination mix.
USDA crop progress: corn 60% good-to-excellent, soybeans 61%; corn maturing ahead of average
The Brock Report, citing USDA NASS · Aug 17 · government data
Corn and soybean condition each slipped one point on the week; corn is 76% in dough and 4% mature (ahead of last year's 2%), soybeans 85% setting pods, with excess rain in the eastern Corn Belt raising lodging and quality concerns. Why it matters: slightly advanced crop development may bring covered-hopper positioning decisions forward for a crop USDA already projects at record size for soybeans and second-largest for corn — a utilization signal for the existing hopper fleet more than a new-car one.

FRA dedicates $5.3 billion to grade crossings and Amtrak fleet upgrades
Progressive Railroading, citing US DOT / FRA · Aug 17 (announced Aug 14) · federal funding
Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced $5.3bn in National Railroad Partnership Program grants, including $2.05bn for 43 new US-built Amtrak trainsets, $140m to overhaul 41 locomotives, and funding to close more than 30 grade crossings and upgrade more than 1,000. Why it matters: mostly passenger money; the crossing work will fall partly on freight-owned lines, though projects were not itemized in the coverage, and the package signals where federal rail dollars are going this cycle. Elsewhere in regulation today: no procedural schedule from the STB on the merger, and no public update from the Belt Railway of Chicago's weekly Clearing Yard reporting; FRA and PHMSA pages blocked our reader.
