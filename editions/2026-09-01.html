President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Rail volumes are running positive on a broad base, not on one commodity. AAR's latest weekly report puts carloads up 3.2% and intermodal up 5.0% year over year, with year-to-date combined traffic up 3.3% through 33 weeks. The gains are spread across metallic ores and metals, chemicals and grain, while motor vehicles and parts is the one clear decliner. A broad advance is a more durable backdrop for wear-part and running-repair consumption than a single-commodity spike, though loadings count movements rather than cars, cycle times or ownership.
  2. The National Steel Car strike is now into its fourth week with no settlement reported. More than 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members in Hamilton have been out since August 12, which makes today day 21. The dispute still turns on wages and on the piecework incentive scheme, and a provincial mediator had been assigned as of August 18 without any reported breakthrough since. Be aware that searches on this strike surface a great deal of coverage of a different National Steel Car strike that ended in 2023 — that dispute is settled and unrelated, and the 2026 stoppage remains open.
  3. The tariff inclusions process is the cost-input channel worth watching, even when rail equipment is not in the round. Comments closed August 27 on a proposal to pull 14 more derivative products into the Section 232 steel, aluminium and copper duties, and no final determination has been announced. Railcars and railcar components are not on this particular list. The mechanism matters more than this round's contents: it is how steel-intensive goods get added to tariff scope over time, on a rolling basis, which makes it a standing input-cost watch rather than a one-off headline.

AAR reports US carloads up 3.2% and intermodal up 5.0% for the week ending August 22
Association of American Railroads — weekly rail traffic report · Aug 26 · association data
US railroads originated 235,885 carloads and 296,577 intermodal units in the week ending August 22, a combined 532,462 units and up 4.2% year over year. Metallic ores and metals (+2,054), chemicals (+1,923) and grain (+1,844) led the gainers while motor vehicles and parts fell 1,837 carloads; year to date through 33 weeks, combined volume of 16,813,896 units is up 3.3% on 2025. Why it matters: growth spread across ores, chemicals and grain is a more supportive backdrop for wear-part and running-repair demand than a narrow move, but these are loadings — they measure what moved, not car counts, cycle times or who owns the equipment.
National Steel Car strike enters its fourth week in Hamilton with no settlement reported
CBC News (Aug 12) and Canadian Occupational Safety on the dispute; World Socialist Web Site (Aug 31) for picket-line reporting · labour · standing watch item
More than 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members have been out at the Kenilworth Avenue North plant since 12:01 a.m. on August 12, which puts today at day 21, after roughly 91% turnout produced about 79% against the company's offer. The dispute still centres on wages and on the incentive scheme paying an extra 25% for meeting production thresholds, which the union links to three deaths at the plant since 2020; an Ontario Ministry of Labour mediator had been assigned as of August 18, and we found no report of a settlement, new offer or resumed bargaining, with coverage dated August 31 still describing workers on the picket line. Why it matters: a stoppage into its fourth week raises the chance of delivery-timing effects for anyone with cars on order there, though current plant production status is not established by any source we could read.

Aberdeen Carolina and Western Railway opens Candor Logistics Park in North Carolina
Progressive Railroading · Aug 31 · short line · industrial development
The short line opened a 30-acre transload and distribution site in Montgomery County that can hold more than 100 rail cars and handles fuels, food-grade products, plastic resins and bulk dry commodities. ACWR interchanges with Norfolk Southern at Charlotte and CSX at Aberdeen, and is targeting $50 million of investment and 50 to 100 jobs across two adjacent graded development parcels. Why it matters: a facility this size adds carload origination on a small base rather than moving national volumes, and is best read as one more sign that short-line industrial development continues to pull traffic onto rail.
SC Ports and the Army Corps open a feasibility study on deepening the Cooper River at Charleston
Progressive Railroading · Aug 31 · ports · intermodal
South Carolina Ports and the US Army Corps of Engineers signed a feasibility study agreement covering the Cooper River stretch between the Leatherman and North Charleston terminals, where North Charleston is maintained at 48 feet against the 52 feet reached elsewhere in a 2022 deepening. Congress authorised the study in the Water Resources Development Act of 2024 and federal money was included in fiscal 2026 appropriations, but no cost estimate or completion date has been set. Why it matters: a feasibility study is the earliest formal stage and commits no construction, though Charleston is served by CSX and Norfolk Southern, so any eventual capacity gain would feed intermodal and carload volumes years out.

Comment window closes on adding 14 derivative products to the Section 232 steel, aluminium and copper duties
Federal Register notice of Aug 6, read through KPMG and Steptoe summaries — the notice page itself was not reachable to us today · trade policy
The Bureau of Industry and Security proposed adding 14 derivative articles to the tariffs' scope — among them certain trailers and semi-trailers, specified cranes and lifting equipment, heat-exchanger parts and filled steel containers for propane and oxygen — with comments due August 27 and most additions carrying a 25% rate. Railcars and railcar components are not on this proposed list, and no final determination has been announced. Why it matters: the inclusions process is the standing mechanism by which steel-intensive goods are drawn into tariff scope over time, so it warrants monitoring as an input-cost channel even in rounds that leave rail equipment out.
NTSB Vice Chair Michael Graham to retire on October 2
Progressive Railroading · Aug 31 · agency leadership
Graham, sworn in to the National Transportation Safety Board in January 2020 and designated Vice Chair for a three-year term in April 2026, will leave the agency on October 2. He was the board member on scene for the 2023 Norfolk Southern derailment at East Palestine, Ohio, and came to the NTSB from a safety role at Textron Aviation. Why it matters: NTSB findings on rolling-stock components can shape industry practice and later rulemaking, so Board composition is worth tracking, although one member's departure does not by itself redirect any open investigation.
