President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Rival railroads are arguing for rejection of UP–NS and, in the alternative, for sweeping conditions. BNSF, CPKC and CSX urged the Surface Transportation Board to reject the deal while describing the trackage rights they will seek if it is approved, including about 824 miles of Norfolk Southern between Chicago and eastern Pennsylvania for BNSF. Union Pacific's revised application in May said it would withdraw if the Board imposed widespread line sales or trackage rights. Formal responsive applications are due November 18; if the Board approves with broad conditions, UP would then have to decide whether to close.
  2. U.S. rail volume dipped in week 36, but the calendar explains part of it. AAR reported total U.S. traffic down 3.7% for the week ending September 12, with carloads down 3.3% and intermodal down 4.1%. This year's week included Labor Day while the 2025 comparison week did not, and the prior week's 13.8% gain had the opposite calendar effect. The year-to-date figure, up 3.4%, is the steadier read for now.
  3. Europe has its first commercial equipment contract for digital automatic couplers, and it is pilot-scale. Knorr-Bremse said ÖBB Rail Cargo Group ordered DAC equipment for 23 freight cars and one locomotive, with live deployment starting in 2028 under the EU-funded PioDAC project. That is a small fleet, but it moves European coupler technology from test rigs toward revenue service. North American cars already use automatic mechanical couplers, and DAC adds automated air, power and data links, so this is a technology watch item rather than a change to our own coupler market.

BNSF, CPKC and CSX lay out the trackage rights they will seek if the UP–NS merger is approved
FreightWaves and Railway Supply accounts of STB filings · filed Sep 9, reported Sep 11–14 · customers · UP–NS merger · standing watch item
In September 9 filings describing the responsive applications they plan to submit, BNSF said it will seek overhead trackage rights over about 824 miles of Norfolk Southern between Chicago and Bethlehem, Pa., plus a neutral Gulf Coast terminal railroad, while CPKC and CSX both seek access over the Kansas City–St. Louis corridor; all three urged the Board to reject the merger outright. Those applications are due November 18, and Union Pacific's revised application in May said it would withdraw if the Board required widespread line sales or trackage rights. Why it matters: the requests may test UP's stated refusal of widespread conditions, so an approval with broad remedies would leave UP choosing whether to close.
U.S. rail traffic falls 3.7% in a Labor Day week; year-to-date volume holds at plus 3.4%
AAR weekly rail traffic report · Sep 16 · associations · traffic data
AAR reported 494,865 U.S. carloads and intermodal units for the week ending September 12, down 3.7%, with grain up 3,461 carloads while motor vehicles and parts fell 3,313, chemicals 3,195 and coal 2,229. Combined U.S. traffic for the first 36 weeks totals 18,385,518 units, up 3.4%, with carloads up 2.7% and intermodal up 4.0%. Why it matters: this year's week contained Labor Day and last year's comparison week did not, so the weekly decline is partly calendar and the year-to-date trend is the better signal.
Knorr-Bremse wins Europe's first equipment contract for digital automatic couplers
Knorr-Bremse news release · Sep 17 · competitor · couplers · Europe
Knorr-Bremse said ÖBB Rail Cargo Group will equip 23 freight cars and one locomotive with its digital automatic couplers, including electronic control units and monitoring sensors, for live operation beginning in 2028 under the EU-funded PioDAC project; no contract value was given. Why it matters: this is a first commercial step for DAC in Europe at pilot scale, and it bears on coupler technology direction there rather than on North American coupler demand.

Union Pacific says more than 500 customers back the Norfolk Southern combination
Union Pacific news release · Sep 16 · customers · UP–NS merger
Union Pacific said shippers in agriculture, energy, fertilizer, forest products, automotive, intermodal and industrial chemicals have endorsed the merger, naming POET Biofuels, Hub Group and Knight-Swift among them, and that recent filings added more than 150 support letters. Why it matters: this is the applicants' own tally for the record, set against rival railroads' opposition and requested conditions due November 18.
Norfolk Southern pitches the merger to intermodal users as cutting railcar handlings
FreightWaves · Sep 17 · customers · intermodal · IANA conference
At the Intermodal Association of North America's conference in Long Beach, NS marketing vice president Shawn Tureman said the combination would convert about 10,000 interline lanes to single-line service and eliminate roughly 2,400 railcar handlings and 60,000 car-miles a day. Why it matters: these are the applicants' projections under Board review, not results; if realised, fewer handlings would mean fewer switching impacts on cars, not more traffic by itself.
TNW Corp.'s Northeast Texas Railway begins service on a 66-mile line
Progressive Railroading · Sep 16 · customers · short lines
NETX now operates about 66 miles between Greenville and Winfield, Texas, serving agriculture, building materials, chemicals, pipe and recycling customers, with interchanges to Union Pacific and CPKC. Why it matters: it adds a short-line operator with two Class I connections, unlikely on its own to affect overall car demand.

Rail-bound containers waited longer at Los Angeles–Long Beach in August
Pacific Merchant Shipping Association data as reported by FreightWaves · Sep 16 · intermodal · ports
Average terminal dwell for rail-bound import containers rose to 6.75 days in August from 6.34 days in July, while truck dwell eased to 2.95 days, as the two ports handled 1,875,899 TEUs. Why it matters: rail boxes are clearing the largest U.S. gateway more slowly, though this is one month's terminal measure and does not identify the cause.

STB announces settlement of Amtrak's 13-year complaint against CN
STB news release · Sep 16 · regulatory · host railroads
The Board said Amtrak and CN resolved Amtrak's 2013 complaint over the terms and compensation for its use of CN's facilities, after interim Board findings in 2019, failed mediation in 2020 and a technical conference in 2025; no financial terms were disclosed. Why it matters: it ends one of the longest-running disputes before the Board, with no direct bearing on freight-car demand.
