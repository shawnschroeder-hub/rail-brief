President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Today's rail traffic headline reads far better than the underlying week. The AAR reported US volume for the week ending September 5 at 533,545 carloads and intermodal units, up 13.8%, with intermodal up 18.0% — against roughly 4% in recent weeks and 3.6% year to date. The week AAR compares against, ending September 6, 2025, contained Labor Day; this year's did not, because the holiday fell on September 7, 2026. The AAR release carries no commentary on that, so the reading is ours — and a negative distortion is likely next week, when a holiday-shortened 2026 week is measured against a full 2025 one. The year-to-date trend in carloads, not either week's combined total, is what should inform planning.
  2. A third party is proposing to re-plumb the New Orleans gateway rather than simply oppose the merger. County Line Rail has asked the Board to authorise a neutral operator, the New Orleans Terminal Railroad, with no Class I ownership, publishing a single tariff at equal pricing, buying about 7.7 miles of track from Norfolk Southern and dispatching about 13.6 miles of cross-terminal trackage. County Line labels it a responsive application inside the merger docket, FD 36873 — a different instrument from the protests and condition requests that have dominated the record so far. If the Board were to entertain structural remedies of this kind, the proceeding becomes less binary than approve-or-deny, and terminal control in other congested gateways becomes a live question. Nothing indicates the Board has accepted the filing or reached its merits.
  3. Hamilton would be at day 29, with the picture unchanged from a week ago. If the National Steel Car stoppage has continued, it has now run four weeks and a day, with a provincial mediator assigned and the union saying it remains available to talk. Nothing published this week establishes a settlement, a revised offer or a bargaining date, and the most recent coverage we could open is still dated August 31 — so the silence is not evidence in either direction. The standing instruction holds: our own call-offs and shipment schedules into Hamilton are the reliable read on plant status.

US rail traffic posts a 13.8% week against a 2025 comparison that included Labor Day
Association of American Railroads · published today, Sep 9, for the week ending Sep 5 · industry data
Total US volume was 533,545 carloads and intermodal units, with carloads at 234,397 (up 8.9%) and intermodal at 299,148 (up 18.0%); nine of ten commodity groups gained, led by metallic ores and metals (+4,859), grain (+4,373) and chemicals (+3,848), while coal fell 3,547 carloads. Across 35 weeks year to date, combined volume is 17,890,653 units, up 3.6% — the more representative figure, since the comparison week ending September 6, 2025 contained Labor Day while this year's week did not, the holiday having fallen on September 7. Why it matters: the holiday mismatch materially distorts the comparison, so the week is no evidence of a demand break — and year-to-date carloads and commodity mix, rather than a combined total that counts containers instead of cars, are the better read on car cycles and wear-parts consumption.
A neutral terminal operator is proposed for the New Orleans gateway inside the merger docket
Progressive Railroading · Sep 8 · regulatory · merger
County Line Rail proposes the New Orleans Terminal Railroad, with its chief commercial officer Bryan Boaz as president, to run a gateway where Norfolk Southern operates the Back Belt Line and Union Pacific controls the Huey P. Long Bridge crossing. Under the plan no Class I would hold an ownership stake, a single tariff would price all carriers equally, NOTR would acquire about 7.7 miles of track from NS and dispatch about 13.6 miles, and the proposal is filed as a responsive application in docket FD 36873. Why it matters: a responsive application seeks affirmative relief tied to the merger rather than simply opposing it, which is worth watching as a template for other congested gateways — though nothing indicates the Board has accepted this filing or reached its merits.

National Steel Car strike would be at day 29, with no settlement in any source we could read
CBC on the dispute and the Ontario Ministry of Labour on mediation; most recent reporting we could open is dated Aug 31 · competitor · labour · standing watch item
Roughly 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members walked out at the Hamilton, Ontario plant on August 12 over wages and an incentive scheme paying an extra 25% of base wage at production thresholds, with a provincial mediator assigned and the union saying it remains available to talk. No source we could open this week reports a settlement, a revised offer or a scheduled bargaining date, and the most recent coverage we could read is dated August 31. Why it matters: if production is still halted, line-side consumption of our components should be well down, though receipts can lag — and with the press silent, our own call-offs and shipment timing into Hamilton are the signal we actually control.

Norfolk Southern applies to discontinue or modify a railroad signal system
Federal Register — FRA notice · published today, Sep 9 · regulatory · notice
The FRA published Norfolk Southern's application alongside two other rail notices the same day — a Delmarva Central waiver request on a movable bridge and a Harrisburg, Lincoln & Lancaster petition to modify existing relief on locomotive safety features. Why it matters: these notices open comment periods rather than deciding anything, and a modification may mean modernisation as readily as removal — they mark where a railroad is reworking fixed plant, not necessarily shrinking it.

Britain sets a target to grow rail freight at least 40% by 2040
UK Department for Transport · Sep 8 · global · policy target
The target is a first milestone toward an existing ambition of at least 75% growth by 2050, and comes with Great British Railways taking strategic oversight of network access under two statutory duties to support freight growth. The government estimates the annual value of goods moved by rail would rise from about £33.7bn to £49.5bn, with construction materials up 45% and higher-value goods up 65%. Why it matters: these are government projections rather than committed volumes, and Britain's wagon fleet and loading gauge differ from North America's — the read for us is directional, on where European wagon and component demand may find policy support.
VIA Rail awards Alstom a CA$4.7 billion order for 313 cars built in Quebec and Ontario
Progressive Railroading · Sep 8 · global · builder award
The contract covers 313 Adessia cars in nine configurations plus 15 years of technical support and spare parts, with engineering at St-Bruno-de-Montarville, car bodies at La Pocatière and final assembly at Thunder Bay, and is expected to create or support up to 670 direct jobs at Alstom Canada. Why it matters: this is passenger equipment rather than our market, and its plants and supplier base are distinct from ours — the read is simply that an award of this size ties up skilled rail-manufacturing labour in Canada for years.
