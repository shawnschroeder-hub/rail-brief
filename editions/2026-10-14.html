President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Four Class I railroads cleared the STB's revenue test. Union Pacific, CSX, BNSF and Norfolk Southern were found revenue adequate for 2025, up from two carriers a year earlier. The trade report credits mostly a lower threshold (9.79% versus 10.68%), and CSX's and NS's own returns fell. The finding is an accounting test that feeds rate-regulation debates; it is not a direct signal of railroad equipment spending.
  2. Right-of-way work carries liability risk. Utah investigators concluded that sparks from a brush-cutting attachment used in Union Pacific vegetation maintenance started the July Adams Fire, which burned about 72 acres and damaged homes. The fire was ruled accidental, but a formal cause finding can support damage claims. It is a reminder that routine maintenance practices, not only train operations, create exposure in fire season.
  3. A busy week of supplier and customer data is close. Commerce's decision on whether to open the Mexican tank car trade investigations is due around October 20, and Wabtec and CSX report third-quarter results on October 21. Greenbrier follows on October 26, Trinity on October 29 and Timken on November 4. Together they will give the first broad read on how fourth-quarter car-building and component demand is shaping up.

Increase in the number of rail companies meeting income adequacy in the US
RaillyNews · Oct 12 · STB · annual revenue adequacy determination (trade-press account)
The Surface Transportation Board found Union Pacific (17.09% return on net investment), CSX (11.40%), BNSF (10.91%) and Norfolk Southern (10.29%) revenue adequate for 2025 against a 9.79% threshold, according to RaillyNews, compared with two carriers in 2024, when the threshold was 10.68%. The report attributes most of the increase to the lower threshold, and CSX's and NS's returns actually declined from 2024 (13.72% and 10.52%). Why it matters: the findings are regularly cited in rate-regulation debates, but they are a backward-looking accounting test and do not by themselves indicate changes in railroad equipment spending.

Sparks from freight railroad equipment caused Adams Fire, state investigation finds
Daily Dispatch · Oct 14 · Union Pacific · state investigation
Utah's Division of Forestry, Fire and State Lands concluded that the July 27 Adams Fire in Washington Terrace, which burned about 72 acres and damaged several homes, was started by sparks from a brush-cutting attachment used in Union Pacific vegetation maintenance, after investigators found a damaged steel J-bolt and metal shavings near the origin. The fire was classified as accidental. Why it matters: a formal cause finding can expose a railroad to damage claims and may prompt tighter fire-season limits on right-of-way maintenance work.

Steel manufacturer Webco selects Port Muskogee for expansion
Progressive Railroading · Oct 13 · metals · shipper expansion
Webco Industries of Sand Springs, Oklahoma, will expand carbon and stainless-steel tube manufacturing at an upgraded, port-owned slitter facility in the Port Muskogee Industrial Park, adding about 10 jobs within six months; no investment figure was given. The port reaches Union Pacific and BNSF through the OmniTRAX-managed Port Muskogee Railroad and also has river barge access. Why it matters: it is a small rail-served steel-products expansion, a modest positive for metals carloads rather than a meaningful demand signal.
Regulatory & Policy 1 story
Illinois commission approves Vermilion County rail safety upgrades
Progressive Railroading · Oct 13 · Illinois Commerce Commission · order approved
The Illinois Commerce Commission approved more than $1.2 million from the state's Grade Crossing Protection Fund for automatic warning signals and surface rehabilitation at three Union Pacific crossings on East North Road near Rankin, with the fund paying 95% of signal costs (capped at $1,214,115) and UP paying the rest plus future signal maintenance. Work must be completed within 18 months, and the fund, financed by motor fuel tax revenue, receives $42 million a year. Why it matters: state crossing programs are a steady, if small, source of public money for safety on freight lines.

Global Rail 1 story
High energy costs threaten rail freight's viability, warns PKP CEO
Railway Gazette International · Oct 12 · Poland · executive interview (remarks made Sept 23)
Alan Beroud, chief executive of Polish state rail holding PKP SA, said traction energy costs, driven by taxes on coal-fired power and the EU Emissions Trading System, threaten rail freight's viability, noting that PKP Cargo has already cut 5,000 jobs and closed some depots. He backed earmarking 10% to 25% of emissions-trading revenue for rail and said military-mobility funding could support flat wagons and lines serving defence sites. Why it matters: energy costs and weak heavy industry are squeezing European wagonload operators, which tempers the wagon-demand outlook in parts of the EU.
