President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Greenbrier's backlog grew for the first time since fiscal 2024. The company reported a new railcar backlog of 14,000 units, valued at about $2.1 billion, as of August 31, and guided fiscal 2027 revenue to $2.8–3.2 billion. It estimates North American industry railcar deliveries of 26,000–28,000 units over its fiscal 2027; that is Greenbrier's own estimate, not RSI-ARCI data. Management said tariff uncertainty, especially in tank cars, delayed long-lived railcar investment decisions, while higher rail traffic and diesel prices lifted early inquiries.
  2. The customer spending news this edition is about terminals, track and locomotives, not cars. CSX described continued investment at its Chambersburg, Pennsylvania intermodal terminal, where volume reached 192,000 units in 2025. Patriot Rail will receive about $47.7 million in federal CRISI grants for locomotives, track and bridges across its short lines. Neither changes freight car demand directly.
  3. A detector-based brake test is moving toward a public hearing. FRA has scheduled a November 18 hearing in St. Paul on CPKC's request to use wayside wheel temperature detectors in place of some manual Class I brake tests, with comments reopened to December 18. Results due this week: CPKC on October 28, GATX and Trinity Industries on October 29 and CN on October 30, then Timken on November 4 and FreightCar America on November 9. The National Steel Car strike that began August 12 has not been reported settled.

Greenbrier Reports Fourth Quarter and Fiscal 2026 Results
Greenbrier · Oct 26 · company release · fiscal year ended August 31
Greenbrier reported fiscal 2026 revenue of $2,461.1 million and diluted EPS of $2.49, with fourth-quarter diluted EPS of $0.27 ($0.39 core, excluding $4 million of facility rationalization costs) and a new railcar backlog of 14,000 units, about $2.1 billion, the first sequential rise since fiscal 2024. For fiscal 2027 it guided revenue of $2.8–3.2 billion, EPS of $2.75–3.75 and its own estimate of 26,000–28,000 North American industry deliveries. Why it matters: this is one builder's view, but a rising backlog would support new-build component demand if orders convert as guided.

Stronger together in China: Knorr-Bremse expands supplier partnerships
Knorr-Bremse · Oct 27 · company release · China sourcing
At a supplier summit in Suzhou, Knorr-Bremse said it plans to broaden its Chinese supplier base and raise local procurement from about €500 million a year, citing 15 locations and more than €1 billion of 2025 revenue in China. CSX invests in Chambersburg terminal growth
Progressive Railroading · Oct 26 · CSX · intermodal terminal
CSX said volume at its Chambersburg, Pennsylvania intermodal terminal rose from about 159,000 units in 2023 to 192,000 in 2025; it added a crane, two reach stackers and parking in 2025, has a maintenance-facility expansion under way and is considering a larger terminal expansion. No dollar figure was disclosed. Why it matters: it is intermodal terminal capacity spending, which relates mainly to well-car and container flows rather than to carload equipment.
Patriot Rail secures $47.7M in CRISI grants for infrastructure, locomotives
Progressive Railroading · Oct 26 · Patriot Rail · FRA CRISI awards
Patriot Rail will receive about $47.7 million in federal CRISI grants: up to $15.2 million to replace 22 locomotives and add four, up to $16.8 million for track, tie and ballast work on five short lines, and up to $15.7 million for eight bridges on six short lines. Why it matters: better track and bridges can let short lines handle heavier cars, but the money goes to infrastructure and locomotives rather than freight cars.

Petition for Waiver of Compliance and Notice of Public Hearing (CPKC air brake testing)
Federal Register (FRA), via GovInfo · Oct 21 · notice · docket FRA-2025-0055
FRA will hold a public hearing on November 18 in St. Paul, Minnesota, with virtual participation, on CPKC's petition for relief from several air brake testing rules so that brake effectiveness can be checked by wayside wheel temperature detectors on heavy grade instead of some manual Class I brake tests. FRA reopened comments to December 18; the notice is not a decision on the petition. Why it matters: wider use of detector-based inspection would change how wheel and brake condition is found in service, which could in time affect wear-part replacement patterns.
