President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Farm groups are lining up against the UP–NS merger. The National Farmers Union, which says it represents more than 220,000 family farmers and ranchers, asked the STB to reject the deal and called for expanded reciprocal switching. Notices of intent to participate in the case are due tomorrow, September 30. Shipper opposition like this tends to push the fight toward competitive conditions, which is where the outcome for rail traffic patterns will be decided.
  2. Rail containers are sitting longer at Los Angeles and Long Beach. Rail-bound container dwell reached 6.75 days in August, the highest reading of 2026, up from 6.34 days in July, according to the Pacific Merchant Shipping Association. The Port of Los Angeles just had its busiest June–August on record, with early holiday shipments cited. Strong import volume supports intermodal demand, but longer dwell also slows equipment turns, so watch whether railroads and ports clear the backlog this fall.
  3. Europe's digital automatic coupler is moving toward commercial service. At a September 23 demonstration, European rail freight leaders showed automated coupling, train composition detection and remote uncoupling, with ÖBB's pioneer trains due in service from 2028 and full fleet migration planned only after 2030. Knorr-Bremse, which already holds Europe's first commercial DAC equipment orders, also signed a freight technology memorandum with the China Academy of Railway Sciences that includes digital couplers and braking. This is a European and Chinese story for now, but it is where a leading coupler and brake competitor is putting its development effort.

"Higher rates, less reliable service": family farmers oppose the UP–NS merger
FreightWaves · Sep 28 · regulatory · UP–NS merger
The National Farmers Union urged the STB to reject the merger, with President Rob Larew saying that when railroads consolidate "family farmers pay the price," and the group backed expanded reciprocal switching and antitrust enforcement. The NFU argues the deal would weaken competition and raise costs for captive agricultural shippers. Why it matters: agricultural opposition adds pressure on the Board to impose competitive conditions, days before notices of intent to participate are due on September 30.
Rail container dwell at Los Angeles and Long Beach hits a 2026 high of 6.75 days
IndexBox, citing the Pacific Merchant Shipping Association · Sep 21 · shippers · intermodal
Rail-bound container dwell rose to 6.75 days in August from 6.34 days in July, while truck-bound dwell eased to 2.95 days, per PMSA data; the Port of Los Angeles handled more than 2.9 million TEUs in June through August, its busiest three months on record. PMSA attributes the volume to resilient consumer demand and early holiday shipments. Why it matters: strong imports support intermodal volume, but longer rail dwell ties up well cars and chassis and points to capacity strain at the ports' rail interface.

Knorr-Bremse and China Academy of Railway Sciences sign freight technology memorandum
Railway Gazette International · Sep 21 · competitors · China
The memorandum, signed in Beijing, covers digital train architecture, predictive maintenance, wagon monitoring and digital automatic couplers, plus driver assistance based on Knorr-Bremse's LEADER system and braking for long-distance and heavy-haul freight. Knorr-Bremse says it builds on more than 20 years of cooperation and moves the relationship to joint development of system-level products. Why it matters: it gives a direct coupler and brake competitor a development channel into China's freight wagon fleet, though a memorandum is not an order.
Note on National Steel Car: the strike by about 1,200 USW Local 7135 members, under way since August 12, has not been reported settled. The last status we could confirm (CHCH, updated September 18) said the company had left talks and no bargaining sessions were scheduled. No settlement has been verified as of this edition.

Railway Association of Canada issues its latest emissions report
Progressive Railroading, citing RAC · Sep 28 · associations · Canada
For 2024, RAC reports Tier 4 locomotives at 10.5% of active fleets, biofuel at over 8% of rail fuel use (up 56% from 2023), Class I emissions intensity down 32.1% since 2005, and CA$4.5 billion invested in Canadian rail infrastructure. Why it matters: the figures describe locomotives and fuel rather than freight cars, but they show the emissions benchmarks Canadian railroads are reporting against under their 2023–2030 agreement with Transport Canada.

Europe's digital automatic coupler project reaches "a turning point" at demonstration
Railway Gazette International · event Sep 23, published Sep 25 · global · Europe
More than 100 freight-sector representatives saw automated coupling, train composition detection with automated brake testing, and remote uncoupling from the cab; ÖBB's pioneer trains enter commercial service from 2028 for a two-year assessment, with full fleet migration planned after 2030 subject to a business case. The European private wagon owners' group UIP called DAC one of its two strategic priorities. Why it matters: the timetable is still long and Europe-specific, but the technology is maturing in the hands of competing coupler suppliers.
UK government explores buying trains rather than leasing them
GOV.UK (Department for Transport) · Sep 28 · global · UK
Under a new rolling stock and infrastructure strategy, Great British Railways will compare public ownership, leasing and other financing for each new fleet instead of defaulting to leasing; the government puts leasing and maintenance costs at £4 billion a year and rolling stock company dividends at £2.5 billion over ten years. Existing leases are unaffected. Why it matters: this is mainly a UK passenger-fleet financing change, but it signals governments questioning the lessor model that also dominates freight car ownership.
