President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Rail volumes keep growing, led by intermodal. AAR data for the week ending October 3 show US traffic up 5.1%, with intermodal up 7.4% and carloads up 2.4%, and seven of 10 carload groups higher. Metals and petroleum products led the carload gains, which is a positive sign for car utilization, though one week does not by itself signal new equipment demand.
  2. Grain is strong in Canada but softer in the US. CN reported a record third quarter for Western Canadian grain at 7.94 million metric tons, while US grain carloads fell 5.2% in the latest AAR week. The two measures differ (Canadian tonnage versus US carloads), so the picture is regional rather than a single North American trend.
  3. UP's CEO projects near-certainty on the merger; the Board has not ruled. Jim Vena said he is "99.99%" confident of approval, while opponents argue the deal concentrates too much market power. Comments, protests and requests for conditions are due at the STB on November 18, and third-quarter earnings begin with CSX on October 21 and Union Pacific on October 22.

CN sets another quarterly grain record
Progressive Railroading · Oct 7 · grain · company-reported figures
CN said it moved a record 7.94 million metric tons of grain and grain products from Western Canada in the third quarter of 2026, above the previous record of 7.48 million metric tons set in 2020, crediting customer demand, supply-chain coordination and its operating plan. Why it matters: CN's figure is Canadian grain tonnage and is not comparable to US grain carloads, which AAR shows down 5.2% in the latest week, so grain strength is regional rather than continent-wide.
Union Pacific CEO "99.99%" confident the Norfolk Southern merger will win approval
FreightWaves · Oct 7 · UP–NS merger · executive remarks
Jim Vena told Fox Business on October 6 that he is "99.99%" confident regulators will approve the deal, while acknowledging some doubt, and the railroads say the combination would cut 24 to 48 hours from transit times by removing east–west interchange delays. Opponents including unions, agricultural groups and competing railroads argue it would give the merged company too much market power. Why it matters: the confidence is the applicant's own; the Board has not ruled, and comments, protests and requests for conditions are due November 18.

AAR weekly traffic: US volume up 5.1% as intermodal gains outpace carloads
AAR data via FreightWaves · Oct 7 · rail traffic · week ending Oct 3
US railroads moved 529,712 carloads and intermodal units in the week ending October 3, up 5.1% from the same week of 2025, with carloads of 230,711 up 2.4% and intermodal units of 299,001 up 7.4%; seven of 10 carload groups rose, led by metallic ores and metals (+12.8%) and petroleum products (+12.2%), while grain fell 5.2%. Combined US traffic for the first 39 weeks reached 19,988,290 units, up 3.5%, and North American volume across nine reporting railroads rose 4.4% for the week. Why it matters: steady carload growth in metals and petroleum is a positive sign for car utilization, though a single week's data does not by itself signal new equipment demand.

Ukraine sets timetable for opening its rail market to competition
Railway Gazette International · Oct 8 · Europe · government roadmap
Ukraine's cabinet approved a reform roadmap on September 23 requiring state railway Ukrzaliznytsia to separate infrastructure management from train operations by the end of 2027, with the 1,435 mm standard-gauge network due to open to competition in 2031. The main 1,520 mm network will not open until martial law ends, and the plan would end the system in which freight revenue covers passenger losses. Why it matters: the timetable is long and Railway Gazette reports there are not yet enough votes in parliament for full market opening, so near-term effects on Ukrainian freight equipment demand are unclear.
