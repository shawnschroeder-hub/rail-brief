President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. GATX's quarter shows lease demand still firm, with renewal pricing gains slowing. GATX's Rail North America segment profit rose to $121.3 million from $70.7 million a year earlier, helped partly by higher gains on asset sales. Renewal lease rates on its own fleet rose 12.4%, down from 22.8% a year ago, and utilization was 98.4% excluding boxcars. That is one lessor's fleet, not a market-wide rate, but it points to a tight existing-car market that is cooling at the margin.
  2. A West Coast port deadline lands tomorrow. About 250 ILWU Local 26 watchmen at Los Angeles and Long Beach terminals face a strike deadline of 5 p.m. Pacific on Wednesday, November 4, after talks were extended from November 1. Their terminals handle roughly 85% of Long Beach's cargo and 33% of Los Angeles's, so a stoppage could interrupt import flows that feed intermodal rail. Talks are continuing with federal mediators.
  3. The UP–NS fight turns procedural; supplier news is quiet. Union Pacific has asked the STB to reverse a judge's order to make public a video of CEO Jim Vena's remarks to shippers, and CPKC has moved to enforce that order; comments on the merger are due November 18. Competitor news was quiet, and the National Steel Car strike that began August 12 has not been reported settled. Timken reports third-quarter results on November 4 and FreightCar America on November 9.

GATX Corporation reports 2026 third-quarter results
GATX (SEC filing) · Oct 29 · company release · earnings · lessor
GATX reported third-quarter net income of $105.2 million, or $2.92 per diluted share, versus $82.2 million, or $2.25, a year earlier, with Rail North America segment profit of $121.3 million versus $70.7 million, helped partly by higher gains on asset dispositions. Its Lease Price Index rose 12.4% (22.8% a year earlier), renewal success was 86.1%, utilization excluding its roughly 9,000 boxcars was 98.4% on a fleet of about 199,100 cars, and the company now expects full-year EPS to modestly exceed the top of its prior guidance range. Why it matters: a lessor this size keeping cars nearly fully placed supports maintenance and running-repair demand, but the slower renewal-rate gains describe GATX's own fleet and are not a market-wide lease-rate measure.
Strike threat at Los Angeles–Long Beach ports delayed as union and employers extend talks
gCaptain · Oct 30 · trade press · ports · labor
The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26, representing about 250 maritime watchmen, extended their strike deadline from November 1 to 5 p.m. Pacific on Wednesday, November 4, with federal mediators assisting. Local 26 members work at terminals handling roughly 85% of Port of Long Beach cargo and 33% at the Port of Los Angeles. Why it matters: a stoppage could disrupt the import containers that feed western intermodal trains, though the coastwide longshore contract is not in play.
Union Pacific refusal to disclose video 'contemptuous,' Canadian Pacific argues
MLex · Nov 2 · legal press · UP–NS merger · STB
Union Pacific has asked the STB to reverse Administrative Law Judge Jenifer Soulikias's order to make public its video of CEO Jim Vena's March remarks at a shipper conference, and filed an emergency motion for a temporary stay, according to MLex. CPKC responded with a motion to enforce the order, arguing UP missed the appeal deadline and did not produce the video by the judge's deadline. Why it matters: the dispute is procedural, but it signals how contested the record will be as comments and protests on the UP–NS merger come due on November 18.

New Georgia hub aims to take 26,000 containers off the road
FreightWaves · Oct 30 · trade press · intermodal · Norfolk Southern
Georgia Ports Authority held the grand opening of its $134 million Gainesville Inland Port, which has run since May 4 with Norfolk Southern service to Savannah five days a week and is designed for 200,000 containers a year at full build-out. The authority expects 26,000 containers to shift from truck to rail in the first year but has not disclosed volumes since opening. Why it matters: inland ports like this add short-haul intermodal flows on a customer's network, though the volumes are modest relative to the Class I system.
Shipper Industries & Demand 1 story
Roberts Bank Terminal 2 listed as a project of national interest
Government of Canada — Major Projects Office · Oct 30 · government · ports · intermodal
Canada listed the Vancouver Fraser Port Authority's Roberts Bank Terminal 2 under the Building Canada Act, a step meant to coordinate the remaining federal approvals for a terminal that would add about 2.4 million TEUs, roughly 50% more container capacity at the Port of Vancouver, including a widened causeway for added road and rail. Trade press put the cost at about C$5.9 billion. Why it matters: if built, it would add long-run import-intermodal volume for CN and CPKC, but construction and any rail traffic are years away.
Regulatory & Policy 1 story
FRA issues $1.24B for grade crossing elimination projects
Progressive Railroading · Nov 2 · trade press · FRA · grants
The FRA awarded $1.24 billion in 91 Railroad Crossing Elimination grants across 37 states for fiscal 2025–2026, covering projects from planning through construction. The largest include $107.7 million for New York State DOT, $90.4 million for the Peninsula Corridor Joint Powers Board in California and $72 million for the Port Authority of Kansas City. Why it matters: grade separations can ease blocked crossings and train delays on customers' lines, but the money flows to public sponsors and civil works, not rolling stock.

Jupiter Wagons wins Indian Railways order for 2,583 open wagons
Railway Gazette International · Nov 2 · trade press (contract round-up item) · India · wagons
India's Ministry of Railways awarded Jupiter Wagons an INR 11.4 billion contract for 2,583 BOXNHL and BOXNS open wagons for bulk freight such as coal and iron ore; no delivery period was given. Jupiter cited its value-chain integration, including an upcoming wheelset facility in Odisha. Why it matters: Indian wagon builders adding in-house wheelsets could narrow the market for outside wheel and component suppliers there over time.
