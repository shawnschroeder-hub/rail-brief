President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The FRA's August 31 deregulatory package reaches into our own catalogue. Six final rules published that day take effect on September 30, and four of them reach equipment rather than paperwork: end-of-car cushioning units, freight cars more than 50 years old, brake system maintenance and inspection, and AC-traction locomotive wheel sets — the last two carried under Regulatory & Policy below. Each relaxes a removal, approval or tolerance criterion rather than changing a design or performance standard. The practical read is that from September 30 the administrative friction of keeping older cars in service falls and condition indicators become more valuable on cushioning units, though letting droplet-showing units stay in service may equally reduce replacement demand, so the net direction is not yet clear.
  2. Restricted speed is the industry's safety subject of the day, from two directions at once. FRA published Safety Advisory 2026-01 this morning, summarising seven recent accidents in which crews failed to operate at restricted speed and pressing railroads on compliance in positive train control restricted mode. Separately, MxV Rail — the AAR's research and testing arm — selected Rail Vision for locomotive sensor testing explicitly aimed at restricted speed enforcement. An advisory is a recommendation and a testing selection is not a purchase or a deployment, but a regulator and an industry technology programme pointing at the same problem on the same day is worth monitoring, and the restricted-speed rules the advisory discusses are enforceable now.
  3. Volume growth is real and broad; the Hamilton stoppage is not resolved. US traffic ran 4.1% ahead of last year in the week ending August 29, with intermodal up 5.7% and carloads up 2.2%, and the gains spread across metals, grain and nonmetallic minerals rather than sitting in one commodity. Sustained running of that kind shows up first in wear parts and running repairs, so read it as an aftermarket signal rather than an order-book one. Meanwhile roughly 1,200 Steelworkers remain out at National Steel Car on day 23 with no settlement in any source we could open — worth checking against our own component call-offs and shipments into Hamilton, since a builder that is not building is not consuming parts.
Top Stories 4 stories
FRA makes permanent the relief letting an end-of-car cushioning unit stay in service with oil droplets
Federal Register — FRA final rule, Docket FRA-2025-0119 · published Aug 31, effective Sep 30 · regulatory · components
The rule amends freight car draft arrangement and end-of-car cushioning unit regulations so a car may remain in service when its EOCC is operative and fitted with a unit condition indicator showing the unit has not discharged, even where clearly formed oil droplets are present. Where a unit carries no condition indicator, the existing duty to repair or replace an EOCC showing clearly formed oil droplets is preserved. Why it matters: this is the item in the package closest to our own product line, raising the value of an indicator-equipped unit while also letting droplet-showing units stay in service, so the net effect on replacement demand is ambiguous and confined to car types using end-of-car cushioning.
FRA drops the special-approval requirement for freight cars over 50 years old and certain listed component types
Federal Register — FRA final rule, Docket FRA-2025-0117 · published Aug 31, effective Sep 30 · regulatory · fleet
Railroads will no longer need FRA special approval to place or continue in service a car more than 50 years old, or one carrying a design or component type listed in appendix A to part 215, and will instead file a notice supplying information previously required in a petition. Why it matters: from September 30, lowering the administrative cost of keeping the oldest cars and appendix A component types in service could at the margin defer some replacement decisions, though AAR interchange and service-life eligibility is set by the industry rather than FRA and is untouched by this rule.
FRA issues Safety Advisory 2026-01 on restricted speed after seven accidents, singling out PTC restricted mode
Federal Register — FRA notice · published today, Sep 3 · regulatory · safety
The advisory reminds railroads and employees of the regulations and operating rules governing restricted speed, summarises seven recent train accidents involving failure to operate at restricted speed, and recommends steps to secure compliance, with particular emphasis on operations in positive train control restricted mode. An advisory is a recommendation rather than an enforceable rule, so it carries no compliance date. Why it matters: low-speed and yard collisions can generate impact damage to couplers, draft gear and truck components, and the underlying restricted-speed regulations remain enforceable now even though the advisory itself adds no new duty.
US rail traffic runs 4.1% ahead of last year in the week ending August 29, led by intermodal
Association of American Railroads — weekly rail traffic report · Sep 2 · association data
US railroads moved 543,212 carloads and intermodal units, up 4.1% year over year, with carloads at 240,021 (+2.2%) and intermodal at 303,191 (+5.7%); North American volume across the US, Canada and Mexico was 741,608 units, up 3.9%. Metallic ores and metals (+2,449 carloads), grain (+1,644) and nonmetallic minerals (+1,219) led the gainers while chemicals (−1,322) and motor vehicles and parts (−1,049) fell, and year to date through 34 weeks combined US volume is up 3.3%. Why it matters: AAR counts originations rather than car-miles and intermodal units are containers rather than cars, so read this as a favourable but partial utilisation signal — one that would reach mileage-driven wheel, bearing and brake demand in the affected fleets well before it reached new-build orders.

Union Pacific and CSX each petition FRA for relief from qualified mechanical inspector requirements
Federal Register — FRA notices of petition for waiver of compliance (UP 2026-17869, CSX 2026-17870) · Sep 1 · customers · mechanical inspection
Both railroads have petitioned FRA for relief from certain regulations concerning inspections performed by qualified mechanical inspectors, and FRA has published each petition for public comment. The notices identify the subject of the relief sought but no decision has been made and no terms have been granted. Why it matters: QMI rules govern who is qualified to perform freight car mechanical inspections, so on the face of the notices the outcome bears on who inspects rather than on how often — and inspection practice is one of the inputs to running-repair component demand.
CSX adds 42 powered switches at Queensgate yard in Cincinnati
Progressive Railroading · Sep 2 · customer · terminal capacity
The installation takes the Cincinnati hump yard to roughly 170 remote-controlled switches, allowing routes to be lined without employees working in the field. CSX disclosed no investment figure or completion date, and its director of hump yard operations, engineering and design, Ryan Raymond, framed the work around crew safety and improving the operation. Why it matters: this is yard-productivity spending rather than rolling-stock spending, and because coupling impact is governed by retarders and process control rather than by switch machines, any read-across to draft gear or end-of-car damage would be speculative.
Norfolk Southern completes multi-year capacity work on its Alabama corridor to the Port of Mobile
Progressive Railroading · Sep 2 · customer · infrastructure
Work begun in 2024 on the 3B Corridor linking northern and central Alabama to Mobile added a siding at Wilton, extended the Lacey siding in Shelby County to reduce blocked crossings, extended four further sidings across Shelby, Wilcox and Clarke counties, and installed centralized traffic control. No investment figure was disclosed; International Paper, which has three mills and several converting plants on the corridor, said the improvements support its operations. Why it matters: added siding capacity and CTC raise how much traffic the corridor can carry to an export gateway and should support service reliability, though the disclosed facts establish potential rather than committed forest-products volume.

Chemical carloads swing from a year-on-year gain to a loss in consecutive weeks
Association of American Railroads — weekly reports for the weeks ending Aug 22 and Aug 29 · Aug 26 and Sep 2 · chemicals · association data
Chemicals ran 1,923 carloads above the prior year in the week ending August 22, at 33,850, then finished 1,322 carloads below the prior year in the week ending August 29, at 33,640. Both are single weeks measured against single year-earlier weeks, and the Labor Day holiday falls inside the next comparison period. Why it matters: chemicals is an AAR commodity classification rather than a car type, though much of it moves in tank cars, and a reversal of this size in consecutive weeks is more likely comparison noise than a turn in underlying demand — worth watching rather than acting on.

MxV Rail selects Rail Vision for locomotive sensor testing tied to restricted speed enforcement
Rail Vision Ltd. — company announcement via GlobeNewswire · Sep 3 · association research · automation
The company has been selected for MxV Rail's locomotive sensor pre-integration testing programme under the TCCO Technology Roadmap, moving from the roadmap stage it entered in December 2024 to evaluation with Class I railroads; MxV Rail is the AAR's research and testing subsidiary. No financial terms were disclosed, and this is a testing selection rather than a purchase or a deployment. Why it matters: the stated purpose is restricted speed enforcement, the same subject as today's FRA advisory, though this is locomotive sensing rather than freight car content — its interest to us is that effective enforcement would reduce the collision damage behind some component replacement.
Railway Association of Canada asks Ottawa to extend 100% immediate depreciation to transportation and warehousing
Progressive Railroading, reporting the association and its consultant Giroux Strategies · Sep 2 · association · Canada · tax policy
The association cites analysis estimating the measure would generate close to C$900 million in GDP a year and attract close to C$600 million in additional private investment, returning C$1.79 of investment and C$2.70 of annual GDP per C$1 of net federal fiscal cost, at a total cost below 0.1% of federal tax revenues. RAC argues transportation and warehousing is the only major Canadian sector carrying a higher marginal effective tax rate than its US counterpart; these are the association's and its consultant's figures, and this is advocacy rather than enacted policy. Why it matters: immediate expensing lowers the after-tax cost of rolling stock and terminal assets, so if it were adopted it would modestly improve the economics of Canadian railcar purchases.
MxV Rail sets out the metallurgy lab it uses to trace steel component failures
MxV Rail — AAR research subsidiary · Sep 1 · association research · materials
The lab examines steel components from track or equipment from nanoscale to macroscale, using a scanning electron microscope, energy dispersive and optical emission spectrometry, Rockwell, Vickers and Brinell hardness testing, and ultrasonic, eddy current, magnetic particle and liquid penetrant inspection. Principal investigator Ananyo Banerjee framed the work around rail welds and why they break, and the described caseload covers rail welds, wheels and the wheel–rail interface. Why it matters: MxV research can inform AAR technical committees and eventual standards, so failure modes characterised here may surface later as specification changes for wheels and castings, though a laboratory capability note signals no specific change under consideration.
Regulatory & Policy 3 stories
FRA amends brake system maintenance and inspection standards, codifying longstanding waivers
Federal Register — FRA final rule 2026-17784 · published Aug 31, effective Sep 30 · regulatory · brakes
The rule amends mechanical equipment safety standards for brake inspections covering both passenger and freight equipment, and writes long-running locomotive brake system maintenance and inspection waivers into the regulations themselves. FRA ties the action to the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act mandate to review whether such waivers are justified for codification, and to Executive Order 14219 on deregulation. Why it matters: codifying relief that already operated by waiver largely formalises existing practice, so the near-term effect on brake component replacement volumes should be limited.
FRA widens the permitted diameter variation for AC-traction locomotive wheel sets
Federal Register — FRA final rule 2026-17788 · published Aug 31, effective Sep 30 · regulatory · wheels
The rule expands the maximum permitted variation in wheel set diameter for locomotives using alternating current traction, responding to a Class I railroad's May 2019 petition for rulemaking and to innovations in traction motor control. It applies to locomotive wheel sets and does not change freight car wheel standards. Why it matters: the first-order effect is less premature wheel matching or truing on locomotives, and whether that reduces new locomotive wheel purchases depends on condemnable limits and wheel life; the freight car wheel market is untouched either way.
FHWA, FRA and FTA finalise their shared environmental review regulations
Federal Register — joint final rule 2026-17904 · published Sep 1, effective on publication · regulatory · permitting
The three agencies finalised their July 2025 interim final rule with minor technical changes, revising their National Environmental Policy Act regulations to reflect the removal of the Council on Environmental Quality's rules, the BUILDER Act amendments, the review provisions of the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act and the Supreme Court's Seven County Infrastructure Coalition decision. Why it matters: this governs environmental review for rail projects requiring federal action, not only federally funded ones, so it bears on the pace at which infrastructure work can proceed rather than on equipment purchasing.

InnoTrans / Messe Berlin — organiser materials · dates confirmed Sep 3 · Europe · trade show
The biennial fair runs September 22 to 25 at Messe Berlin, with exhibitors from around 60 countries and an outdoor display of roughly 3,500 metres of track carrying everything from tank wagons upward.
