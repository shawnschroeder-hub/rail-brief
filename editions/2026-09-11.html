President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. CN has set out what it wants from the UP–NS merger, and it is Midwest track. On September 9 CN filed the conditions it intends to seek: preserved competitive access at a list of Illinois and Iowa points, trackage rights between Kansas City and St. Louis, and a lease of UP's Neff Yard in Kansas City. The filing follows a July memorandum of understanding with UP on remedies, so it reads as a negotiated position rather than opposition. Nothing is decided — notices of intent to participate are due September 30 and comments November 18 — but conditions of this shape could change which carrier serves which yards across our home region, and with it where component routing and repair demand sit.
  2. Demand is solid but not accelerating, and this week's headline overstates it. AAR's week ending September 5 shows US volume up 13.8% year over year, with intermodal up 18% and metallic ores and metals up 26.6%. But the year-to-date pace is 3.6%, and a gap that wide between one week and the 35-week pace suggests a calendar or base effect rather than a step change; the Port of Los Angeles tells the same story, with a record summer of 2.9 million TEUs yet only 1.5% growth for the year. For us that supports utilisation-driven wear on fleets already in service — it is not yet the signal that would justify reading a new-build cycle into the data.
  3. Hamilton would be at day 31, and the record is still silent. The National Steel Car stoppage by roughly 1,200 USW Local 7135 members began August 12, and no source we could read today reports a settlement, a revised offer or even a scheduled bargaining date. The only account refreshed since August 31 is a partisan labour publication arguing for abolition of the plant's piecework incentive scheme, which tells us about advocacy rather than negotiations. That silence is not evidence in either direction, which is why our own call-offs and shipment timing into Hamilton remain the most direct read we have on whether the plant is running.

CN files the conditions it will seek in the UP–NS merger, centred on Illinois, Iowa and Kansas City
Progressive Railroading · published today, Sep 11, on a Sep 9 filing · merger · customers
CN filed a description of anticipated conditions asking the Surface Transportation Board to preserve competitive access at nine Illinois points including Hillsboro, Carlinville, Bloomington, Granite City and Danville, and at Iowa locations where it says the merger would reduce Class I competition. CN also proposes trackage rights between Kansas City and St. Louis and a lease of UP's Neff Yard in Kansas City, and Olivier Chouc, CN's senior vice president and chief legal officer, said the conditions "would preserve competitive access in key Midwest markets, expand CN's reach and create additional opportunities to grow with our customers" — the filing follows a July memorandum of understanding between CN and UP on remedies for the $85 billion transaction. Why it matters: conditions of this kind reshape which carrier serves which yards and shops after a merger, so Midwest component routing and the location of repair demand could shift, though this is a request rather than a Board decision and the proceeding runs to final briefs in May 2027.
UP tests a Powder River Basin coal export route through Mexico's Port of Guaymas
Progressive Railroading · published today, Sep 11 · customers · Mexico · export
Union Pacific said it has completed multiple test trains and loaded a vessel in a trial moving coal from Peabody's North Antelope mine in Wyoming over UP to Nogales, Arizona, then via Ferromex to the Port of Guaymas, with the first shipment destined for Vietnam. UP described the work as a feasibility exercise with Peabody, Ferromex, port operators and US and Mexican government stakeholders, and published no tonnage figures. Why it matters: a committed export lane would put loaded unit-train mileage onto the Nogales–Guaymas corridor inside our Mexican footprint, with wheel, bearing and brake wear accruing across the full Wyoming-to-port cycle — but this is a test rather than a booked service, and US coal carloads were still down 5.8% year over year in the latest AAR week.
US rail volume up 13.8% in week 35, but the year-to-date pace holds at 3.6%
Progressive Railroading, reporting AAR data · Sep 10, for the week ending Sep 5 · traffic data
US railroads moved 533,545 combined carloads and intermodal units, with carloads at 234,397 (up 8.9%) and intermodal at 299,148 (up 18%). Ten of eleven commodity groups rose — metallic ores and metals up 26.6% to 23,122 carloads, grain up 24.1% to 22,509 and chemicals up 12.9% to 33,653 — while coal fell 5.8% to 58,037; through 35 weeks US volume stands at 17,890,653 units, up 3.6%, with Canada up 1.5% and Mexico up 7.7%. Why it matters: the 13.8% weekly jump sits far above the year-to-date pace, which suggests a calendar or base effect in the comparison week rather than a step change in demand, and steady utilisation of existing fleets is a wear-and-repair signal rather than a new-build one.

World Socialist Web Site, a partisan labour publication — the most recent account we could open, dated Sep 10 · competitor · labour · standing watch item
Counting from the August 12 walkout by roughly 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members over wages and a piecework incentive scheme, the Hamilton, Ontario stoppage would be at day 31 today, and the September 10 account argues for abolishing that incentive scheme rather than reporting on negotiations. No source we could read gives a settlement, a revised offer or a scheduled bargaining date, and mainstream coverage has not been refreshed since August 31. Norfolk Southern opens a daily intermodal lane between Huntsville and the Port of Charleston
Progressive Railroading · published today, Sep 11 · customers · intermodal
The new daily direct service links Huntsville, Alabama with Charleston, South Carolina, extending rail access for shippers in northern Alabama and middle Tennessee seeking southeast markets and the port. South Carolina Ports president and chief executive Micah Mallace called the lane "a meaningful addition for our customers," and no start date, volume or train-size figures appeared in the coverage we could read. Why it matters: new daily intermodal lanes typically add cycles to well cars and containers already in service rather than creating new-build demand, so the near-term read for us is incremental running-repair exposure rather than orders.
Shipper Industries & Demand 1 story
Port of Los Angeles posts a record summer at 2.9 million TEUs, with loaded exports the weak segment
Progressive Railroading · published today, Sep 11, on August data · intermodal · trade
The port handled 955,907 TEUs in August and 2.9 million across June to August, with loaded imports of 500,302 TEUs roughly level with a year earlier, loaded exports down 9% to 115,561 and empties up 4% to 340,044. Through eight months it stands at just over 7 million TEUs, up 1.5% on 2025 and 6% above its five-year average, and executive director Gene Seroka said there is "good momentum heading into the final months of the year." Why it matters: import volume at San Pedro Bay feeds the intermodal traffic now growing at roughly double the carload rate and supports utilisation of existing well-car fleets, though a 9% loaded-export decline is the weaker segment and one port is not the national trade position.
Regulatory & Policy 1 story
102 House Democrats press the STB to weigh rail jobs first in the UP–NS review
FreightWaves · Sep 3 letter · merger · labour · policy
One hundred and two House Democrats, led by Representative Valerie Hoyle of Oregon and coordinated with the Teamsters Rail Conference, urged the Surface Transportation Board to "fully evaluate what this merger could mean for jobs, safety and accountability" before approving the $85 billion transaction. The national president of the Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers and Trainmen, who also heads the Teamsters Rail Conference, has said UP's offer of "lifetime employment" for union members does not fully protect some positions. Why it matters: a legislators' letter is advocacy rather than a procedural step and does not itself change the Board's schedule, but labour conditions are among the more common merger outcomes and would bear on shop headcount and maintenance practice at two of our largest customers.

Knorr-Bremse newsroom, a company announcement · for InnoTrans, Berlin, Sep 22–25 · competitor · global
The company says it will appear in Berlin for the first time positioned as an integrated Tier 1 systems partner for the rail ecosystem, showing braking systems, digitally networked subsystems, energy-efficiency technologies and infrastructure products as a combined offer. Separately, Knorr-Bremse Rail Systems and Chiltern Railways have extended a low-adhesion monitoring trial by a further twelve months after what they describe as a successful first year of data collection. Why it matters: a component maker repositioning as a systems integrator bids for wider scopes of supply and is worth watching on that basis, though this is company positioning ahead of a trade fair, and its New York Air Brake arm is the more direct North American freight exposure to watch.
