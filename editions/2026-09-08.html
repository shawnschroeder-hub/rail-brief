President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Opposition to the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern transaction keeps broadening, though the Board's substantive calendar has not moved. Two more rail unions, TCU/IAM and IAM District 19, joined the Stop the Rail Merger Coalition on September 4, a group that already contains two Class I competitors in BNSF and CPKC. Separately, the Board extended the deadline for notices of intent to participate to September 30 after the National League of Cities said roughly 12,000 local governments with rail crossings had not yet decided whether to take part. Every other date held, so the read for us is duration rather than outcome: the record stays open well into 2027, which prolongs planning uncertainty at both railroads even though nothing yet shows it changing their capital spending.
  2. A second builder has now pointed at 2027, rather than 2026, as the year that matters. FreightCar America's second-quarter release cut full-year guidance to 3,500–3,900 cars, a 10.3% decline, because a late production ramp pushed deliveries into early 2027 — even as its backlog rose 121% sequentially to $344 million. That sits alongside the TrinityRail outlook carried Friday, which put industry output stepping up to 30,000–35,000 cars in 2027. These are two companies' own figures on different bases — one a production-timing shift, the other an industry outlook — so neither corroborates the other, but both make 2027 the year to test our capacity assumptions against.
  3. Hamilton is four weeks out with no settlement or renewed bargaining, and the long weekend produced nothing new. The National Steel Car stoppage reached day 28 today; an Ontario Ministry of Labour mediator is assigned and the union says it remains available to talk. One caution: searching this dispute surfaces an article about a deal ending a 41-day strike, but that is dated August 2023 and describes the previous dispute, not this one. The practical instruction stands — our own call-offs and shipment schedules into Hamilton will say more about plant status than the press currently can.
Top Stories 3 stories
Two more rail unions join the coalition opposing the UP–NS merger
Progressive Railroading · Sep 4 · regulatory · labour
TCU/IAM and IAM District 19 joined the Stop the Rail Merger Coalition, which already brings together rail labour organisations with two Class I competitors, BNSF and CPKC, against Union Pacific's proposed acquisition of Norfolk Southern. IAM District 19 president Reece Murtagh said the unions want binding protections against furloughs, abolished positions, relocations and contracting out; the report says the two represent "thousands of rail workers" without giving a figure. Why it matters: more unions behind one coalition strengthens the constituency pressing for binding labour conditions, which bears on what protections the Board may be asked to impose rather than on whether the merger succeeds.
STB pushes the UP–NS participation deadline from September 4 to September 30
Progressive Railroading, reporting an STB decision of Aug 31 · Sep 2 · regulatory · merger
The Board granted a National League of Cities request to extend the deadline for filing notices of intent to participate, after the League said roughly 12,000 local governments with rail crossings had not yet determined whether to take part and that the original date fell less than 30 days after the schedule was released and over Labor Day weekend. All other dates in the August 18 procedural schedule stand, leaving comments, protests and requests for conditions due November 18, responses and rebuttal due February 16, 2027, and final briefs due May 28, 2027. Why it matters: this moves only the notice-of-intent deadline and not the Board's own timetable, and with final briefs not due until May 28, 2027 the applicants' mid-2027 completion target remains theirs rather than the Board's.
Greenbrier names Brian Comstock to succeed Lorie Tekorius as chief executive
Greenbrier press release · Aug 31 · competitor · leadership
Tekorius, chief executive and president and a Greenbrier employee since 1995, will retire effective January 6, 2027, with Comstock — currently executive vice president and president of The Americas, with nearly 30 years at the company — succeeding her that day and taking her board seat. Tekorius will provide transition services after departure and will not stand for re-election at the 2027 annual meeting. Why it matters: a chief executive change at one of the two largest North American car builders can shift commercial posture and sourcing priorities over time, though an internal successor named four months ahead points to a planned handover rather than a break.

National Steel Car strike reaches day 28 with no settlement in any source we could read
CBC on the dispute and the Ontario Ministry of Labour on mediation; most recent reporting we could open is dated Aug 31 · competitor · labour · standing watch item
Roughly 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members have been out at the Hamilton, Ontario plant since August 12 over wages and an incentive scheme paying an extra 25% of base wage at production thresholds, and nothing published across the long weekend establishes a settlement, a revised offer or a scheduled bargaining date. A search result headline describing a new deal ending a 41-day strike refers to the separate 2023 dispute — that article is dated August 2023. Why it matters: a builder four weeks into a stoppage is likely consuming far fewer production components, so our exposure is best read from our own call-offs and shipment timing into Hamilton rather than from the press.
Knorr-Bremse puts wayside monitoring at the centre of its InnoTrans line-up
Knorr-Bremse newsroom · Sep 8 · competitor · product
The company said today it will present wayside solutions at InnoTrans in Berlin, framing strong fleets, robust networks and upgrades to existing lines as the priorities for rail's next phase. Why it matters: wayside detection sits adjacent to our catalogue rather than inside it, but systems that measure bearing, wheel and brake condition in service inform when operators inspect and remove those components.

CSX reports a 24% drop in injury rate and best-in-class fuel efficiency for 2025
Progressive Railroading, reporting CSX's 2025 sustainability report · Sep 4 · customers · company-reported
CSX reports cutting its FRA personal-injury frequency rate 24% and its train accident rate 13% year over year, and claims the best fuel efficiency among US Class Is at 0.968 gallons of diesel per thousand gross ton-miles. The report also cites the Howard Street Tunnel completed ahead of schedule, the Blue Ridge Subdivision restored, and continued locomotive modernisation and safety-technology investment. Why it matters: the injury and accident rates follow FRA reporting definitions while the peer-leading fuel claim is CSX's own, and none of these is a useful proxy for our demand, which tracks wear, mileage and bad-order rates rather than accident counts.

FRA's repeal of outdated railroad workplace safety requirements takes effect September 30
Federal Register — FRA final rule · published Aug 31, effective Sep 30 · regulatory · final rule
The final rule removes specific roadway worker provisions the agency judges obsolete — the broader roadway and bridge worker safety regime stays in force — and adds a special-approval procedure for alternative bridge worker safety approaches, one of six FRA final rules published that day. Why it matters: this sits on the maintenance-of-way side rather than on car components, so the direct read for our catalogue is limited — it matters mainly as further evidence of how far the agency's current deregulatory package reaches.
Investor Relations & Earnings 1 story
FreightCar America's backlog jumps 121% while its 2026 delivery guidance falls
FreightCar America second-quarter release, filed with the SEC · Aug 3 · competitor · earnings
For the quarter ended June 30 the company reports a backlog of 3,972 units valued at $344 million, up 121% sequentially, on deliveries of 927 units and revenue of $113.1 million against $118.6 million a year earlier. It puts its share of industry new-railcar orders at approximately 45% — the company's own estimate, not RSI-ARCI data — and cut full-year guidance to 3,500–3,900 cars, down 10.3% year over year, as a later-than-planned production ramp pushed part of 2026's deliveries into early 2027. Why it matters: components are consumed during build rather than at car delivery, so a slipped delivery year moves some call-offs but not all — our own schedules into this builder will show how much, and the 2027 build plan matters more than the guidance cut itself.
