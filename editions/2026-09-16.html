President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Hamilton is at day 36 with no talks scheduled. CHCH News reported this morning that the USW Local 7135 strike at National Steel Car is in its sixth week, that no new negotiations are planned, and that the company has not responded to repeated requests for comment. The union president framed the dispute around the plant's output-linked incentive pay as well as wages. The 2023 stoppage ran 41 days; with no talks scheduled, our own call-offs into Hamilton remain the practical read on near-term shipment requirements there, which is not the same as underlying railcar demand.
  2. The UP–NS applicants are building a public record of political support ahead of the September 30 notice-of-intent deadline. Union Pacific said South Carolina Governor Henry McMaster wrote to the Surface Transportation Board backing the combination, and put total support at more than 2,000 stakeholders nationwide. The Board's formal status is unchanged: it adopted a procedural schedule on August 18, and notices of intent to participate are due September 30. Support letters go into the record, but they are not a Board finding, and September 30 is when parties must say they intend to take part.
  3. Savannah's container volume is flat, and BNSF keeps signing tenants for its planned California inland port. Savannah handled 529,523 TEUs in August, essentially level with a year ago, though the Georgia Ports Authority's July–August total was up 2.2%. BNSF separately signed its second logistics tenant in a month, Spearpoint Logistics, for the planned Barstow International Gateway. These are separate signals and neither is a rail traffic figure: one shows a major gateway holding steady, the other a railroad building a long-lead bid for more inland freight.

South Carolina's governor writes to the STB in support of the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger
Union Pacific release via PR Newswire; filing deadline from the STB merger page · Sep 15 · customers · UP–NS merger · standing watch item
Union Pacific said Governor Henry McMaster wrote to the Board that the combination "presents meaningful opportunities" for South Carolina, and the release repeated the applicants' own estimate of 2.1 million truckloads moved off highways and claimed support from more than 2,000 stakeholders nationwide. The Board adopted a procedural schedule on August 18 and has extended the deadline for notices of intent to participate to September 30. Why it matters: support letters add to the record but are not a Board finding, and September 30 is when parties must say they intend to take part.
BNSF signs Spearpoint Logistics as a tenant at its planned Barstow International Gateway
BNSF news release · Sep 15 · customers · intermodal
BNSF said Spearpoint will set up warehousing, brokerage, heavyweight intermodal and cross-dock operations for freight from Asia and Mexico at the master-planned Southern California facility, following a similar agreement with NFI announced on August 17. The release gave no capacity, acreage or opening-date figures. Why it matters: BNSF is lining up logistics tenants to feed an inland rail hub linked to the Southern California ports, a bid for more import traffic that is still a plan rather than new volume.

National Steel Car strike reaches week six with no new negotiations planned
CHCH News · Sep 16 · competitor · labour · standing watch item
About 1,200 members of United Steelworkers Local 7135 have been out since August 12, and CHCH reported that as of this morning, day 36, no new negotiations were planned and the company had not responded to repeated requests for comment. Local president Frank Crowder said the union wants changes to the plant's incentive program, which pays 25% above base wage once an output threshold is met, on both safety and wage grounds. Why it matters: the dispute turns on how the plant pays for output, not only on the size of a raise, and no bargaining date is set.

CN and CPKC together supply 91% of grain car orders in week 5 of the crop year
Ag Transport Coalition data as reported by GX94 Radio · Sep 14 · customers · Canada · grain
The coalition reported that the two railways combined supplied 91% of the grain cars shippers ordered in week 5, up from 90% the week before, with CPKC at 93% (its eighteenth straight week at 90% or better) and CN at 86%, down from 89%. Why it matters: this is one week's fill rate, with CN trailing CPKC and the new-crop peak still ahead.
Shipper Industries & Demand 1 story
Savannah's August container volume flat as Brunswick auto and machinery units fall 5%
Georgia Ports Authority figures as reported by Progressive Railroading · Sep 15 · intermodal · automotive · demand
The authority moved 1.03 million TEUs across July and August, up 2.2%, while Savannah's August volume of 529,523 TEUs was essentially flat, with loaded imports up 1.5% and exports up 4.2%. Brunswick handled 60,572 auto and heavy-machinery units in August, down 5%, as heavy-machinery volume rose nearly 22% but U.S. auto exports declined, which the report tied to competition from Chinese producers. Why it matters: these are port counts with no rail split given, so softer vehicle exports through Brunswick are at most a watch item for export autorack traffic.

Italy's state freight group orders eight bi-mode locomotives from Stadler
RailFreight.com · Sep 16 · global · Europe · locomotives
Mercitalia Shunting & Terminal, part of FS Logistix, ordered eight EUROLIGHT Dual electric-diesel locomotives with a 20-tonne axle load for use on lighter Italian lines, part of an investment programme the group puts at about €1 billion; no contract value or delivery date was given. Why it matters: this is locomotive rather than freight-car spending and does not touch our products directly, but it shows a European state freight operator continuing to fund fleet renewal.
