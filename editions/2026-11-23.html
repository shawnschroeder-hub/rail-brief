Tentative union deal averts strike at Port of LA-Long Beach
FreightWaves; also reported by Reuters via gCaptain · Nov 21 · ports · labor
The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26 reached a tentative agreement on Saturday, November 21, covering about 250 watchmen at the Los Angeles and Long Beach ports, with the existing contract extended pending a ratification vote by Local 26 members. No terms or ratification timeline were disclosed; before the deal, PMA said its offer included a $3,200 one-time bonus and would lift average full-time watchman earnings from more than $170,000 to $198,000. Why it matters: the strike threat to import boxes feeding western intermodal trains is lifted for now, but it is not closed until members ratify.

Wabtec Corporation Appoints Rich J. Freeland to the Board of Directors
Wabtec · Nov 20 · governance · company release
Wabtec named Rich J Freeland, former President and Chief Operating Officer of Cummins, to its board effective Monday, November 23, according to the company. Freeland, 69, held that Cummins role from 2014 until he retired in 2019 and now chairs the board of Valvoline. Asia–US East Coast ocean freight rates top $10,000, up 300% from a year ago
FreightWaves, citing Xeneta · Nov 23 · intermodal · ocean rates
Xeneta data put Far East–US East Coast spot rates at $10,901 per FEU and Far East–US West Coast at $8,203 for the week ending November 19, each down about 2% on the week but up 297.8% and 304.3% year over year. Offered capacity into both coasts rose about 11% from October 19, and Xeneta chief analyst Peter Sand said the return of container services to the Red Sea had cooled in November. Why it matters: rates this high can shift some import routings between coasts and the inland intermodal mix, though spot pricing alone does not show where volume will land.

AAR Statement on Senate Passage of the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026
AAR · Nov 19 · policy · trade-body statement
The AAR welcomed Senate passage of the permitting-reform bill, with president and chief executive Ian Jefferies saying it "can help make project reviews timelier and more predictable while preserving important environmental protections." The statement urged the House to advance the bill and named no rail-specific provision. Why it matters: the bill still needs House action, and any benefit would come mainly through quicker track and terminal projects, not equipment demand.
