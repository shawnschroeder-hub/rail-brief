President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Three Class I railroads reported higher third-quarter volume, but fuel surcharges drove much of the revenue growth. Union Pacific's revenue carloads rose 6%, CSX's total volume rose 5% and Norfolk Southern's volume rose 3%. Norfolk Southern said fuel surcharges accounted for about eight points of its 14% revenue gain, and Union Pacific's freight revenue excluding fuel surcharge rose 6% against a 15% rise in total revenue. Traffic and revenue at railroads are not a measure of railcar orders or component demand.
  2. Union Pacific set its 2026 capital plan at $3.5 billion, part of which buys out existing leased equipment. Its year-to-date capital investments of $2.834 billion include $320 million for early locomotive and freight car lease buyouts, which change who owns cars rather than adding new ones. CEO Jim Vena said the company looks forward to the next steps in the STB's merger review. The STB's latest-news page shows no merger action after its September 18 denial of motions seeking summary denial.
  3. Competitor news was quiet today, and several supplier and lessor results arrive over the next two weeks. Greenbrier reports on October 26, GATX before the market opens on October 29, CN before the market opens on October 30 and Timken on November 4; Trinity Industries had not yet reported third-quarter results. The National Steel Car strike that began August 12 has not been reported settled.

Union Pacific Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results
Union Pacific (SEC Form 8-K press release) · Oct 22 · Union Pacific · third-quarter results
Union Pacific reported third-quarter operating revenue of $7.2 billion, up 15%, while freight revenue excluding fuel surcharge rose 6% and revenue carloads rose 6%; diluted EPS was $3.49 (adjusted $3.53) and the operating ratio was 59.1% (adjusted 58.8%). The company updated its 2026 capital plan to $3.5 billion, and its year-to-date capital investments of $2.834 billion include $320 million for early locomotive and freight car lease buyouts. Why it matters: lease buyouts move existing cars onto the railroad's own books rather than adding new ones, so the capital figure should not be read as new-car demand.
Norfolk Southern reports third quarter 2026 results
Norfolk Southern (SEC Form 8-K press release) · Oct 22 · Norfolk Southern · third-quarter results
Norfolk Southern reported record third-quarter railway operating revenue of $3.5 billion, up 14%, with higher fuel surcharges accounting for about eight points of that growth and volume up 3%. Diluted EPS was $3.37 (adjusted $3.62), and the operating ratio was 67.4%, or 65.4% adjusted, which is 210 basis points worse than a year earlier; the adjusted figures exclude merger-related costs and effects of the Eastern Ohio incident. Why it matters: most of the revenue gain came from fuel surcharges rather than volume, and the company cited rising fuel costs as a headwind.

CSX Corporation third quarter 2026 results
CSX (SEC Form 8-K press release) · Oct 21 · CSX · third-quarter results
CSX reported record third-quarter revenue of $4.02 billion, up 12%, on total volume of 1.69 million units (up 5%, with intermodal up 7%), and diluted EPS of $0.57, up 30% from an adjusted $0.44 a year earlier. Operating income rose 44% to $1.57 billion for a 39.1% operating margin, which the company attributed to higher fuel surcharge revenue plus volume and pricing gains in merchandise, intermodal and coal. Why it matters: CSX gave no numerical outlook, so the results describe one quarter's traffic and pricing rather than its equipment spending plans.

ASLRRA secures $5.6M CRISI grant for Short Line Training Center
Progressive Railroading · Oct 21 · ASLRRA · federal workforce-training grant
The American Short Line and Regional Railroad Association received $5.6 million in FRA CRISI funding for a $7.5 million project, including a 25% ASLRRA match, to expand the Short Line Training Center first funded by a 2020 CRISI grant to the Iowa Northern Railway. Planned work includes at least 24 computer-based training programs, up to 48 in-person seminars and new technology. Why it matters: the grant funds short-line workforce training rather than track or equipment.

New York short line wins $11.2 million federal rail grant
FreightWaves · Oct 21 · Ontario Midland Railroad · FRA CRISI award
Ontario Midland Railroad, which serves Wayne and Monroe counties in upstate New York, received $11.2 million in CRISI funding to rehabilitate about 10 miles of track, replace about 9 miles of rail, resurface 8 grade crossings and replace 7 turnouts. The award is part of $20.8 million in combined CRISI funding with the Falls Road Railroad, which received about $9.6 million to rehabilitate roughly 40 miles between Brockport and Lockport. Why it matters: it is track work on two short lines rather than rolling-stock purchasing.
