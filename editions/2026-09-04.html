President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The clearest forward signal this week came from a competitor's commercial chief, not from a data series. TrinityRail's Charley Moore puts roughly 200,000 North American cars near end of life and forecasts industry output rising from about 25,000 cars this year to 30,000–35,000 in 2027. He names tariff uncertainty and higher steel input costs as what is holding current build rates below that level. Treat these as one builder's estimates rather than RSI-ARCI data, and note that cars approaching end of life do not convert one-for-one into replacements — but a forecast step up of 20–40% in build rates is worth testing against our own order book.
  2. Canadian grain has opened the crop year at record strength on both railroads. CN moved 2.5 million tonnes of Western Canadian grain in August, while CPKC moved 2.54 million tonnes and 26,051 carloads of grain and grain products across the first four weeks of the crop year, each beating a record set in August 2020. The two cover different periods and different commodity scopes, and record originations show throughput rather than fleet utilisation. The near-term read for us is mileage and load-cycle wear on covered hoppers rather than new orders, though sustained strength could eventually support car buying.
  3. No new public development on Hamilton, and the silence is itself worth noting. The National Steel Car stoppage would be at day 24 if still out, with a mediator assigned, the union saying it is willing to talk, and no readable report of a settlement, a revised offer or even a scheduled bargaining date. The most recent coverage we could open is dated August 31, so we are four days without a fresh read on the dispute. That argues for going direct: our own call-offs and shipment schedules into Hamilton will say more about plant status than the press currently does.

TrinityRail's commercial chief puts about 200,000 North American cars near end of life, with output stepping up in 2027
Railway Supply, reporting TrinityRail chief commercial officer Charley Moore · Sep 3 · competitor · company estimate
Moore estimates roughly 200,000 cars are approaching end of life across North America, and projects industry output of about 25,000 railcars in 2026 rising to 30,000–35,000 in 2027. He attributes current build rates running below that level to tariff uncertainty and higher steel input costs delaying customers' capital decisions, and says TrinityRail is working to offset manufacturing pressure through automation, changes in domestic sourcing and supplier negotiations. Why it matters: these are one builder's estimates rather than RSI-ARCI industry data, and a 20–40% step up would lift industry-wide new-build component demand though our share depends on car mix and awarded content; the brake he names, steel cost and tariff uncertainty, may bear on our own inputs too.
CN and CPKC both set August grain records to open the 2026-27 crop year
Progressive Railroading, reporting both railroads · Sep 3 · customers · grain
CN moved 2.5 million metric tonnes of Western Canadian grain in August, beating its previous monthly record of 2.34 MMT set in August 2020, while CPKC moved 2.54 MMT and 26,051 carloads of grain and grain products across the first four weeks of the crop year that began August 1, also beating an August 2020 record. The two figures are not directly comparable — CN reports Western Canadian grain tonnage, CPKC reports tonnes and carloads for grain and grain products — and each is a record for its own period rather than a sustained run rate. Why it matters: a strong opening to the crop year points to heavy covered hopper activity, which is a wear-parts and running-repair signal, though record originations measure throughput and do not by themselves establish fleet-wide utilisation.
National Steel Car strike reaches day 24 with no settlement reported in any source we could read
CBC and CHCH on the dispute and the Ontario Ministry of Labour on mediation; the most recent reporting we could open is dated Aug 31 · competitor · labour · standing watch item
Roughly 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members have been out at the Hamilton, Ontario plant since August 12, after a vote with about 91% turnout went nearly 80% against the company's offer, over wages and the incentive scheme paying an extra 25% of base wage at production thresholds. The Ministry of Labour has said a mediator is assigned and remains available and the union says it is willing to resume talks, but nothing we could open establishes a settlement, a revised offer or a scheduled bargaining date. Why it matters: a builder in its fourth week of stoppage is likely consuming sharply fewer production components, so the exposure to watch remains our own call-offs, shipment timing and inventory into Hamilton.

Belt Railway files a fourth Clearing Yard report with the STB, the last three on a Wednesday cadence
Surface Transportation Board — Belt Railway of Chicago data reporting page · filing dated Sep 2 · Chicago congestion · standing watch item
The Belt Railway filed a weekly report on September 2 covering August 24–30, following filings on August 11, 19 and 26, under the Board's August 3 request for an operations update and weekly metrics on car volumes and dwell at Clearing Yard. The underlying figures are posted as spreadsheet attachments we could not open, so we carry the filing itself rather than any dwell or inventory number from it. STB publishes a control exemption for Henry Posner III over the Morristown & Erie Railway
Federal Register — Surface Transportation Board notice · published today, Sep 4 · regulatory · short line
The Board published a notice of exemption for Henry Posner III to acquire control of the Morristown & Erie Railway, a New Jersey short line. Publication starts an exemption process rather than completing a control change — it remains subject to the notice's stated effective date and to any stay or revocation request — and the Federal Register entry carries no abstract, so the terms sit in the filing itself. Why it matters: short line control changes rarely move component volumes on their own, but they can change who sets maintenance and capital priorities on that property.
Coast Guard removes the operating regulation for a CSX Savannah River bridge converted to a fixed span
Federal Register — Coast Guard final rule · published today, Sep 4 · regulatory · infrastructure
The CSX Transportation railroad bridge at mile 60.9 near Clyo, Georgia was converted to a fixed bridge in August 2026, so the drawbridge operating regulation no longer applies and is being removed from the rules. Why it matters: this is housekeeping rather than policy — any operating benefit came from the August conversion itself, which removed an opening-related delay point on a CSX route toward Savannah, not from deleting the obsolete rule.
