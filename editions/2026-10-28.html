Top Stories 1 story
Backlog grows as 35,000 containers await rail service at LA–Long Beach
FreightWaves · Oct 28 · trade press · port rail
As of October 26, 35,688 import containers were waiting for on-dock trains at the two ports (19,626 at Los Angeles, 16,062 at Long Beach), according to a compilation of port rail reports, while Pacific Merchant Shipping Association data put average rail-container dwell at 7.19 days in September as both ports set monthly volume records. Logistics provider CH Robinson cited limited Southern California intermodal railcar availability, and the Pacific Maritime Association warned of a possible strike by about 250 port watchmen from November 1. Why it matters: longer dwell cuts well-car turns and effective capacity on BNSF and Union Pacific intermodal flows, though one snapshot does not establish a lasting equipment shortage.

SMART-TD secures wage increases for CPKC Soo Line conductors
Progressive Railroading · Oct 27 · CPKC · labor
SMART-TD conductors on CPKC's Soo Line ratified a new agreement with retroactive hourly wage increases and further raises effective January 1, 2027, with 75.6% voting in favor on 81% participation when voting closed October 15. The employees operate trains in North Dakota, Minnesota, Wisconsin, Illinois and Indiana. Why it matters: a ratified contract lowers near-term labor-disruption risk on CPKC's U.S. Midwest lines.

Audi selects NS-served Georgia site for first U.S. manufacturing facility
Progressive Railroading · Oct 27 · automotive · Norfolk Southern
Audi will build its first U.S. plant at the Middle Georgia Megasite, which is directly served by Norfolk Southern, with annual capacity of up to 200,000 vehicles; construction is scheduled to begin in 2027 and production in the early 2030s. No investment figure was disclosed. Why it matters: it is a long-dated addition to Southeast automotive traffic, relevant to autorack and parts flows rather than to near-term car demand.

AAR · Oct 27 · association release · hazmat safety
AAR recognized 17 shippers, including Cargill, CHS, CITGO, OxyChem and Nouryon, that recorded zero non-accident hazardous-material releases in 2025 and were nominated by at least four Class I railroads; Kemira earned its 11th consecutive award. Global Rail 1 story
Grupo Mexico drops out of Argentine rail privatisation in setback for Milei
Buenos Aires Times · Oct 21 · press report · Argentina
Grupo México, which owns Ferromex, will not bid for state-run Belgrano Cargas, which has about 14,000 km of freight rail of which 7,600 km are operational, after the government kept an "open access" model letting several operators share the track; the company had pledged an initial US$3 billion in April and had partnered with Wabtec. The remaining top contender reported is a consortium of a local rail operator and grain exporters led by Cargill and Bunge. Why it matters: it removes a major North American operator, and its Wabtec partnership, from one of Latin America's larger freight-rail rebuilds, at least for now.
