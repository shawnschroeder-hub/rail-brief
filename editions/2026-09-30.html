President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The US–China tariff deal opens a door for coal and grain exports. Washington and Beijing agreed to cut tariffs on $60 billion of goods, and China committed to import 10 million metric tons of US coal a year in 2027 and 2028, with lower tariffs on corn, wheat and sorghum but not soybeans. The trade truce now runs through January 10, 2027. If the coal and grain purchases materialize, they would move largely by rail to export terminals, but this is a political framework, not signed cargo, so treat it as potential demand rather than a firm volume.
  2. Canada's export rail chain is getting investment and holding up on grain. Canpotex is putting CA$500 million into the rail-car unloading and ship-loading systems at its Neptune potash terminal in Vancouver, served by CN, with completion expected in 2028. CN and CPKC together filled 90% of grain car orders last week, down from 94% the week before. Both point to steady bulk export demand in the Canadian network, though neither is a new railcar order.
  3. A city derailment and a new bearing monitor put condition monitoring in view. A freight train derailed in Minot, North Dakota, early this morning, damaging a gas line; no injuries or residential impacts were reported and no cause has been given. Separately, voestalpine is marketing a trackside acoustic sensor that it says detects roller-bearing damage long before failure. Wayside detection of bearing and wheel condition is an area where railroads and suppliers are adding technology, which matters for how and when wear parts are replaced.
Top Stories 3 stories
US and China agree tariff cuts on $60 billion of goods, including a coal purchase commitment
NBC News · Sep 28 · trade policy · coal and grain
Under a reciprocal framework, each country cuts tariffs on $30 billion of the other's non-sensitive goods; China will lower tariffs on US corn, wheat, sorghum, meat, logs and wood products, excluding soybeans, and committed to import 10 million metric tons of US coal a year in 2027 and 2028. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the deal improves market access for about 30% of US exports to China, and the trade truce was extended through January 10, 2027. Why it matters: coal and grain are core rail export commodities, so the deal could add carload demand if purchases follow, but the volumes are commitments, not shipments.
Freight train derails in Minot, North Dakota; gas line damaged, no injuries reported
KFYR-TV, citing the City of Minot · Sep 30 · safety · derailment
Minot Fire responded just after 3:30 in the morning to a derailment near 2nd Avenue SW; hazmat crews located a leak from a damaged gas line, which the utility MDU moved to secure, and the 6th Street underpass was closed. The city said no residences were affected, and KFYR's report did not give the railroad, car count or cause. Why it matters: the cause is not yet known, and any finding pointing to equipment rather than track would be relevant to component suppliers.
Canpotex invests CA$500 million in its Neptune potash export terminal
Progressive Railroading · Sep 29 · shippers · Canada
The project at Neptune Bulk Terminals in the Port of Vancouver, served by CN, modernizes the shiploaders, conveyors and dumper pit used to unload rail cars and load vessels, with work expected to finish in 2028; Canpotex says more than 15 million tons of potash move through its supply chain each year. It follows CA$1.5 billion invested at Neptune over the past 20 years. Why it matters: potash is a heavy unit-train commodity, and faster terminal unloading supports steady covered-hopper demand on CN rather than creating new car orders on its own.

CN releases its 2026–2027 winter plan
CN via GlobeNewswire · Sep 29 · customers · Canada
CN says it is upgrading a fleet of more than 100 distributed air braking cars, replacing 21 engines and compressors, and has invested about $64 million in the technology since the program began; it also holds a two-year US waiver for higher-airflow operations between Chicago and Winnipeg and is seeking a permanent exemption in Canada. Why it matters: cold-weather braking performance limits train length and speed in winter, so CN is investing in brake-air capacity rather than cutting train size.

Mixed week-7 grain car results for Canada's major railways
620 CKRM, citing the Ag Transport Coalition · Sep 29 · shippers · grain
CN and CPKC together supplied 90% of shipper grain car orders in week 7, down from 94%; CN rose from 88% to 90%, its first time at that threshold in ten weeks, while CPKC fell from 98% to 89%, its first reading below 90% in twenty weeks. The coalition said system performance has held at 90% or better for four straight weeks. Why it matters: car supply is keeping pace with most orders as the Canadian harvest moves, though a sharp one-week drop at CPKC is worth watching.
Global Rail 2 stories
InfraRed funds to acquire Scandinavian freight operator Hector Rail for SKr2.61 billion
Railway Gazette International · Sep 28 · global · Europe
InfraRed Capital Partners funds will buy 100% of Hector Rail from Ancala, with closing expected by late 2026; Hector runs about 100 mostly electric locomotives in Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Germany, and under Ancala its revenue rose from SKr1.18 billion to SKr1.65 billion while EBITDA grew from SKr72 million to SKr199 million. The buyer plans more locomotive procurement and heavy maintenance programs. Why it matters: infrastructure investors are still paying up for European rail freight operators, a sign of confidence in the sector's long-term economics.
voestalpine launches a trackside acoustic sensor for early bearing-damage detection
voestalpine Railway Systems · Sep 9 · global · technology
The company says its zentrak acoustic monitoring sensor uses trackside microphones to detect defects in the inner ring, outer ring and rolling elements of tapered and cylindrical roller bearings, tracking each bearing's trend over its life and alarming at customer-set thresholds; no customers or performance figures were given. Why it matters: acoustic bearing detection already exists in North America, and more such systems could shift bearing replacement toward condition-based removals rather than fixed intervals.
