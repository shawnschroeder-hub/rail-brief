President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Wabtec raised its 2026 earnings guidance, but its own components commentary points to lower North American rail build. Third-quarter sales were $3.12 billion (up 8.1%), Freight segment sales were $2.237 billion (up 6.9%), and adjusted EPS guidance is now $10.85–$11.05. Freight Components sales rose 5.3% as industrial, maintenance-of-way and international demand offset lower North American rail build, and Equipment sales rose 0.3% on lower locomotive deliveries. These are one competitor's results and its description of its own mix, not industry order or build data.
  2. FreightCar America is adding component supply to its aftermarket business. It completed the acquisition of Trinity Equipment Company, a Norton Shores, Michigan supplier of original-equipment and replacement freight car components and fastening solutions, its third acquisition in less than a year. Terms were not disclosed, and the company said it expects the deal to be immediately accretive. The size of the acquired business is unknown, so this is a strategy signal about one builder rather than a measure of market share.
  3. Weekly traffic is strong but intermodal-led, and a run of earnings reports begins tomorrow. AAR reported U.S. traffic up 8.5% for the week ending October 17, with intermodal up 12.6% and carloads up 3.5%. Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern report third-quarter results on October 22, Greenbrier on October 26 and CPKC on October 28. Weekly traffic volume is not a measure of railcar orders or component demand.

Wabtec Corp. posts Q3 results, increases full-year guidance
Progressive Railroading · Oct 21 · Wabtec · third-quarter results (figures also checked against Wabtec's SEC Form 8-K press release)
Wabtec reported third-quarter sales of $3.12 billion (up 8.1%), Freight segment sales of $2.237 billion (up 6.9%) and an adjusted operating margin of 22.9%, and raised and narrowed its 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $10.85–$11.05. Freight Components sales rose 5.3% as industrial, maintenance-of-way and international demand offset lower North American rail build, while Equipment sales rose 0.3% on lower locomotive deliveries; total backlog was $31.4 billion, including a 12-month backlog of $9.0 billion. Why it matters: the North American rail build comment is Wabtec's description of its own components mix, so it should not be read as an industry build or order figure.

CSX eliminates more than 100 management positions
FreightWaves · Oct 21 · CSX · management restructuring confirmed by a company spokesman
CSX eliminated 109 operations management positions as part of a restructuring of its engineering functions, which a spokesman confirmed to FreightWaves after the changes were first outlined in the spring. Of the affected employees, 58 were offered new operations roles and 51 were offered severance, and in January CSX laid off 166 management employees, about 5% of its non-union workforce at the time. Why it matters: it is a management-headcount change at one railroad and says nothing about its equipment purchasing or maintenance spending.

AAR Reports Weekly Rail Traffic for the Week Ending October 17, 2026
AAR · Oct 21 · weekly rail traffic report
U.S. railroads reported 540,135 carloads and intermodal units, up 8.5% from the same week last year, with carloads of 232,120 (up 3.5%) and intermodal of 308,015 containers and trailers (up 12.6%). Nine of 10 carload commodity groups rose, including metallic ores and metals (up 2,946 to 23,306) and grain (up 1,470 to 22,486), while nonmetallic minerals fell 1,089 to 32,428; year to date through 41 weeks, carloads are up 2.7% and intermodal is up 4.6%. Why it matters: growth was led by intermodal rather than carloads, and weekly traffic volume is not a measure of railcar orders or component demand.

FRA awards $2.3B in CRISI grant funding
Progressive Railroading · Oct 20 · FRA CRISI program · national award announcement
The FRA selected 105 projects from a record 368 applications for $2.3 billion in 2025–2026 CRISI funding across 45 states, with more than $1.9 billion in non-federal match. The largest awards include up to about $249 million for the Alaska Railroad's new 32-mile Port MacKenzie line and up to $204.8 million to rehabilitate Oregon's Coos Bay Rail Line for 286,000-pound double-stack cars. Why it matters: the awards fund track, port-connection and line-capacity work rather than rolling-stock purchases.
Autonomous railcar technology earns $16M federal grant
FreightWaves · Oct 21 · Columbia Terminal Railroad and Intramotev · CRISI-funded autonomous railcar test
The City of Columbia, Missouri, which operates the Columbia Terminal Railroad, received $16.2 million in CRISI funding to deploy two Intramotev TugVolt autonomous railcar units, built at Intramotev's St. Louis facility, and to upgrade track on the short line's 21.4-mile Centralia–Columbia route. The project proceeds in phases under an FRA-approved test program, starting with remote switching at the Columbia terminal and moving to autonomous freight service on the full mainline, with no timetable given. Why it matters: it is a two-unit pilot on one municipal short line, so it is a technology test rather than a fleet purchase.
