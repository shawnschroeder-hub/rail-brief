President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Traffic — the weekly data is out, with metals the standout mover. AAR's report for the week ending August 15 shows US carloads up 1.9% and intermodal up 2.7% year over year, with North American volumes stronger still at 4.9%; metallic minerals and ores rose 19.2%, which FreightWaves attributes to Section 232 tariffs and higher domestic mill utilisation. Motor vehicles and parts fell 13.2% and coal 5.7%. Year to date North American volume is up 2.9%, so the railroads are moving more carloads and containers than a year ago — though the published figures are unit counts, not tonnage, and only selected commodity groups were reported.
  2. Supply base — the National Steel Car stoppage is into its second week and mediation has not actually started. Roughly 1,200 USW Local 7135 members have been out since August 12, and while an Ontario Ministry of Labour mediator has been assigned, active mediation had not begun as of August 18. The sticking point is a pay structure that adds 25% of base wage once a production threshold is met, which the union argues pressures workers on safety. For a components supplier this is the item with the most direct consequence: while that line is stopped, component consumption at the plant stops with it, and deliveries into it are likely to be deferred.
  3. Merger — no new Board action since August 18, and Union Pacific has taken its case public. The STB has issued nothing further since the August 18 procedural schedule in its review of Union Pacific's proposed acquisition of Norfolk Southern, but Union Pacific published a signed piece on August 19 naming BNSF and CPKC as the opposition and characterising their position as protecting their own routes rather than shippers' interests. That is advocacy, not a filing, and it does not change the schedule — comments remain due November 18 — but it signals how hard the applicants intend to litigate the competitive-harm argument in public as well as on the record.

AAR week ending August 15: US traffic up 2.4%, metallic ores up 19.2%, autos down 13.2%
FreightWaves, citing AAR data · Aug 19 · weekly traffic data
US carloads were 233,261 (+1.9%) and intermodal 291,838 (+2.7%) for a combined 525,099 units; the nine North American reporting railroads moved 719,824 units, up 4.9%, with carloads up 4.7% and intermodal up 5.1%. Metallic minerals and ores led at +19.2% — attributed by FreightWaves to Section 232 tariffs and higher domestic mill utilisation — with forest products up 10.5%, while motor vehicles and parts fell 13.2% and coal 5.7%. Year to date US carloads are 7,276,025 (+2.7%) and intermodal 9,005,409 (+3.8%). Why it matters: growth held up across both carloads and intermodal, and the two commodities most tied to heavy-axle-load equipment cycles pulled in opposite directions — ores up sharply, coal down again. Because only the leading movers were reported, how broadly based the gain was cannot be judged from this release; a single week is not a trend, and none of it translates directly into car orders.
National Steel Car strike enters its second week; mediator assigned but mediation not yet underway
Railway Supply · Aug 19 · labour
About 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members have been out since August 12, after a vote with 91% turnout rejected the company's offer by roughly 80% on August 11. The dispute centres on an incentive programme paying an additional 25% of base wage once a production threshold is met, which the union says pressures employees to compromise safety; an Ontario Ministry of Labour mediator has been assigned, but active mediation had not begun as of August 18. Why it matters: National Steel Car is an OEM car builder that installs undercarriage components, so component deliveries into that plant are paused while the line is down. With mediation not yet started, the stoppage has no visible end date — and railcar work does not transfer quickly between builders, so the near-term effect is delayed schedules rather than reallocated orders.
Greenbrier builds 50 gondolas from near-zero-emissions steel for a closed-loop scrap circuit
Greenbrier, SSAB Americas and Alter Trading via PR Newswire · Aug 20 · company announcement · car builder
The cars were built with SSAB Zero steel and high-strength grades intended to cut weight while holding structural integrity; Alter Trading will use them to haul end-of-life steel and recycled metals back to SSAB's Iowa mill, where the material is remelted. Greenbrier's Brian Comstock said the lighter design "increases payload capacity, improves transportation efficiency, and helps reduce lifecycle emissions"; no tonnage or emissions figures were released. Why it matters: a 50-car order is commercially small, and the engineering point is about payload economics rather than component loading — cutting tare weight raises the revenue payload carried within the same gross rail load limit, so the trucks, bearings and draft system see no increase in their rated maximum. The item to watch is whether low-carbon steel and high-strength lightweighting move from one showcase build into routine procurement specifications.

Grain rail carloads at a series record as fuel surcharges hit 61 cents a mile
RFD-TV, citing USDA's Grain Transportation Report · Aug 4 · government data
Year-to-date grain rail carloads reached a record for the available series, up 16% on last year and 24% above the three-year average, with weekly Class I grain carloads running 26% above average; July rail fuel surcharges averaged a record 61 cents per mile. Why it matters: record grain movement means the covered-hopper fleet enters harvest running hard, which points to running-repair and wear-part demand — driven by car-miles and loaded cycles rather than carload counts alone — more than to new builds. The record surcharge lifts the fuel-linked portion of what shippers pay, which is not the same as total freight cost rising.
Regulatory & Policy 1 story
Union Pacific takes the merger fight public, naming BNSF and CPKC as the opposition
Union Pacific — company statement · Aug 19 · merger advocacy, Docket FD 36873
In a signed piece, strategy head Todd Rynaski defends UP's agreements with CN covering Eagle Pass access to Mexico and operating rights on the Elgin, Joliet & Eastern, arguing they add competition; he casts BNSF and CPKC as protecting their own franchises, writing that a three-carrier CN–UP–FXE route with two interchanges "struggles to compete with CPKC's single-line route." This is the railroad's own characterisation of its opponents, not a filing or a Board finding. Why it matters: the substance of the merger case is now being argued in public ahead of the November 18 comment deadline, and Eagle Pass and the EJ&E are exactly the kind of routing conditions that would shape Mexican and Chicago-area traffic flows. Elsewhere in regulation: the Board has issued nothing since its two August 18 releases, and there is still no public update from the Belt Railway of Chicago's weekly Clearing Yard reporting.
