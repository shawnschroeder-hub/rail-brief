President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Supply — a major railcar builder is on strike while order intake shows early stabilization. About 1,200 United Steelworkers members at National Steel Car in Hamilton, Ontario have been on strike since August 12 after rejecting the company's final offer; the plant's 2023 strike ran 41 days. Trinity sees about 25,000 North American deliveries in 2026 with orders roughly matching deliveries, and FreightCar America's backlog more than doubled on about 45% of the quarter's industry orders. A prolonged Hamilton stoppage pauses component pull-through from that plant and can shift orders and delivery slots to other builders.
  2. Network — Chicago's Clearing Yard is under STB scrutiny just as carloads grow. The Board's August 3 letter to the Belt Railway of Chicago cites Clearing Yard car inventory up 57% and dwell up 72% year on year (to 5,048 cars and 31 hours) and requires weekly reporting. AAR's week 31 traffic was +3.0% (carloads +1.8%, intermodal +4.1%) on grain and metals, and USDA projects a record soybean crop. Congestion at the hub traps cars — tightening effective fleet supply — and puts Chicago fluidity back at the center of the merger debate.
  3. Structure — the UP–NS merger review is awaiting a procedural schedule. The STB accepted the revised application for consideration on May 28 and holds it in abeyance; the railroads' July 27 filing offered four new customer commitments, and Board member Karen Hedlund was confirmed through 2030. A schedule is expected "in the coming weeks"; the applicants target mid-2027 on a timetable the Board has not set, against organized opposition. If approved, expect procurement consolidation at two large Class I customers, with STB conditions shaping fleets and routings.

National Steel Car: about 1,200 Hamilton workers on strike since August 12 after rejecting the company's final offer
CHCH News (Hamilton); also CBC · Aug 12 · labor · no settlement reported as of Sunday night
USW Local 7135 members rejected the company's "last, best and final" offer with a 91% turnout and roughly 80% voting no, citing wages that trail inflation, an incentive program worth about a quarter of pay, and safety after three fatalities in 2020–22. The plant's 2023 strike ran about six weeks and settled for 13% over three years. Why it matters: one of North America's largest freight-car builders is idle — component pull-through pauses, and a long stoppage shifts orders and delivery slots to other builders.
STB tightens oversight of the Belt Railway of Chicago as Clearing Yard car inventory rises 57% and dwell 72%
Progressive Railroading, citing the STB's August 3 letter · Aug 5 · regulatory oversight
Weekly car inventory at Clearing Yard rose from 3,212 to 5,048 cars year on year and dwell from about 18 to 31 hours, a trend the Board notes coincides with CSX shifting classification work from Barr Yard to Clearing. BRC — owned by the six Class I railroads — must report weekly from August 10, and the Board wrote the trend "could be indicative of growing congestion in the larger Chicago complex and the region." Why it matters: Chicago is the interchange hub for every customer on this list; cars dwelling 31 hours are cars not earning, and Chicago fluidity is now a live regulatory topic while two BRC owners seek to merge.
AAR weekly rail traffic, week ending August 8: total +3.0%, carloads +1.8%, intermodal +4.1%
Association of American Railroads · Aug 12 · weekly data · next report Wednesday
Grain (+2,113 carloads) and metallic ores and metals (+1,710) led the gainers; coal (−3,766) and motor vehicles and parts (−904) fell for a second week, and year-to-date carloads are +2.7%. Why it matters: carload growth is back but narrow, and traffic is the proxy for wear-part demand — the year-to-date figure is the sturdier read.
FreightCar America Q2: backlog up 121% to 3,972 cars; delivery guidance cut as production slips into 2027
FreightCar America, via GlobeNewswire · Aug 3 · earnings
Orders of about 3,000 cars were about 45% of industry orders in the quarter, the company says, while revenue fell to $113.1m on 927 deliveries and full-year deliveries were cut to 3,500–3,900. Why it matters: the strongest single sign that order intake has stabilized from a low base — implying an industry total in the mid-6,000s for the quarter — even as conversion into built cars slips.
UP–NS merger: supplemental filing in, Board member confirmed, review schedule expected within weeks
Surface Transportation Board (FD 36873); Union Pacific; The American Prospect · Aug 7 (latest) · regulatory docket
The application is accepted for consideration and held in abeyance; the July 27 filing offered expanded gateway pricing, a preserved Class I option for 3-to-2 and 2-to-1 shippers, temporary alternative access if service declines, and a rate-relief process, and a procedural schedule is expected "in the coming weeks." Why it matters: nothing is approved and no schedule exists — the commitments are the applicants' offers, and the Board's eventual conditions will decide which carriers, gateways and fleets carry traffic.
USDA August report: record soybean crop, second-largest corn crop, corn exports raised
DTN/Progressive Farmer, citing USDA · Aug 12 · government data
Corn is put at 16.013bn bushels with exports raised 75m to 3.275bn; soybeans at a record 4.519bn bushels. Why it matters: supports covered-hopper utilization and export unit trains through the fourth quarter — though crop size is not rail share, and the estimates revise again in September.

Five arrested in Southern California rail cargo-theft probe after shots fired at a BNSF railway officer
FreightWaves · Aug 14 · security
During an attempted burglary of a moving northbound BNSF train near Devore in the Cajon Pass on August 11, a suspect fired a handgun toward a railway officer; five men were arrested two days later, one charged with attempted murder, cargo theft and conspiracy. Why it matters: armed theft crews on the busiest western corridor endanger crews and disrupt intermodal flows — a customer operating problem that also feeds the case for more secure equipment and monitoring.
GATX Q2: Rail North America 98.0% utilized, renewal lease rates up 16.8%, guidance raised
GATX release (wire republish) · Jul 30 · earnings
Net income was $103.4m ($2.84 a share) on revenue up 34.8% (a comparison that includes the ~101,000-car Wells Fargo Rail fleet acquired January 1); full-year EPS guidance rose to $9.90–10.30. Why it matters: the tightest fleet reading the market produces — historically the condition that precedes new-car orders — and GATX is now one of the largest aftermarket accounts in North America.
CN reports a record 2025-26 crop year: more than 33.8 million tonnes of Western Canadian grain
CN, via GlobeNewswire (corrected release) · Aug 11 · press release
The prior record was 31.2m tonnes in 2024-25, and July's 2.62m tonnes was also a record for the month. Why it matters: a second straight record year means CN's covered-hopper fleet ran at high intensity all year — a proxy for wheelset, bearing and brake wear.
CPKC sets annual Canadian grain record: 30.66 million tonnes, up 11%
CPKC · Aug 4 · press release
The total ran 16% above CPKC's three-year average, with quarterly records in Q1 and Q2. Why it matters: the second Canadian carrier to report a record grain year — with CN, roughly 64m tonnes under the two carriers' respective measures — and CPKC has guided to accelerated growth in the second half.
BNSF launches faster, six-day-a-week intermodal service from Phoenix to Dallas–Fort Worth
BNSF Railway · Aug 11 · press release
Transit is just over three days to the Alliance Intermodal Facility, with Logistics Park Phoenix opening in early 2027, aimed at converting highway freight. Why it matters: the largest Class I outside the merger is investing in the fastest-growing part of the network; it becomes equipment demand only if the lane is sustained.

BTS freight transportation index falls in June for a third straight month
Progressive Railroading, citing the Bureau of Transportation Statistics · Aug 14 · government data
The index was 134.9, down 0.3% on the month and 1.7% on the year, with rail carloads among the decliners and intermodal among the gainers. Why it matters: the macro counterpoint to the weekly AAR gains — a seasonally adjusted, multimodal series; the July reading in mid-September is the reconciliation point.
Mexico pushes for lower auto tariffs as USMCA talks press on; fourth round set for September
CBT News, citing The Wall Street Journal · Aug 13 · trade policy
Mexico proposes taxing only the non-North-American content of a vehicle, potentially cutting the effective top tariff from 25% to 5–10%; steel and aluminum tariffs remain unresolved and Canada talks have not started. Why it matters: autorack and cross-border intermodal volumes move with the outcome, and the steel and aluminum tariffs bear directly on castings and steel inputs.
