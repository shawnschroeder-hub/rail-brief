President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The Class I railroads are lining up on opposite sides of the UP–NS merger as comments come due. Opening comments are due at the STB today, November 18, and FreightWaves' roundup of earnings-call remarks shows CPKC saying rejection is its preferred outcome while CN says its agreement with Union Pacific mitigates its concerns. BNSF has called the deal anti-competitive. The Board's decision is expected in 2027, so today's filings are an input rather than an outcome.
  2. States keep funding short-line track and sidings. North Carolina DOT awarded $13.3 million to 12 short lines, the Port of Wilmington and three shippers, matched by railroad, manufacturer and ports-authority money. Grants of this kind support the smaller railroads that handle first- and last-mile car movements, though any effect on railcar component demand is indirect.
  3. Indian freight loadings set an October record. Indian Railways loaded 145.07 million tonnes in October, up 8.32% from a year earlier, according to the Ministry of Railways. Loading is a measure of how hard the wagon fleet is working, not of new wagon orders, so it is a supportive backdrop for Amsted's Indian operations rather than a demand signal on its own.

Transcontinental merger of Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern divides rail leaders
FreightWaves, citing company earnings calls and statements · Nov 17 · merger · trade-press roundup

Regulatory & Policy 1 story
North Carolina DOT awards grants to short lines, port authority and shippers
Progressive Railroading, citing NCDOT · Nov 17 · state funding · short lines
The department awarded $13.3 million from its Freight Rail and Rail Crossing Safety Improvement program, created in 2014, to 12 short lines, the Port of Wilmington and three shippers, with the grants matched by railroad, manufacturer and ports-authority investment. The largest awards named are $2.4 million for Aberdeen Carolina and Western track and switch work, $2 million for Great Smoky Mountains Railroad track and sidings, and $1.4 million for new tracks at the Port of Wilmington. Why it matters: state grants like these help fund short-line track and siding upgrades, though any effect on railcar component demand is indirect.
Global Rail 1 story
Indian Railways sets October freight record at 145.07 million tonnes
Maritime Gateway, citing the Ministry of Railways · Nov 5 · India · freight loading
Indian Railways loaded 145.07 million tonnes in October, the highest total for the month and up 8.32% from 133.93 million tonnes a year earlier, with food grains up 26.3% and domestic coal up 16%. April–October loading reached 978.78 million tonnes, up 4.67%. Why it matters: strong loadings show a busy wagon fleet in a market where Amsted operates, but loading growth does not by itself signal new wagon orders.
