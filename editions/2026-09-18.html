President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Union Pacific's CEO gave the applicants' first public rebuttal of rivals' trackage-rights requests in the UP–NS proceeding. At an investor conference, Jim Vena said BNSF's request for roughly 800 miles of trackage rights over a combined UP–NS network "goes against the fundamental principle of how business should work," arguing UP would simply charge a per-car-mile fee that raises the cost for whichever railroad uses the rights. BNSF, CPKC and CSX filed requests along these lines on September 9 (BNSF's own filing cited about 824 miles) while urging the Board to reject the merger outright. Responsive applications are due November 18, and Union Pacific's own May filing said it would withdraw if the Board ordered widespread line sales or trackage rights.
  2. Rail fuel surcharges on grain shipments have climbed to record highs during harvest season. USDA data reported by Reuters show the average surcharge reached 48 cents per rail-car mile in the second week of September, up 153% from a year earlier, and now equal to about 11% of total rail transportation cost for corn and soybeans versus 5% a year ago; Surface Transportation Board data cited in the same report show railroads collected $2.93 billion in surcharges industry-wide in the second quarter. BNSF, CSX, Norfolk Southern, CN, CPKC and Union Pacific were all named as assessing the higher charges. The increase squeezes grain-shipper margins at peak harvest, though the report does not quantify any resulting effect on covered-hopper demand.
  3. The National Steel Car strike is now in its sixth week with no negotiations scheduled. Roughly 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members have been off the job at the Hamilton, Ontario plant since August 12; CHCH's September 16 report, the most recent we could read, put the strike at day 36 and said no new talks were planned, and we found no newer report today. National Steel Car is one of North America's major freight-railcar builders, and the prolonged shutdown continues to raise open questions about its production capacity. We will keep carrying this as a standing watch item until it resolves.

ASLRRA names its 2026 President's safety award recipients
Progressive Railroading account of an ASLRRA announcement · Sep 17 · associations · safety
The American Short Line and Regional Railroad Association named sixteen winners across four categories based on FRA safety data, using revised 2026 criteria that groups eligible railroads by employee-on-duty hours and train miles rather than the prior regional divisions; winners include Indiana Rail Road, the Port Terminal Railroad Association and the Terminal Railroad Association of St. Louis. Why it matters: this recognizes short-line safety performance and has no direct bearing on component demand, but short lines are part of the network our railcars move across.

Regulatory & Policy 1 story
South Carolina's governor writes the STB in support of the UP–NS merger
PR Newswire (Norfolk Southern release) · Sep 15 · regulatory · UP–NS merger
South Carolina Governor Henry McMaster sent a letter to the Surface Transportation Board backing the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger, saying "expanding the role of rail" can help the state's transportation, congestion and environmental goals, and Norfolk Southern CEO Mark George said the deal would give businesses "more seamless single-line access to more destinations." The letter adds a state-government voice to the record alongside the more than 500 customer endorsements Union Pacific reported last week. Why it matters: political support does not affect the Board's substantive review, but it signals building state-level backing ahead of the Board's November 18 deadline for responsive filings and conditions requests.
