President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Digital automatic couplers are drawing public funding and competitor positioning, but both moves are pre-commercial. Knorr-Bremse said today it signed a memorandum of understanding with the China Academy of Railway Sciences covering digital automatic couplers, heavy-haul braking and wagon monitoring, and framed the long-term potential of its rail-freight business — not of couplers alone — at about €1 billion a year. On September 4 Germany's federal transport ministry opened the first call under a €36m programme funding DAC retrofits, trials and studies, a separate initiative from the Chinese work rather than a shared specification. An MOU is a statement of intent and a funding call is not an order, and neither changes North American interchange requirements; what they show is that the coupling interface is attracting sustained public and competitor attention, including in China, where we operate.
  2. Long Beach set an August record, but on thin growth. The port moved 919,992 TEUs last month, its busiest August on record, though the year-over-year gain was 2% and volume for the year to date is up just 1.3%. Imports rose 3.6% while empties slipped slightly, and the port's chief executive framed the month as shippers adapting to tariffs rather than as a surge in demand. These are marine-terminal container counts, and the port did not break out how much of the cargo moved inland by rail. The fair read is volume holding at a high level, not evidence of accelerating intermodal demand.
  3. Hamilton would be at day 35, and the search record around it is easier to misread than ever. The USW Local 7135 stoppage that began at 12:01 a.m. on August 12 would be at day 35 today if unresolved, and nothing we could read reports a settlement, a revised offer or a date to return to the table. A Railway Age headline reading "National Steel Car Strike Over" now sits high in search results alongside the USW Canada page making the same claim — both describe the 2023 dispute, which ran 41 days and was ratified on August 8, 2023. That is the fourth edition in which this brief has had to set that trap aside, now a trade-press headline alongside the union's own page rather than the union page alone. Our own call-offs into Hamilton are the better read on near-term demand to us, though they do not by themselves establish whether the plant is running.

Knorr-Bremse signs a rail-freight technology MOU with China's state railway research academy
Knorr-Bremse newsroom, a company posting · Sep 15 · competitor · technology · China
Knorr-Bremse said today it has signed a memorandum of understanding with the China Academy of Railway Sciences covering digital automatic couplers with wireless functions, braking systems for long-distance and heavy-haul service, wagon monitoring, predictive maintenance and driver assistance based on its LEADER technology. The company put the long-term global revenue potential of its rail-freight business at approximately €1 billion a year and the European opportunity in the mid triple-digit million-euro range annually, describing the agreement as building on more than 20 years of cooperation; an MOU is a statement of intent, not a booked order. Why it matters: a competitor is positioning in freight coupling and heavy-haul braking markets adjacent to our own, in a country where we also operate — and the revenue figures are the company's own long-term framing of its rail-freight business, not contracted volume.
Port of Long Beach moves 919,992 TEUs in its busiest August on record
Port of Long Beach figures as reported by Progressive Railroading · Sep 14 · intermodal · demand
The port handled 919,992 twenty-foot equivalent units in August, up 2% year over year, with imports at 456,100 TEUs (up 3.6%), exports at 99,754 (up 4%) and empties at 364,138 (down 0.39%). Year-to-date volume through August reached 6,678,078 TEUs, 1.3% above the first eight months of 2025, and chief executive Noel Hacegaba said shippers "continue to adapt to tariffs and geopolitical uncertainty." Why it matters: a record August on 2% growth reads as volume holding rather than accelerating, and these are marine-terminal container counts — the port did not break out how much of the cargo moved inland by rail.

Strike start and ballot figures from CBC Hamilton; the competing "strike over" headlines were readable only as search listings, not as article pages · walkout Aug 12 · competitor · labour · standing watch item
Counting from the walkout at 12:01 a.m. on August 12 by United Steelworkers Local 7135, whose members cast 1,092 of about 1,202 ballots in rejecting the company's offer, the Hamilton, Ontario stoppage would be at day 35 today if unresolved, and no source we could read reports a settlement, a revised offer or a scheduled bargaining date. A Railway Age headline reading "National Steel Car Strike Over" again ranked near the top of search results, but it describes the 2023 dispute — 1,475 workers, a 41-day strike from June 29 ratified by 87.4% on August 8, 2023 — as does the USW Canada page carrying the same claim. Illinois regulators approve $1.1 million for two grade crossings on a Watco short line
Illinois Commerce Commission order as reported by Progressive Railroading · Sep 14 · regulatory · safety
The Illinois Commerce Commission approved about $1.1 million from the state's Grade Crossing Protection Fund for the East Solon Road and Blivin Street crossings near Solon Mills and Spring Grove, on the Wisconsin & Southern Railroad, a Watco subsidiary. The order covers up to $866,228 for signal design and construction, with the fund paying 95% of that signal work and the railroad 5%, plus $261,043 for crossing surface materials, and requires the work within 18 months. Why it matters: this is signal and crossing-surface spending that does not touch our product lines, carried as home-state regulatory context — the fund commits about $42 million a year on this pattern, from motor fuel tax receipts.
Global Rail 1 story
Germany opens the first call under a €36m programme to fit digital automatic couplers to freight trains
Railway Gazette International · Sep 8, on a funding directive published Sep 4 · global · technology
The Federal Ministry of Transport published its PioDAK funding directive in the Bundesanzeiger on September 4, opening the first call for proposals under a €36m programme administered by the Federal Office for Logistics and Mobility. The round funds three areas: equipping or retrofitting pioneer freight trains with digital automatic couplers for use in operational conditions, further research and development, and feasibility studies covering strategic needs, benefits, risks and implementation. Why it matters: Germany is putting public money behind DAC retrofits while a major supplier pursues the technology in China — separate programmes rather than one standard — and North American interchange requirements are unaffected by either.
