STB denies Union Pacific's appeal and orders the Vena speech recording made public
American-Rails.com, citing STB Decision 45 · Nov 5 · trade press · UP–NS merger · STB
In Decision 45, served November 4 in the UP–NS merger docket, the Surface Transportation Board denied Union Pacific's appeal of a judge's order as untimely, said it would also have denied it on the merits, and required UP to certify by 5:00 pm Eastern on November 5 that it had removed the confidential label from CEO Jim Vena's March 17 shipper-conference recording and served it on all parties. The Board said that if the certification is not filed it anticipates an abeyance order that would pause the schedule, and CPKC's separate October 30 request that the Board dismiss the application awaits an applicants' reply due November 19. Why it matters: opponents argue the remarks chilled shipper participation, so the recording could shape the comments due November 18, though the Board has not ruled on the merger itself.
AAR Reports Weekly Rail Traffic for the Week Ending October 31, 2026
Association of American Railroads · Nov 4 · association data · rail traffic
US railroads moved 233,816 carloads in the week ending October 31, up 3.0% from a year earlier, and 280,264 intermodal units, up 3.9%, for combined traffic of 514,080, up 3.5%; seven of ten carload groups rose, led by metallic ores and metals (up 3,883 to 25,059), chemicals (up 2,542) and motor vehicles and parts (up 1,351), while grain fell 2,092 to 23,078. Year to date, US carloads are up 2.7% and intermodal 4.7%, and North American combined traffic across nine reporting railroads rose 4.3% for the week, with Mexico up 11.1% in carloads and 26.9% in intermodal. Why it matters: steady carload growth in metals, chemicals and autos supports car utilization and wear-part demand, but weekly gains do not by themselves signal new-car orders.
Port strike delayed again at Los Angeles, Long Beach ports
FreightWaves · Nov 5 · trade press · ports · labor
The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26, which represents about 250 terminal watchmen, extended their contract again, to midnight Pacific on Friday, November 6, after the previous extension had set a Wednesday 5:00 pm deadline; federal mediators are assisting. The affected terminals handle about 85% of Long Beach cargo and 33% at Los Angeles, and the employers warned that other ILWU members could honor picket lines. Why it matters: a walkout would most directly hit the import containers that feed western intermodal trains, and the new deadline falls on Friday night.

8 inches of rain shut down busiest US rail line for 60 hours
FreightWaves, interviewing Trains' Bill Stephens · Nov 4 · trade press · Class I service · BNSF
Eight inches of rain over three days at Laguna, New Mexico, closed BNSF's Southern Transcon from Saturday, October 24, through Tuesday, October 27, about 60 hours, after a second wave of storms extended a closure that would otherwise have lasted roughly 24 to 30 hours. BNSF could neither move loaded containers east nor reposition empty cars west, raising container dwell at Los Angeles and Long Beach, and its weekly intermodal volume fell 0.4% after a 14% gain the prior week while Union Pacific posted a 17% gain. Why it matters: a short outage on the main Southern California–Chicago corridor quickly backs up ports and car cycles, though the effect reported so far is delay rather than lost volume.
Regulatory & Policy 2 stories
Ground rules set for responsive applications in the UP–NS case
American-Rails.com, citing STB Decision 46 · Nov 5 · trade press · STB decision served
In Decision 46, served November 4, the STB granted in part and denied in part CPKC's and CN's requests for waivers and clarifications on responsive applications in the UP–NS case, letting CPKC file consolidated information for its US rail affiliates and CN combine several sub-dockets. The Board declined to classify four CPKC proposals in advance as minor transactions — trackage rights on the Polo Line and Kansas City Terminal, the Techny line and the Lufkin Subdivision, plus the Wylie Intermodal Terminal reconveyance — saying it needs more information on operating and service effects. Why it matters: the conditions rival railroads seek would, if granted, shape traffic flows around a combined network, and the ruling keeps those requests under fuller review.
FRA extends comments on a Union Pacific inspection waiver to December 2
American-Rails.com, citing an FRA notice · Nov 5 · trade press · comment deadline
FRA extended to December 2 the comment period on a Union Pacific petition seeking relief from certain provisions of 49 CFR part 229, the federal locomotive safety standards, on inspections by qualified mechanical inspectors (docket FRA-2026-1420). The extension, published in the Federal Register on November 2, adds 30 days at the request of the AFL-CIO's Transportation Trades Department, and FRA has not ruled on the petition. Why it matters: the petition covers locomotives rather than freight cars, but who performs mechanical inspections is a closely watched precedent for Class I maintenance practice.

Stella-Jones shares Q3 results, performance optimization plan
Progressive Railroading · Nov 4 · trade press · earnings · tie supplier
Stella-Jones reported third-quarter sales of C$979 million versus C$958 million a year earlier, net income of C$51 million versus C$88 million and adjusted EPS of C$1.39 versus C$1.59, after C$32 million of impairment and restructuring costs tied mainly to network optimization. The company said a combined performance plan targets up to $100 million in cumulative cost savings over three years and that it is pursuing higher-margin railway tie business, including renewing key Class I contracts. Why it matters: Stella-Jones is a tie supplier rather than a car-component competitor, so its tie commentary is a read on Class I track maintenance, not on railcar demand.

Wabtec · Nov 2 · company release · financing
Wabtec priced $500 million of 5.900% senior notes due February 25, 2037, with closing expected November 4. It plans to use the proceeds, together with revolver, short-term borrowings or commercial paper, to repay its 3.450% senior notes maturing November 15, 2026. Why it matters: this is routine refinancing at a higher coupon and does not change Wabtec's operating outlook.
Global Rail 3 stories
Combined transport in Europe back in decline in Q3
RailFreight.com, citing UIRR · Nov 5 · trade press · Europe · intermodal
Combined road-rail transport volumes in Europe fell 1.23% year on year in the third quarter, ending a brief spring stabilization, according to the trade association UIRR's quarterly newsletter. UIRR cited congested German corridors, very low Rhine water levels, rising track access charges and weak growth, and said its members expect a negative business climate over the next 12 months. Why it matters: European intermodal weakness contrasts with North American gains and is a soft signal for European wagon and component demand.
One-day French national strike to disrupt rail freight
RailFreight.com · Nov 5 · trade press · France · labor
A one-day national strike over the cost of living on November 5, supported mainly by staff at infrastructure manager SNCF Réseau, was expected to cause widespread but localized freight disruption, with most impact in the evening, according to DB Cargo France. DB Cargo France flagged cancellations including Forbach–Irun, Chalon–Marseille-Fos, two Calais–Perpignan trains and a Paris/Valenton–Metz combined service. Why it matters: a one-day stoppage mainly delays flows, but it adds to recovery pressure with a weekend line closure north of Paris already planned.
Freight volume collapses by 36%: Ukrainian Railways set for more severe loss in 2026
RailFreight.com, citing Forbes Ukraine · Nov 5 · trade press · Ukraine
Ukraine's Deputy Minister of Infrastructure Volodymyr Shemayev told Forbes Ukraine that Ukrainian Railways' freight volumes have fallen about 36% since the end of July, and Forbes Ukraine reported 400 locomotives lost to Russian drone attacks this year. RailFreight.com estimates the railway's 2026 net loss could reach 18 billion hryvnia (about €355 million), against a summer forecast of 10 to 12 billion. Why it matters: the losses deepen Ukraine's need for rolling stock and could weigh on its grain and steel export flows.
