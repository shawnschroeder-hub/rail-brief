President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Intermodal is carrying the volume gains. AAR data show U.S. intermodal up 10.4% in the week ending October 10 against a 3.4% rise in carloads, and a FreightWaves analysis puts Union Pacific's intermodal gain at 19.4% and BNSF's at 12.5%. Metals and motor vehicles carloads rose while coal and food fell. Intermodal growth leans on well cars and platforms rather than the carload fleet, so its pull on component demand is narrower than the headline totals suggest.
  2. East Coast ports are funding long-term capacity. The Port of Philadelphia has lined up $837 million to more than double its container capacity, and Georgia Ports received the Army Corps permit for a $2.3 billion, 3.5 million TEU terminal at Savannah. Both are served by Norfolk Southern and CSX. The Savannah project is not due to start construction until January 2028, so these are long-dated signals for eastern intermodal rather than near-term traffic.
  3. Next week brings key decisions and data. Commerce's decision on whether to open the Mexican tank car trade investigations is due around October 20. Wabtec and CSX report third-quarter results on October 21, and Union Pacific confirmed today that it reports on October 22. These will give the first read on whether the intermodal surge is translating into railroad spending plans.

AAR Reports Weekly Rail Traffic for the Week Ending October 10, 2026
AAR · Oct 14 · weekly rail traffic · industry data
U.S. railroads moved 534,256 carloads and intermodal units, up 7.2% year over year, with carloads up 3.4% to 232,004 and intermodal up 10.4% to 302,252; eight of ten carload groups rose, led by metallic ores and metals (+3,621 to 22,048), while coal fell 1,156 to 57,526. North American volume was 725,403 units, up 5.9%, and U.S. year-to-date volume is up 3.6% (carloads +2.7%, intermodal +4.4%). Why it matters: metals and motor-vehicle carloads are firming, but most of the growth is intermodal, which uses a different car fleet from the carload business.
Port of Philadelphia secures funding for $837M expansion
Progressive Railroading · Oct 15 · ports · capacity investment
PhilaPort has assembled $837 million — $371.5 million from Pennsylvania, $266 million in U.S. DOT grants and $200 million in private investment — for its largest expansion, aimed at raising container capacity 122% and auto capacity 18%. Norfolk Southern and CSX serve the port. Why it matters: added container and auto capacity on the East Coast could support future rail intermodal and autorack traffic for both eastern Class I railroads, though timing depends on construction.
Georgia Ports secures permit for Savannah Container Terminal
Progressive Railroading · Oct 15 · ports · permit approved
The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers approved the Georgia Ports Authority's permit for a 350-acre terminal on Hutchison Island, ending a three-year review; the roughly $2.3 billion project is planned for 3.5 million TEUs of capacity, with construction to start in January 2028 and finish in the mid-2030s. The port is served by CSX, Norfolk Southern and the Savannah Port Terminal Railroad. Why it matters: it is a long-dated addition to Southeast intermodal capacity rather than a near-term traffic driver.

Why Rail Is Booming: Intermodal & Carload Growth Explained
FreightWaves · Oct 14 · Class I railroads · analysis of AAR data
FreightWaves reports week-40 intermodal gains of 19.4% at Union Pacific, 12.5% at BNSF, 8.9% at CSX and 5.4% at Norfolk Southern, tying the western lead to record Los Angeles and Long Beach port volumes. It attributes the shift toward rail to higher diesel prices and tight trucking capacity, and says rail service has largely held up. Why it matters: if diesel prices ease or truck capacity loosens, some of this traffic could move back to the highway, so the gains should not be read as permanent.
BNSF expands cargo security partnership with Overhaul
Progressive Railroading · Oct 14 · BNSF · company announcement (BNSF release dated Oct 13)
BNSF expanded its nearly decade-long partnership with risk-monitoring firm Overhaul, linking the BNSF Police Department with Overhaul's intelligence teams, which watch intermodal freight for tampering, fraud and theft and relay verified alerts directly to the railroad. Overhaul, for which BNSF is the exclusive railroad partner, claims a 99.9% protection rate for monitored shipments. Why it matters: cargo theft from intermodal trains remains a live problem for western railroads, and security is becoming part of how they compete for high-value freight.
BLET members ratify first contract with Georgia Central Railway
Progressive Railroading · Oct 14 · Genesee & Wyoming · labor agreement
Engineers and trainmen at Georgia Central Railway, a 211-mile Genesee & Wyoming short line between Savannah and Macon, ratified their first BLET contract, two years after voting to join the union in October 2024; the number of workers covered was not disclosed. The agreement sets pay rates, seniority, discipline, grievance and benefit rules. Why it matters: union organizing continues to spread across short lines, which can raise labor costs but also reduces the risk of disruption at a Savannah-area feeder railroad.

Indiana Harbor Belt to serve new Ozinga cement mill
Progressive Railroading · Oct 15 · construction materials · shipper expansion
Ozinga opened a low-carbon cement mill in East Chicago, Indiana, expected to produce 1 million tons of slag cement a year, with onsite rail and access to all Class I railroads through the Indiana Harbor Belt Railroad; no investment figure was given. Why it matters: it is a new rail-served bulk shipper in the Chicago gateway, a modest positive for covered-hopper traffic in the region.
