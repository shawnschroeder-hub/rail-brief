President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Traffic is running well ahead of last year, led by intermodal, with automotive the one clear soft spot. For the week ending August 22, U.S. carloads and intermodal units together were up 4.2% year over year. On the AAR's own figures intermodal supplied roughly two-thirds of the week's incremental units, with the remainder from carload strength in metallic ores and metals, chemicals and grain; motor vehicles and parts fell 1,837 carloads, the largest single decline of the week. Originations measure what moved, not how hard the fleet is working or what anyone is ordering, so read this as a supportive demand backdrop rather than a signal on new-car builds or component purchasing.
  2. The National Steel Car strike is now in its third week with no settlement reported. More than 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members in Hamilton have been out since August 12 — today is day 17. The most recent account this brief's reader could open, dated August 27, describes the strike continuing with no resumed bargaining; an Ontario Ministry of Labour mediator was assigned in mid-August but active mediation had not begun as of August 18, and nothing later was found. Plant production status is not established by any source read. Worth keeping in proportion: if a buyer eventually moves an order to another builder, industry demand for trucks, wheels and bearings shifts rather than disappears, so our exposure depends on our share at the receiving builder, not on National Steel Car alone.
  3. Norfolk Southern has extended its locomotive modernization programme with Wabtec to 33 more units. The ES44DC locomotives will be converted from DC to AC traction, with deliveries running through 2027. This is a decision about 33 specific locomotives rather than a stated change in capital policy, and it is locomotive scope — it carries no direct read-through to freight-car undercarriage. It is also worth remembering that Class I railroads own a minority of the North American freight-car fleet: operating lessors and shippers drive most new-car demand, so a railroad's locomotive capital choices are a weak proxy for what happens on the car side.

U.S. rail traffic up 4.2% in week 33, led by intermodal, with metals, chemicals and grain up and motor vehicles down
Association of American Railroads — weekly rail traffic report · Aug 26 · traffic data
For the week ending August 22, U.S. railroads originated 532,462 carloads and intermodal units, up 4.2% against the same week in 2025 — 235,885 carloads, up 3.2%, and 296,577 containers and trailers, up 5.0%. On those figures intermodal accounted for roughly two-thirds of the week's added units. The largest carload gains were metallic ores and metals, up 2,054 carloads to 23,663; chemicals, up 1,923 to 33,850; and grain, up 1,844 to 22,260, while motor vehicles and parts fell 1,837 carloads to 15,849. Canadian railroads reported carloads up 12.9% and Mexican railroads intermodal up 33.5%; U.S. year-to-date traffic through 33 weeks stands at 16,813,896 carloads and intermodal units, up 3.3%. Why it matters: commodity categories do not map one-for-one to car types — grain moves largely in covered hoppers, chemicals in tank cars and covered hoppers, metals and ores in gondolas and open hoppers — so a week's commodity mix is a rough guide to which parts of the fleet are working, not a car-type demand forecast. Higher loadings can lift running-repair and wear-part consumption if sustained, but that depends on mileage, duty cycle and fleet velocity, none of which this report measures. The automotive decline is the figure most worth tracking, with the caveat that autoracks sit largely in the railroad-owned TTX pool and so follow different purchasing dynamics from lessor-owned bulk cars.
Norfolk Southern extends Wabtec modernization to 33 more locomotives, converting DC units to AC traction
Progressive Railroading · Aug 27 · customer capital programme · competitor activity
The agreement covers 33 ES44DC Evolution Series units, which Wabtec will rebuild as ES44ACM locomotives with AC traction motors, upgraded control systems and added digital diagnostics; the companies cite improved adhesion, hauling performance and maintenance planning with reduced lifecycle cost. The first locomotive shipped in August 2026 and the remaining 32 are scheduled through 2027, with the work performed at Wabtec's U.S. facilities. Norfolk Southern was the original ES44DC launch customer in 2004, and the two companies have completed more than 1,000 DC-to-AC conversions together since 2015. No contract value was disclosed. Why it matters: this is locomotive scope and carries no direct read-through to freight-car trucks, side frames, bolsters or bearings, so it should not be treated as a demand signal for our categories. The revenue Wabtec captures here comes from the railroad's locomotive capital and mechanical budgets, which run parallel to — not in competition with — the freight-car repair budgets our aftermarket business serves. Its interest is as one more data point on how a major customer is spending on asset life extension, not as evidence of a wider capital policy.
National Steel Car strike reaches day 17 with no settlement reported and mediation not confirmed as under way
Railway Supply (Aug 19) and World Socialist Web Site (Aug 27) · labour · standing watch item
More than 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members at the Kenilworth Avenue North plant in Hamilton, Ontario have been on strike since August 12, having rejected the company's offer on roughly 91% turnout with about 78–80% against; the dispute centres on wages and on an incentive scheme paying an additional 25% of base wage for meeting production thresholds, which the union argues pressures workers on safety. As of the August 19 trade account an Ontario Ministry of Labour mediator had been assigned but active mediation had not begun; an August 27 account — from an outlet that reports the dispute from an explicitly partisan standpoint, cited here only for the fact that the strike continues — describes workers entering a third week without pay and reports no resumed bargaining or new company offer. Neither source establishes current production status at the plant. Why it matters: the near-term supplier effects are about timing and working capital — delivery dates, release schedules, receiving appointments, and who holds inventory while a build waits — and these vary with contract terms and build stage rather than turning on any particular day count. If the dispute runs long enough that a buyer places work with another builder, industry-level demand for undercarriage components shifts rather than disappears; what changes is our share of it, which depends on our position at the receiving builder. Treat plant status as unconfirmed rather than assume a shutdown, and treat this as a single-builder question, not a market signal.

USDOT seeks comment on a corridor model for utility development along highway and rail rights-of-way
Progressive Railroading; Federal Register notice of Aug 18 · Aug 27 · request for information, comments close Sept 12
America's Great Corridors of Commerce contemplates state transportation departments and railroads negotiating access along eligible existing corridors with private "corridor managers" who would design, develop, operate and maintain transmission lines, fibre, water pipelines and similar utilities, with the initiative describing lease revenue as a source that could fund repairs and upgrades on the corridor. The USDOT Build America Bureau has published a request for information on the model; comments close September 12, an informational webinar is set for September 1, and the Bureau says it intends to select up to five initial priority corridors for technical support, streamlined permitting and public-private partnership structuring. Why it matters: the initiative exists and USDOT intends to select corridors, but this stage is a request for information — not a rule, a grant award, a corridor selection or any obligation on a railroad, and participation would be voluntary. Note also that it does not create a new property right: whether any given owner can accommodate a utility still turns on title, deed and easement terms, operating needs, safety clearances and local and environmental approvals, none of which an RFI resolves. Any connection to equipment or component purchasing would be several steps removed and is speculative today.

Škoda Transtech to cut 117 jobs at its Finnish rolling-stock plant after losing a tram award to Stadler
Yle (Finnish public broadcaster) · Aug 25 · rolling-stock manufacturing · Europe
Škoda's Finnish subsidiary concluded change negotiations on August 25 and will reduce its workforce by 117, with targeted furloughs also possible, at the Otanmäki plant in Kajaani. The company attributes the cuts to prolonged uncertainty around the Helsinki and Vantaa tram procurement, which Stadler won in October 2025 with a contract reported at €271 million covering 30 city trams and 33 rapid trams plus parts and lifecycle support; the reduction is smaller than the up-to-200 estimated when negotiations opened in late May. Why it matters: this is passenger rolling stock in a European market and carries no read-through to North American freight-car components or to our order book. It is carried as a market-structure note: the loss of a single large award was enough to trigger a substantial workforce reduction at the unsuccessful bidder, a reminder of how concentrated award risk is in rolling-stock manufacturing. The reporting describes headcount, not the removal of physical capacity.
Stadler commits SFr 33 million to modernise its Biel steel foundry
Railway PRO · Aug 15 · steel castings · Europe
The investment — SFr 33 million, about €35 million — covers a new dispatch and shipping hall, extensions to the existing industrial building at Johann-Renfer-Strasse in Biel, new filter systems and roughly 8,900 square metres of rooftop photovoltaics. Stadler expects work to start in early 2027 and the new infrastructure to enter service in 2028, projecting a roughly 15% reduction in total energy consumption and about 80% less foundry-sand waste, while securing the site's 115 existing jobs with modest headcount growth. The foundry produces steel castings used in rail vehicles worldwide; no increase in casting output is claimed. Why it matters: the value here is as a cost and timeline reference point for foundry modernisation of this type — energy, emissions, waste handling and logistics rather than added output — at roughly two years from announcement to commissioning. Treat the figure as indicative only: it cannot be read across to a North American side-frame or bolster foundry without normalising for process, output, labour cost, scope and environmental requirements. No North American freight qualification, customer or capacity plan is reported.
