President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. Greenbrier will change chief executives in January, and the successor comes from the commercial side of the house. Lorie Tekorius retires on January 6, 2027 after the annual meeting, handing over to Brian Comstock, currently executive vice president and president of the Americas. Comstock has run the commercial and leasing teams, which means the relationships and the order book are the ground he knows best. A four-month runway with transition support reads as an orderly, planned handover, though a new chief executive's strategic priorities are not knowable from a succession announcement.
  2. September 30 has quietly become the date to circle, for two unrelated reasons. The Board moved the deadline for notices of intent to participate in the UP–NS review from September 4 out to September 30, and separately the five-year federal surface transportation authorization expires that same day. Neither is a decision point on the merger's merits — the participation change is administrative and every other date in the August 18 schedule stands. The convergence simply means one calendar day now carries both the notice deadline for merger participants and the end of the current authorization period for federal surface-transportation programmes.
  3. The Hamilton stoppage is into a fourth week, and there is still no readable evidence either way on plant output. Roughly 1,200 Steelworkers have been out at National Steel Car since August 12, which makes today day 22, with a mediator assigned since August 18 and no reported settlement, new offer or resumed bargaining. What we cannot establish from any source we could open is current production status at the plant, so treat delivery-timing exposure as a question to put to anyone with cars on order there rather than a conclusion. Note too that searches on this dispute still surface heavy coverage of a different National Steel Car strike that ended in 2023 — that one is settled and unrelated.
Top Stories 3 stories
Greenbrier names Brian Comstock to succeed Lorie Tekorius as president and chief executive
The Greenbrier Companies — company announcement · Aug 31 · competitor · leadership
Tekorius will retire as president and chief executive on January 6, 2027 following the annual shareholder meeting, and is expected to provide transition services afterwards. She joined Greenbrier in 1995 and has held the chief executive role since 2022, having previously served as chief financial officer and as president and chief operating officer; Comstock, currently executive vice president and president of the Americas, has led the commercial and leasing teams and is expected to succeed her on the board. Why it matters: a four-month handover with transition support indicates an orderly, planned succession at a major North American railcar builder, though a commercially-grounded successor can still shift leasing appetite, pricing or sourcing, so strategic direction remains unknown.
STB pushes the UP–NS notice-of-intent deadline from September 4 to September 30
STB decision of Aug 31, reported by Trains News Wire — we could read the report in summary only, not the decision document · regulatory · standing watch item
The Board's chief counsel granted a National League of Cities request to move the deadline for filing notices of intent to participate out by 26 days, leaving every other date set in the August 18 procedural schedule unchanged. The League argued that an estimated 12,000 local governments sit in railroad-served communities, many had yet to decide whether to take part, and the original window straddled vacation season and the Labor Day weekend; filing a notice puts a party on the service list without obliging it to participate actively. Why it matters: this is the current notice deadline and changes who may take part rather than the merits or the overall timetable, and because the later dates are unchanged, new participants have a shorter window in which to prepare.
National Steel Car strike reaches day 22 with no settlement reported in sources we could read
Railway Supply (Aug 19) on the dispute and mediation status; World Socialist Web Site (Aug 31) for picket-line reporting · competitor · labour · standing watch item
Roughly 1,200 United Steelworkers Local 7135 members have been out at the Hamilton, Ontario plant since 12:01 a.m. on August 12, after about 91% turnout produced close to 80% against the company's offer. The dispute still turns on wages and on the incentive scheme paying an extra 25% of base wage for meeting production thresholds, which the union links to three deaths at the plant between 2020 and 2022; a mediator had been assigned as of August 18, and the most recent coverage we could read, dated August 31, still describes workers on the picket line. Why it matters: a stoppage into its fourth week raises the chance of delivery-timing effects for anyone with cars on order there, though no source we could open establishes current production status at the plant.

OmniTRAX puts autonomous rail-condition and temperature monitoring on its Texas Panhandle short line
Progressive Railroading · Sep 1 · short line · safety technology
OmniTRAX has deployed Athena Industrial Services' Advanced Rail Condition Detector and TrueTemp Link on its Panhandle Northern Railroad, where the systems monitor dark territory autonomously for train presence, broken rails and track temperature. The company cites the Panhandle's wide winter-to-summer temperature range as the reason it wants continuous coverage rather than periodic inspection. Why it matters: these are track sensors, not the rolling-stock detectors — hot bearing, acoustic bearing and wheel impact load — that actually trigger component replacement, so the direct read-across to undercarriage aftermarket demand is minimal.

Study puts economic activity tied to Georgia's ports at $181 billion, up 4% on the year
Progressive Railroading, reporting a University of Georgia Terry College of Business study for the Georgia Ports Authority · Sep 1 · ports · intermodal
The study estimates statewide economic activity associated with Georgia's ports at $181 billion in fiscal 2025, an increase of about $7 billion or 4% year on year, supporting 605,616 full- and part-time jobs, with more than 44,500 added since fiscal 2021. Georgia Ports handled 5.67 million TEUs in fiscal 2026 and is working through a roughly $5 billion expansion targeting 55% cargo growth over ten years, including five new container berths at Savannah; Savannah and Brunswick are served by Genesee & Wyoming short lines that interchange with CSX and Norfolk Southern. Why it matters: an impact study measures economic footprint rather than rail volume and is published for the port itself, so the item to track is the berth programme, since added capacity reaches well-car and truck demand only if the extra boxes actually move inland by rail.

Current federal surface transportation authorization, including the rail title, ends September 30
Congressional Research Service report R48881 — read in summary only, the report page was not reachable to us today · legislation · 28 days out
The current authorization period for federal surface transportation programmes, enacted in the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, ends after fiscal 2026 on September 30, covering the rail title alongside highway and transit. A reauthorization bill, the BUILD America 250 Act, was introduced in May and ordered reported by the House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee with amendments, which is a committee step rather than passage or enactment. Why it matters: funds already appropriated and awards already made are not cancelled by an authorization lapse, and because CRISI and grade-crossing grants pay for track, signals and bridges rather than rolling stock, the direct exposure for railcar components is limited.

Knorr-Bremse — company newsroom · Aug 19 · competitor · Europe
