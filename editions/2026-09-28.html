President's Brief — three takeaways
  1. The UP–NS review is moving, but not smoothly for the applicants. In a concurring opinion to the Board's unanimous September 18 decision keeping the application alive, the STB's newest member, Richard Kloster, called the filing shallow on competition remedies and criticized concessions being released slowly. He stressed he was not prejudging the merits, and notices of intent to participate are due this Wednesday, September 30. The signal is that conditions, not just approval or denial, are likely to be where the case is fought.
  2. CSX is pulling back on track maintenance labor for the winter. The maintenance-of-way union says CSX will furlough about 1,100 engineering workers by mid-November, with some positions possibly reposted in March; CSX calls it aligning teams with seasonal workflows. This is track and structures work, not car shops, so it does not bear directly on freight-car component demand. It is, however, another sign that Class I railroads are managing headcount tightly heading into third-quarter earnings on October 21–22.
  3. High diesel is feeding through to grain shippers during harvest. Harvest Public Media reports that railroad fuel surcharges have more than doubled over the past year with diesel above $6 a gallon, costs that analysts say tend to be passed back to farmers through lower local grain prices. Railroads move roughly 1.6 million carloads of grain a year, about 24% of domestic grain movements, per the report. Higher shipping costs are a margin issue for shippers rather than a direct volume signal, but they are worth watching alongside harvest carloadings.

FreightWaves · Sep 23 · regulatory · UP–NS merger
In a concurring opinion to the Board's unanimous September 18 decision denying motions to dismiss, member Richard Kloster wrote that the application lacks detail on how UP will address competition concerns and offers no meaningful concessions, while saying he was not prejudging the merits. Why it matters: skepticism from a recent administration appointee suggests the Board may press the applicants for stronger competitive conditions before any decision.
CSX to furlough about 1,100 maintenance-of-way workers by mid-November, union says
WKRC Local 12, citing BMWED and CSX · Sep 23 · customers · labor
The Brotherhood of Maintenance of Way Employes Division says CSX notified it on September 14 of furloughs covering track inspection, maintenance and construction roles, with some positions possibly reposted in March; CSX says it is adjusting its engineering organization to align with seasonal workflows. The union warns the cuts raise safety risks. Why it matters: this is track labor rather than car maintenance, so the direct link to freight-car component demand is limited, but it shows a Class I tightening costs ahead of its October 21 earnings.
Railroads are charging more to ship grain as fuel surcharges double
Harvest Public Media via KCUR · Sep 28 · shippers · agriculture
Railroad fuel surcharges have more than doubled over the past year with diesel above $6 a gallon, according to the report, and farm-economy sources quoted say transport costs are typically passed back to growers through lower local prices. Railroads move about 1.6 million carloads of grain a year, roughly 24% of domestic grain movements, per the report. Why it matters: higher shipping costs squeeze shipper margins during harvest, though they are not by themselves evidence of lower rail grain volumes.

PHMSA final rule adopts ASME Section XII for multi-unit tank car tanks and other transport tanks
Federal Register (PHMSA final rule) · published Sep 28 · effective Oct 28 · regulatory · hazmat
The rule incorporates the 2015 edition of ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code Section XII for construction and continued service of certain cargo tank motor vehicles, cryogenic portable tanks and multi-unit tank car tanks, plus National Board Inspection Code provisions for their continued service. PHMSA says the change brings cost savings and regulatory flexibility without compromising safety. Why it matters: it covers multi-unit tank car tanks (ton containers), not conventional tank cars, so the effect on freight-car component demand should be minimal.

Etihad Rail launches Abu Dhabi–Fujairah port freight service
World Cargo News · Sep 27 · global · Middle East
Etihad Rail and AD Ports Group started a three-times-weekly, 200 km service linking Fujairah Terminals on the Indian Ocean with Abu Dhabi's industrial zone, letting cargo be booked through to the inland zone for clearance. Why it matters: along with this month's Saudi rolling-stock orders, it shows Gulf freight rail networks moving from construction into commercial service.
